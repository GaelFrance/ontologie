## What you are asked to review

- **Legal:** Are the default waterfall, assessment powers, auction obligations and loss allocation tools described in a way that fits the rulebooks of the reference CCPs? Is a Credit Support Arrangement distinct from a collateral arrangement in your documentation?

## 1. Concepts you approve

| Concept | Definition | Your verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[Risk Exposure](concepts.md#risk-exposure)** | Amount that an observing entity stands to lose toward an identified counterparty, account or scope (a client, a group of clients defined for risk purposes, an executing broker, a central counterparty or a clearing service) if that counterparty fails to perform, or if positions the observing entity carries for it must be closed out, at an as-of time and in a currency basis. It states its risk area (client clearing, execution or CCP), its measure basis (current exposure, potential future exposure, stress loss or contingent obligation) and whether collateral and credit support held are taken into account. How it is calculated is set by the observing entity's risk methodology and is not part of this definition. Distinct from Margin Requirement, which is an amount a margin provider must provide, and from Position, which is an open quantity. | |
| **[Risk Limit](concepts.md#risk-limit)** | Cap that an identified party, usually through its risk function, sets on a risk exposure, a quantity or an activity of an identified counterparty, account, trader, access or product scope, with an owner, a basis (for example quantity, notional, margin, credit exposure or stress loss), a version, an effective period, any warning level, and the action expected when it is reached (warn, refer, block, reduce). Kinds include the clearing limit, the pre-trade limit, the client credit limit and the CCP exposure limit. It is a decision of the party that sets it, not a rule provision, and a change to it is not a rule change. A cap set by a regulator or a venue on positions is a Position Limit; a cap on collateral is a Concentration Limit. | |
| **[Limit Utilisation](concepts.md#limit-utilisation)** *(added in round 2)* | Part of a risk limit used by an identified counterparty, account or scope at an as-of time, stated on the limit's own basis and version, as an amount or a share of the limit, with whether pending orders and trades not yet accepted are counted, as the limit states. No formula is set at this phase. | |
| **[Limit Breach](concepts.md#limit-breach)** *(added in round 2)* | Event in which the utilisation of a risk limit exceeds the limit, or reaches a warning level the limit defines, at an identified time, recording the limit version in force, the excess, who was told and the action taken (warning, referral, temporary increase, block of new orders or trades, reduction of positions). A breach of a limit the firm set is not a regulatory breach; exceeding a Position Limit is. Distinct from a rejected Pre-trade Control Check, which prevents an order from causing a breach. | |
| **[Stress Loss](concepts.md#stress-loss)** *(added in round 2)* | Loss that an observing entity estimates for an identified portfolio, account, counterparty or clearing service under a named stress scenario (historical or hypothetical, with its version and as-of date), stated before or after the collateral held, as declared. It is used to size and monitor risk limits, client margin uplifts and the sizing of CCP default resources. Scenario design and calculation are not part of this definition. | |
| **[Client Clearing Exposure](concepts.md#client-clearing-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure of a clearing broker toward a client it clears, or toward a group of clients defined for risk purposes: what the broker could lose if the client failed to meet a margin call or another obligation and the broker had to close out, hedge or transfer the client's positions, after the collateral and credit support it holds from the client, at an as-of time. It includes amounts the broker has paid or committed to the CCP for the client before receiving them from the client (for example a CCP intraday call funded before the client pays). Distinct from the CCP's exposure to the clearing member, from CCP Exposure (the member's exposure to the CCP) and from the margin requirement on the client's account. | |
| **[Client Credit Assessment](concepts.md#client-credit-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Process by which a clearing broker's or executing broker's risk function reviews a client, or a group of clients defined for risk purposes, before onboarding, periodically and on a trigger event, to decide whether to clear for it or extend credit to it, under which risk limits, margin uplift and conditions, and with which client risk rating. Outcome values: approved, approved with conditions, declined, under review. Distinct from know-your-client checks, which are a compliance process, and from an Offering Eligibility Criterion, which states who may receive a service. | |
| **[Client Risk Rating](concepts.md#client-risk-rating)** *(added in round 2)* | Time-bounded internal grade that a provider's risk function gives to a client, or to a group of clients defined for risk purposes, on the provider's own scale, as the result of a client credit assessment; it drives risk limits, client margin uplifts and how often the client is reviewed. It is not an external credit rating and not a Regulatory Classification. | |
| **[Credit Support Arrangement](concepts.md#credit-support-arrangement)** *(added in round 2)* | Agreement by which a third party (for example a parent company giving a guarantee, or a bank issuing a letter of credit) undertakes to meet a client's obligations toward its clearing broker or executing broker, in full or up to an amount, if the client does not. It is credit support beyond the collateral the client provides. Distinct from a Collateral Arrangement, under which assets are transferred or pledged. | |
| **[Client Default Management](concepts.md#client-default-management)** *(added in round 2)* | Process run by a clearing broker after a Client Default under the client clearing agreement: stopping new business, hedging, closing out or transferring the client's positions, applying the client's collateral and any credit support, and claiming any remaining shortfall from the client, with its steps, dates and outcome per account. Distinct from a CCP's default management after a Clearing Member Default, which may include Porting and a Default Management Auction. | |
| **[Pre-trade Limit](concepts.md#pre-trade-limit)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk limit applied in a pre-trade control check to an order before it reaches the venue, or by the venue on entry, set by the executing broker, the trading venue member, the clearing member or the venue for an identified client, trader, access or session and instrument scope: for example maximum order size or value, price collar, credit or exposure cap, position cap, or order and message rate. A clearing limit can be applied as a pre-trade limit. Distinct from a Position Limit, which a regulator or venue rule sets on positions held. | |
| **[Kill Switch Activation](concepts.md#kill-switch-activation)** *(added in round 2)* | Event in which an authorised party (the executing broker, the trading venue member, the clearing member or the venue) cancels all, or a defined set, of a participant's open orders and blocks new ones for a stated scope (client, trader, access session, venue or segment) at an identified time, until it is lifted. Distinct from the rejection of a single order by a pre-trade control check and from a venue trading halt, which stops trading in an instrument for everyone. | |
| **[Execution Error](concepts.md#execution-error)** *(added in round 2)* | Event in which an execution, or its booking or allocation, departs from the client's instruction or from the intended order through an error on the executing side: wrong instrument, side, quantity, price or account, a duplicate order, or an order entered outside its limits. It records who caused it and how it was resolved: correction, cancellation or price adjustment by the venue under its error trade rules, or transfer to an error position. Distinct from an Operational Exception, which is any situation needing handling, and from a Trade Amendment agreed between counterparties. | |
| **[Error Position](concepts.md#error-position)** *(added in round 2)* | Position held in an account that the executing broker keeps for the purpose, arising from an execution error, carried at the broker's own risk until it is closed out or hedged. It is not client business: its gain or loss is not client revenue. | |
| **[Unaccepted Give-up Exposure](concepts.md#unaccepted-give-up-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure that the giving side (the executing broker and its giving clearing member) carries on trades or allocated quantities executed for a client and not yet accepted by the receiving clearing member, whether pending take-up, rejected or lapsed, so that they stay with the giving member with their margin and default risk. It ends for a quantity when the receiving member's CCP account records it (decision 5). | |
| **[CCP Exposure](concepts.md#ccp-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure of a clearing member toward a central counterparty, per clearing service: what the member could lose if the CCP failed, or used the member's resources after another member's default or in a recovery, covering margin and default fund contribution posted that are not protected from the CCP's insolvency, committed further contributions (assessments) and, where the member guarantees the CCP's performance to its clients, client positions. Distinct from the CCP's exposure to the member, which the CCP covers with margin and the default fund, and from Client Clearing Exposure. | |
| **[CCP Risk Assessment](concepts.md#ccp-risk-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Process by which a clearing member's risk function reviews a central counterparty and its clearing services, before membership and periodically: financial resources, default waterfall, rules on assessments and loss allocation, margin methodology, governance, regulatory status (for example qualifying CCP) and published stress results. Its outcome sets the member's CCP exposure limits and conditions. Distinct from the CCP's own review of the member against its Access Requirements. | |
| **[Default Waterfall](concepts.md#default-waterfall)** *(added in round 2)* | Versioned order, set in a clearing service's rules, in which a central counterparty uses its financial resources to cover losses from a clearing member default: the defaulter's margin and default fund contribution, the CCP's dedicated own resources, the non-defaulting members' default fund contributions, assessments, any further own resources and then recovery tools, with the size or cap of each layer where the rules state it. Changing it is a rule change. | |
| **[CCP Dedicated Own Resources](concepts.md#ccp-dedicated-own-resources)** *(added in round 2)* | Amount of its own capital that a central counterparty commits, under its rules or a regime's requirement, to a stated layer of its default waterfall for a clearing service, usually before the non-defaulting members' default fund contributions are used (often called skin in the game). Distinct from the CCP's other capital, which does not stand in the waterfall. | |
| **[Default Fund Assessment](concepts.md#default-fund-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Obligation of a non-defaulting clearing member, under a clearing service's rules, to pay a further contribution to a central counterparty when its prefunded default fund has been used or is insufficient, up to the cap and within the period the rules set (assessment powers). Distinct from the prefunded Default Fund Contribution and from margin. | |
| **[Default Management Auction](concepts.md#default-management-auction)** *(added in round 2)* | Event, run by a central counterparty after a Clearing Member Default under its default management procedures, in which the defaulter's positions, usually hedged and grouped in portfolios, are offered to clearing members, some of which may be required to bid; it records the auction date, portfolio, participants, result and any effect on members' default fund contributions (for example juniorisation of poor bidders). Distinct from a close-out in the market and from Porting. | |
| **[CCP Loss Allocation](concepts.md#ccp-loss-allocation)** *(added in round 2)* | Event by which a central counterparty, once the prefunded layers of its default waterfall are used, or after a loss not caused by a member default, passes remaining losses to clearing members and, through them, possibly to their clients, with tools its rules or a recovery and resolution regime provide: assessments, variation margin gains haircutting, partial or full tear-up of contracts, or cash calls. It records the tool, the clearing service, the date and the amounts allocated per member. | |

## 3. Open questions on meaning

A proposed answer comes from the expert review; it is **not a decision** until the owner confirms it.

### Risk (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Risk area (client clearing, execution, CCP) versus risk type (credit, market, liquidity, operational, legal) versus risk measure (exposure, stress loss, limit utilisation) versus the risk function, which is an internal team and not a party.

**Proposed answer:** Never answer a question about "risk" without fixing three things: the risk area (the client we clear, execution, or the CCP), the observing party (who could lose) and the measure (exposure, stress loss, limit utilisation, breach). The risk function sets limits and assessments but is not a party and holds no role. Risk types (credit, market, liquidity) describe the cause and are values, not concepts, at this phase. Confidence medium.

<sub>reference LD-A0036</sub>

### Limit (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Firm risk limit (clearing limit, pre-trade limit, client credit limit, CCP exposure limit) versus position limit set by a regulator or venue versus concentration limit on collateral.

**Proposed answer:** A Risk Limit is a decision of the party that sets it; its kinds are the Clearing Limit, which governs acceptance for clearing, and the Pre-trade Limit, which screens orders; one limit can serve both. A Position Limit is a regulator's or venue's rule on positions held. A Concentration Limit caps how much of an asset counts as collateral. Ask who set the limit and on what. Confidence high.

<sub>reference LD-A0037</sub>

### Exposure (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Clearing broker's exposure to a client versus the executing side's exposure on trades not yet taken up versus a clearing member's exposure to a CCP versus the CCP's exposure to the member; gross or net of collateral; current or stressed.

**Proposed answer:** An exposure always has an observing party and a counterparty: the clearing broker toward its client (Client Clearing Exposure), the giving side toward trades not yet taken up (Unaccepted Give-up Exposure), the clearing member toward a CCP (CCP Exposure). The CCP's exposure to the member is what margin (Margin Requirement) and the default fund cover; it is not one of these. State the basis (current, potential, stress) and whether collateral is counted. Confidence high.

<sub>reference LD-A0038</sub>

### Default (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Client default toward its clearing broker versus clearing member default declared by a CCP versus CCP failure, recovery or resolution versus a settlement fail or an unmet margin call versus a default value or setting.

**Proposed answer:** Ask who defaults toward whom. A Client Default is declared by the clearing broker under the client clearing agreement and leads to Client Default Management. A Clearing Member Default is declared by the CCP and leads to porting, auctions and use of the Default Waterfall. A CCP in recovery allocates losses (CCP Loss Allocation). A settlement fail or an unmet margin call can lead to a default but is not one. Confidence high.
