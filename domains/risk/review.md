## 1. Concepts you approve

| Concept | Definition | Your verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[Risk Exposure](concepts.md#risk-exposure)** | Amount that an observing entity stands to lose toward an identified counterparty, account or scope (a client, a group of clients defined for risk purposes, an executing broker, a central counterparty or a clearing service) if that counterparty fails to perform, or if positions the observing entity carries for it must be closed out, at an as-of time and in a currency basis. It states its risk area (client clearing, execution or CCP), its measure basis (current exposure, potential future exposure, stress loss under a named scenario, or contingent obligation), whether it is stated before or after collateral and credit support held and, if after, at which collateral valuation. How it is calculated is set by the observing entity's risk methodology and is not part of this definition. Distinct from Margin Requirement, which is an amount a margin provider must provide, and from Position, which is an open quantity. | |
| **[Risk Limit](concepts.md#risk-limit)** | Cap or permitted range that an identified party, usually through its risk function, sets on a risk exposure, a quantity or an activity of an identified counterparty (for example a client or an executing broker), account, trader, access or product scope, with an owner, a basis (for example quantity, notional, margin, credit exposure or stress loss), a version, an effective period, any warning level, and the action expected when it is reached (warn, refer, reject the trade or order, block new orders or trades, reduce). Kinds include the clearing limit, the pre-trade limit, the client credit limit and the CCP exposure limit. It is a decision of the party that sets it for that counterparty or scope, not a rule provision, and a change to it is not a rule change, even where a rule requires such limits (the requirement is a Regulatory Obligation). A parameter that a venue publishes in its rules or contract specifications for all participants (for example a price band or a maximum order quantity) is a Rule Provision applied in the same check, and a change to it is a Rule Change; a limit a venue sets for one participant, or that a participant sets in the venue's risk tools, is a Risk Limit. A limit a CCP sets on its own members is the CCP's control, recorded under its rules, not a Risk Limit of the firm. A cap set by a regulator or a venue on positions is a Position Limit; a cap on collateral is a Concentration Limit. | |
| **[Limit Utilisation](concepts.md#limit-utilisation)** *(added in round 2)* | Part of a risk limit used by an identified counterparty, account or scope at an as-of time, stated on the limit's own basis and version and any temporary increase in force, as an amount or a share of the limit, with whether pending orders and trades not yet accepted are counted, as the limit states. Its complement, the unused part of the limit, is called headroom. No formula is set at this phase. | |
| **[Limit Breach](concepts.md#limit-breach)** *(added in round 2)* | Event in which the utilisation of a risk limit exceeds the limit at an identified time, recording the limit version and any temporary increase in force, the excess, whether the breach is active (new business) or passive, who was told and the action taken. Reaching a warning level is recorded as a warning, not as a breach. A breach of a limit the firm set is not in itself a breach of a rule; exceeding a Position Limit is not a Limit Breach (see Position Limit). Distinct from a rejected Pre-trade Control Check and from a take-up or submission acceptance refused on a clearing limit, which stop one order or trade whether or not a breach has occurred. | |
| **[Client Clearing Exposure](concepts.md#client-clearing-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure of a clearing broker toward a client it clears, or toward a group of clients defined for risk purposes, at the member-to-client tier, whatever the client clearing legal model: what the broker could lose if the client failed to meet a margin call or another obligation and the broker had to close out, hedge or transfer its positions, stated before or after the collateral and credit support held from the client, as declared, at an as-of time. Where the client clears for Indirect Clients, it includes what the client owes for them. It is stated per client even where positions sit in an omnibus CCP account. It includes amounts the broker has paid or committed to the CCP for the client before receiving them. For a give-in it starts for each quantity at its registration to the broker's account. Distinct from the CCP's exposure to the clearing member, from CCP Exposure (the member's exposure to the CCP) and from the margin requirement on the client's account. | |
| **[Client Risk Group](concepts.md#client-risk-group)** *(added in round 2)* | Group of clients that a provider's risk function treats as one for risk purposes over a stated period, because their credit is connected (for example a legal group, or a fund manager and the funds it manages); the risk function sets its membership, which need not follow the Commercial Group or the legal group. | |
| **[Client Credit Assessment](concepts.md#client-credit-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Process by which a clearing broker's or executing broker's risk function reviews a client, or a group of clients defined for risk purposes, before onboarding, periodically and on a trigger event (for example a rating downgrade, a limit breach, a missed margin call, adverse news, a material change in activity), to decide whether to clear for it or extend credit to it, under which risk limits, margin uplift and conditions, and with which client risk rating, the internal, time-bounded grade it records as part of its outcome. Outcome values: approved, approved with conditions, declined; while it runs, the assessment is under review. Distinct from know-your-client checks, which are a compliance process, and from an Offering Eligibility Criterion, which states who may receive a service. | |
| **[Third-party Credit Support](concepts.md#third-party-credit-support)** *(added in round 2)* | Agreement by which a third party (for example a parent company giving a guarantee, or a bank issuing a standby letter of credit) undertakes to meet a client's obligations toward its clearing broker or executing broker, in full or up to an amount, for a validity period, if the client does not; it is enforceable against the third party, and a comfort letter is not. It does not count as cover for a margin requirement: a guarantee or letter of credit accepted as an eligible collateral asset, or assets a third party delivers or pledges for the client, are collateral under a Collateral Arrangement. Distinct from a Collateral Arrangement, including an ISDA Credit Support Annex. | |
| **[Error Position](concepts.md#error-position)** *(added in round 2)* | Position held in an error account in the name of the party responsible for an execution error (the executing broker or a third-party executing member), carried through its clearing member or at the CCP, at that party's own risk until it is closed out or hedged. It is not client business: its gain or loss is not client revenue. A quantity rejected at take-up that stays with the giving clearing member is an error position only once moved to the error account. | |
| **[Unaccepted Give-up Exposure](concepts.md#unaccepted-give-up-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure carried on quantities of trades executed for a client in a listed clearing service that are registered to the giving clearing member and not yet registered to the receiving clearing member's CCP account, whether pending take-up, accepted but not yet registered, rejected or lapsed. Each figure states its observing party: the giving clearing member, which keeps those quantities at the CCP with their margin, fees and default risk, or the executing broker, where it is not itself that member, which bears them toward its clearing member under the give-up agreement. It ends for each quantity at its Give-up Registration to the receiving member's account, or when the quantity is transferred or closed out under the CCP's rules and the give-up agreement. An OTC trade submitted for clearing and not accepted is not covered. Distinct from Client Clearing Exposure, which the receiving clearing broker carries once the quantity is registered to it, and from an Error Position. | |
| **[CCP Exposure](concepts.md#ccp-exposure)** *(added in round 2)* | Kind of risk exposure of a clearing member toward a central counterparty, per clearing service, stated separately for the house account and the client accounts: what the member could lose if the CCP failed, or used the member's resources after another member's default or in a recovery or resolution. It covers the replacement of the member's house positions; amounts due from the CCP and not yet received; collateral it has posted for margin requirements, including any excess and, under collateralised-to-market treatment, variation margin collateral, to the extent that the collateral arrangement does not protect it from the CCP's insolvency, as the member's legal assessment states; its Default Fund Contribution in all cases; committed further contributions (assessments) up to their cap; and, where the member stays liable to its clients if the CCP fails (as counterparty to the client back-to-back trades under the principal-to-principal model, or under a guarantee it has given), the client trades and client collateral concerned. Distinct from the CCP's exposure to the member and from Client Clearing Exposure. | |
| **[CCP Risk Assessment](concepts.md#ccp-risk-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Process by which a clearing member's risk function reviews a central counterparty and its clearing services, before membership, periodically and on trigger events: financial resources, default waterfall, rules on assessments and loss allocation, margin methodology, governance, regulatory standing (authorisations and recognitions, qualifying CCP status for capital purposes, any equivalence relied on), as recorded from public sources; the assessment reads these statuses and does not determine them; and published stress results. Its outcome sets the member's CCP exposure limits and conditions. Distinct from the CCP's own review of the member against its Access Requirements. | |
| **[Default Waterfall](concepts.md#default-waterfall)** *(added in round 2)* | Versioned order, set in a clearing service's rules and constrained by any regime that applies, in which a central counterparty uses its financial resources to cover losses from a clearing member default, with the size or cap of each layer where the rules state it. Under EU EMIR, for example, the order is the collateral the defaulter provided for its own house margin requirements, the defaulter's default fund contribution, the CCP's dedicated own resources, the non-defaulting members' default fund contributions, then other resources such as assessments (to verify); the order is always read from the clearing service's rules. Collateral recorded for client accounts is not a layer: it covers only losses on those clients' positions, as the segregation applied allows. Tools beyond the waterfall belong to CCP Loss Allocation; losses not caused by a member default do not run through it. A change to its order, layers, caps or tools is a Rule Change; a periodic recalculation of a layer's amount under unchanged rules is not. | |
| **[Default Fund Assessment](concepts.md#default-fund-assessment)** *(added in round 2)* | Committed obligation of a non-defaulting clearing member (or, where the rules say so, of a sponsoring clearing member for a sponsored participant), under a clearing service's rules, to pay a further contribution to a central counterparty beyond its prefunded default fund contribution when the default fund has been used or is insufficient after a clearing member default, up to the cap and within the period the rules set (including any cooling-off period). A call of it gives rise to a Settlement Obligation; the commitment itself is not one. Distinct from the prefunded Default Fund Contribution and its replenishment after use, from margin, and from a Regulatory Obligation. | |
| **[CCP Loss Allocation](concepts.md#ccp-loss-allocation)** *(added in round 2)* | Event by which, after the resources of its default waterfall are used for a member default, or after a loss not caused by a member default, remaining losses of a central counterparty are passed to clearing members and, through them, possibly to their clients, by the CCP under its rules and recovery plan or by a resolution authority under a recovery and resolution regime. Variation margin gains haircutting reduces the variation margin payments the CCP makes, not the requirement as determined. Whether a loss reaches a client depends on the clearing service's rules for client accounts and on the client clearing agreement. It records the acting party, the tool, the clearing service, the triggering event, the date and the amounts per member. | |

## 3. Open questions on meaning

A proposed answer comes from the expert review; it is **not a decision** until the owner confirms it.

### Risk (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Risk area (client clearing, execution, CCP) versus risk type (credit, market, liquidity, operational, legal) versus risk measure (exposure, stress loss, limit utilisation) versus the risk function, which is an internal team and not a party.

**Proposed answer:** Never answer a question about "risk" without fixing three things: the risk area (the client we clear, execution, or the CCP), the observing party (who could lose) and the measure (exposure, stress loss, limit utilisation, breach). The risk function sets limits and assessments but is not a party and holds no role. Risk types (credit, market, liquidity) describe the cause and are values, not concepts, at this phase. Confidence medium.

### Limit (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Firm risk limit (clearing limit, pre-trade limit, client credit limit, CCP exposure limit) versus position limit set by a regulator or venue versus concentration limit on collateral.

**Proposed answer:** A Risk Limit is a decision of the party that sets it for one counterparty or scope. Its kinds include the Clearing Limit, which decides whether a trade is accepted for clearing, the client credit limit, which caps the clearing broker's exposure to the client (where one limit does both, it is a clearing limit), and the pre-trade limit, which screens orders. One limit can be applied in both places. A parameter that a venue publishes for all participants (price band, maximum order quantity) is a Rule Provision. A Position Limit is a regulator's or venue's rule on positions held. "Concentration limit" alone means Concentration Limit in a collateral context; a firm cap on position concentration is a Risk Limit, and a CCP concentration add-on is a Margin Requirement Component. Ask who set the limit, on what, under what authority, and who applies it. Confidence high.

<sub>reference LD-A0037</sub>

### Exposure (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Clearing broker's exposure to a client versus the giving side's exposure on quantities not yet registered to the receiving member versus a clearing member's exposure to a CCP versus the CCP's exposure to the member; gross or net of collateral; current or stressed.

**Proposed answer:** An exposure always has one observing party and one counterparty: the clearing broker toward its client (Client Clearing Exposure); the giving clearing member, or the executing broker where it is not that member, toward the client on quantities not yet registered to the receiving member (Unaccepted Give-up Exposure); the clearing member toward a CCP (CCP Exposure). The CCP's exposure to the member is what the CCP sizes the member's Initial Margin Requirement against and covers first with the collateral the member provides, then with the member's Default Fund Contribution and the mutualised layers of the Default Waterfall; it has no concept of its own. State the basis, whether collateral is counted and at which valuation. Confidence high.

### Default (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Client default toward its clearing broker versus a client that clears for indirect clients, seen from each tier, versus a clearing broker defaulting toward its client versus clearing member default declared by a CCP versus a sponsored clearing participant declared in default by the CCP versus CCP failure, recovery or resolution versus a settlement fail or an unmet margin call versus a default value or setting.

**Proposed answer:** Ask who defaults toward whom. A Client Default is declared by the clearing broker under the client clearing agreement and is followed by the steps listed on it (close-out, hedging, transfer, use of collateral and credit support). Where the client clears for indirect clients, say from which tier the default is seen; a clearing broker can also default toward its client. A Clearing Member Default is declared by the CCP and may lead to Porting of client positions and collateral, close-out or an auction of house positions and non-ported client positions, and use of the Default Waterfall. A sponsored clearing participant can be declared in default by the CCP; how that is modelled is pending a question for our firm. A CCP in recovery allocates losses (CCP Loss Allocation). A settlement fail or an unmet margin call can lead to a default but is not one. "Default" can also mean a default value or setting, which is not an event. Confidence high.
