### Product Family

*A family of similar traded products, such as 'equity index futures' or 'fixed-to-floating interest rate swaps', described without reference to any exchange.*

**Definition.** Classification grouping of contract specifications, instruments or trades that share economic and contractual characteristics (asset class, underlying type, contract form such as future, option, forward or swap, settlement form), independent of any venue and of any counterparty; it may be organised in levels from asset class down to the finest product level at which an OTC product identifier is assigned. Not a venue's contract specification and not a service offering; a classification code such as a CFI code is a value of the classification, not the family's identity. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Index futures listed on Venue V and on Venue W both belong to the product family 'equity index future'.

**Not to be confused with.** Contract Specification: a family groups many venues' contracts; a contract specification is one venue's listing of one contract.

**Words people use:** “product” (Any)

**Note.** Label changed from "Product" on 2026-11-01; meaning unchanged. OTC classification can align with the ISDA OTC taxonomy, CFI (ISO 10962) and UPI (ISO 4914), to verify. [03, 05, 07]

**Connected to:**

- [Contract Specification](#contract-specification) *classified as* Product Family — A classification code such as CFI is a classification value, not an identity.
- [Underlying](#underlying) *drives the classification of* Product Family
- [Clearing Eligibility Schedule](#clearing-eligibility-schedule) *lists as eligible with trade term ranges otc* Product Family
- [Regulated Product Class](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulated-product-class) *is mapped to never equal to* Product Family

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Product, instrument and OTC contract” (open, answer proposed); “Product” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Contract Specification

*The rulebook page of one listed contract: underlying, size, tick, expiry months, settlement method and trading hours, from which all its series are created.*

**Definition.** Versioned set of standard terms, published by the trading venue that lists a derivative contract and usually designated by that venue's product code, from which the contract's tradeable instruments (series) are created: underlying, size or multiplier, currency, price quotation and tick, expiry cycle and key-date rules, settlement method, option terms where relevant (call or put, exercise style, strike rules, premium or futures-style), adjustment rules and trading hours. Each version has an effective date and states the series to which it applies.

**Example.** Venue V's specification for Contract K: a future on Index I, ten units per index point, quarterly expiries, cash settled.

**Not to be confused with.** Instrument: the specification is the template; the instrument is one series created from it, such as the March expiry.

**Breaks down into:** [Underlying](#underlying) (part of), [Key-Date Rule](#key-date-rule) (part of)

**Words people use:** “product” (Any); “exchange product code, listed contract” (Listed markets)

**Connected to:**

- *classified as* [Product Family](#product-family) — A classification code such as CFI is a classification value, not an identity.
- *defined by* [Trading Venue](#trading-venue) — The venue that lists the contract; time-bounded.
- [Trading Venue Segment](#trading-venue-segment) *lists contracts of* Contract Specification
- [Flexible Series](#flexible-series) *is created within the limits of* Contract Specification
- [Clearing Contract](#clearing-contract) *maps one or more of* Contract Specification
- [Clearing Eligibility Schedule](#clearing-eligibility-schedule) *lists as eligible* Contract Specification
- [Offering Coverage Line](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#offering-coverage-line) *states its product scope by* Contract Specification
- [Block Trade](../execution/concepts.md#block-trade) *has minimum size set per* Contract Specification

### Underlying

*The thing a derivative is 'on': a share, an index, an interest rate, a commodity, or another derivative.*

**Definition.** Asset, index, rate, commodity, other instrument or measurable variable to which a derivative contract refers and from which its value is derived, identified with its source (index administrator, benchmark, delivery specification) and, where the contract settles physically, the deliverable grade or basket.

**Example.** Contract K is a future on Index I, published by Administrator A; an option on Contract K's June future has that future as its underlying.

**Not to be confused with.** Instrument: the underlying is what the derivative refers to; it is an instrument only when it is itself a listed derivative.

**Part of:** [Contract Specification](#contract-specification)

**Values (draft list):** equity; equity index; interest rate or benchmark; bond or bond basket; currency pair; commodity; listed derivative; volatility or other index; credit

**Words people use:** “underlier”; “reference asset”; “reference entity” (credit)

**Before using it, know:** Which underlying source applies (index version, benchmark, deliverable basket) at the relevant date.

**Connected to:**

- *drives the classification of* [Product Family](#product-family)
- *can itself be option on a future* [Instrument](#instrument)
- *corporate events on it can trigger* [Contract Adjustment](#contract-adjustment)
- *its market s holidays can close a contract* [Business Calendar](#business-calendar)

### Key-Date Rule

*The rule that says, for each series, when it stops trading, when it expires and when it settles, for example 'third Friday of the month, or the day before if that is a holiday'.*

**Definition.** Rule within a contract specification that determines, for each series, its role-qualified key dates (first trading day, last trading day, expiry, exercise deadline, first notice day, final settlement day, delivery day) from the expiry cycle and one or more named business calendars, including how a date falling on a non-business day is moved.

**Example.** Contract K: last trading day is the third Friday of the expiry month on Venue V's calendar; if closed, the preceding business day.

**Not to be confused with.** Business Date: the rule computes the dates; the resulting dates are business dates with roles.

**Part of:** [Contract Specification](#contract-specification)

**Values (draft list):** key dates: first trading day; last trading day; expiry day; last exercise day; first notice day; last notice day; first delivery day; last delivery day (delivery period); due date; final settlement day; settlement date; the time and consequence of an exercise or notice deadline belong to the Cut-off read against that day

**Words people use:** “expiry rule”; “last trading day rule”; “roll convention” (OTC usage)

**Before using it, know:** Which calendar version the rule uses; a calendar change can move key dates without changing the rule.

**Connected to:**

- *is resolved against* [Business Calendar](#business-calendar)
- *produces key dates of* [Business Date](#business-date)
- *gives each series its key dates* [Instrument](#instrument)
- *is changed by and a calendar change can move its results* [Rule Change](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-change)

### Instrument

*One specific thing you can buy or sell that is the same for everybody: for listed derivatives, a series such as the March future or the June 100 call.*

**Definition.** Standardised derivative specification, identifiable independently of any counterparty pair and any single trade, at the level at which it is listed or assigned an instrument identifier: for listed derivatives the series (contract, expiry and, for options, strike and call or put), including a strategy listed by the venue, whose legs are other instruments, and a flexible series created on request; for OTC derivatives only where a standard instrument identifier is assigned, including OTC derivatives traded on a venue that carry one. A bespoke OTC contract is not an instrument and its terms belong to the Trade. The asset, index or rate a derivative refers to is its underlying; it is an instrument only when it is itself a listed derivative. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Contract K, June expiry, call, strike 100 on Venue V is one instrument; a spread buying June and selling September is another.

**Not to be confused with.** Trade: the instrument is what is traded; a trade is one deal in it between two parties. A bespoke swap has no instrument, only a trade.

**Breaks down into:** [Listed Strategy](#listed-strategy) (kind of), [Flexible Series](#flexible-series) (kind of), [Settlement Price](#settlement-price) (state of)

**Words people use:** “product” (Any)

**Note.** An OTC derivative has an Instrument only where a standard instrument identifier is assigned; trades with the same identifier can still differ in rate and notional, so the Trade carries the full terms. Listed strategies are instruments with legs. [01, 03, 07]

**Connected to:**

- *listed under* [Contract Specification](#contract-specification) — Listed instruments only; one specification version effective at listing.
- [Underlying](#underlying) *can itself be option on a future* Instrument
- [Key-Date Rule](#key-date-rule) *gives each series its key dates* Instrument
- [Contract Adjustment](#contract-adjustment) *changes or creates* Instrument
- [Strategy Leg Execution](../execution/concepts.md#strategy-leg-execution) *is in the leg* Instrument
- [Listed Position](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#listed-position) *is held in* Instrument
- [Expiry](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#expiry) *ends* Instrument

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Product, instrument and OTC contract” (open, answer proposed); “Product” (open, answer proposed); “OTC and listed” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Listed Strategy

*A ready-made combination, such as a calendar spread or an option straddle, that a venue lets you trade in one go.*

**Definition.** Instrument created by a trading venue, either predefined by the venue or defined on request by a participant, that combines two or more leg instruments in stated ratios and directions so that the combination trades as one, at one net price; its execution produces one execution per leg, and positions are held in the legs, not in the strategy.

**Example.** A calendar spread on Venue V: buy one June future and sell one September future of Contract K, quoted as one price difference.

**Not to be confused with.** Two separate trades in the same legs: a strategy is traded as one instrument at one net price, legging in is two independent executions.

**Kind of:** [Instrument](#instrument)

**Values (draft list):** legs: each named by leg instrument, direction relative to the strategy and ratio

**Words people use:** “spread, combo, complex instrument” (venue usage); “multi-leg instrument” (FIX)

**Before using it, know:** Whether the strategy was defined by the venue or by a participant, and whether the question concerns the strategy or its legs.

**Connected to:**

- *is made of* [Listed Strategy](#listed-strategy)
- *is executed as one execution per leg* [Execution](../execution/concepts.md#execution)
- *results in positions in its legs* [Position](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#position)
- *may be charged per leg or per strategy by venue rule* [Exchange Fee Charge](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#exchange-fee-charge)

### Flexible Series

*A series with custom terms, such as an unusual expiry date or strike, that a venue creates on demand for a participant.*

**Definition.** Listed series created on a participant's request within limits set by the contract specification or venue rules, with a non-standard expiry date, strike, exercise style or settlement method; it is listed, identified and cleared like a standard series but may have separate eligibility, minimum size, trading and fee rules.

**Example.** Client A asks Venue V to create a Contract K option expiring on 17 August with strike 103; the venue creates it the same day.

**Not to be confused with.** Standard series: listed in the regular cycle and known in advance; a flexible series exists only once requested.

**Kind of:** [Instrument](#instrument)

**Words people use:** “flex, flexible contract, custom series” (venue usage)

**Before using it, know:** That the series may appear intraday and be absent from start-of-day reference files.

**Connected to:**

- *is created within the limits of* [Contract Specification](#contract-specification)
- *may have its own eligibility in* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *may have its own fees under* [Fee Schedule](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#fee-schedule)

### Settlement Price

*The official end-of-day (or final) price that the exchange or CCP publishes for a series and that positions are valued at.*

**Definition.** Official price for a business date, of a stated kind (daily or final), published by the venue or by the CCP under its rules for a venue series or a series of the clearing contract; used to value listed positions, including for variation margin on futures and futures-style options and for premium margin, and to settle expiring contracts. Cleared OTC positions are valued by the CCP's own valuation, not by a settlement price.

**Example.** CCP Z publishes the daily settlement price of Contract K June for Monday; at expiry it publishes the final settlement price.

**Not to be confused with.** Last traded price: the settlement price is set by the venue or CCP's method, and may differ from any traded price.

**State of:** [Instrument](#instrument)

**Values (draft list):** daily settlement price; final settlement price; provisional; corrected

**Words people use:** “EDSP” (exchange delivery settlement price, final kind)

**Before using it, know:** Publisher (venue or CCP), kind (daily or final), business date and whether it is provisional or corrected.

**Connected to:**

- *may be published per series of* [Clearing Contract](#clearing-contract)
- *values listed positions for* [Variation Margin Requirement](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#variation-margin-requirement)
- *is used in its final kind by* [Final Settlement](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#final-settlement)
- [Position Valuation](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#position-valuation) *uses* Settlement Price — Listed positions; cleared OTC positions are valued by the CCP's own valuation, not by a settlement price.

### Trading Venue

*An organised marketplace, such as an exchange or a multilateral trading facility, where many participants' buying and selling interests meet under the venue's rules.*

**Definition.** Multilateral trading system or facility, operated under its own rules by an identified market operator, in which multiple third-party buying and selling interests meet, or privately negotiated trades are registered under its rules, so that contracts result; identified by a market identifier code and possibly divided into segments. Includes regulated markets, MTFs, OTFs, designated contract markets and swap execution facilities. Distinct from its operator, from a central counterparty, from a systematic internaliser or single-dealer platform, and from trade reporting services. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Venue V, a regulated market operated by Operator O, with an equity derivatives segment and a commodity derivatives segment.

**Not to be confused with.** The operator or the CCP: the operator is the company running the venue; the CCP clears the trades and is a separate entity even in the same group.

**Breaks down into:** [Trading Venue Segment](#trading-venue-segment) (part of)

**Words people use:** “exchange” (Any)

**Note.** Identify by ISO 10383 MIC; operating MIC for the venue, segment MIC for a segment (to verify per use). Venue type (regulated market, MTF, OTF; US DCM, SEF) is a Regulatory Classification. [01, 05, 06]

### Trading Venue Segment

*A section of an exchange, such as its equity derivatives market or its commodity market, that can have its own rules, hours and fees.*

**Definition.** Distinct part of a trading venue, defined by the venue's rules, with its own scope of contracts or participants and possibly its own trading rules, sessions, fees or regulatory status; identified, where assigned, by a segment market identifier code linked to the venue's operating code.

**Example.** Venue V has an equity derivatives segment and a commodity derivatives segment, each with its own segment code.

**Not to be confused with.** Trading Venue: the segment is part of one venue; a separately authorised venue run by the same operator is a different venue.

**Part of:** [Trading Venue](#trading-venue)

**Words people use:** “segment MIC” (ISO 10383); “market segment”; “trading platform” (venue usage)

**Before using it, know:** Whether a record or rule refers to the whole venue (operating level) or to one segment.

### Business Date

*A day that a business event counts towards, always stated with its role (trade date, clearing date, value date) and the calendar it is read against.*

**Definition.** Calendar day (not a timestamp) assigned to a business object or event under a stated date role, such as trade date, CCP clearing date, value date or last trading day, resolved against a named business calendar and time zone; the event's UTC timestamp is kept beside it and need not fall on the same calendar day.

**Example.** A trade executed at 23:30 local time in Venue V's evening session has the next business day as its trade date.

**Not to be confused with.** Timestamp: the timestamp is the exact moment in UTC; the business date is the day the event counts for, which can differ.

**Before using it, know:** Date role, business calendar, time zone

**Connected to:**

- *resolved against* [Business Calendar](#business-calendar) — Together with a date-role value and time zone; both mandatory in operational queries.

### Business Calendar

*The published list of open and closed days, early closes and sessions for one venue, contract, CCP, currency or office.*

**Definition.** Versioned set of business and non-business days for a stated scope (trading venue or segment, contract, clearing service, payment system or currency, or a firm location), with early closes and, where relevant, the trading sessions and processing windows of each day and the rule that assigns their activity to a business date, published by an identified calendar owner in a stated time zone.

**Example.** CCP Z's clearing calendar shows 26 December as closed although Venue W, in another country, is open.

**Not to be confused with.** Cut-off: the calendar says which days count; a cut-off sets the time on such a day by which a step must be done.

### Trading Session

*A part of the trading day, such as the opening auction, continuous trading or an evening session, with its hours and the trade date it counts towards.*

**Definition.** Named period of a venue's trading day for a stated scope (venue, segment or contract), with start and end times in the venue's time zone, the trading phase it allows (auction, continuous, trade-at-settlement, registration only) and the rule that assigns activity in it to a business date.

**Example.** Venue V's evening session runs from 18:00 to 22:00 local time and counts towards the next business day's trade date.

**Not to be confused with.** Business Calendar: the calendar says which days are open; the session says which hours of a day and which trade date they count for.

**Part of:** [Business Calendar](#business-calendar)

**Values (draft list):** pre-open; opening auction; continuous trading; closing auction; evening or overnight session; negotiated-trade registration window

**Words people use:** “trading hours”; “trading phase”; “extended hours”

**Before using it, know:** Venue time zone and daylight-saving rule; which contracts the session applies to.

**Connected to:**

- *assigns trade dates of* [Business Date](#business-date)
- *is defined by* [Trading Venue](#trading-venue)
- *positions cut offs relative to* [Cut-off](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#cut-off)

### Processing Window

*A named stretch of a clearing day when a CCP or payment system accepts a given step.*

**Definition.** Named period of a clearing date or payment day, for a stated scope (clearing service, payment system), during which a process step is accepted, for example an intraday or end-of-day margin cycle, a give-up and take-up window, a late give-up or correction window, an exercise window or a payment cycle, with start and end times in a stated time zone and the business date it belongs to. Cut-offs, CCP reports, margin runs and positions are read against it. It is distinct from a Trading Session, which covers the venue's trading hours.

**Example.** CCP Z accepts give-ups for the clearing date from the end of trading until early evening, then opens a shorter late-correction window.

**Not to be confused with.** Cut-off: the cut-off is the latest time and its consequence; the window is the whole period when the step is accepted.

**Part of:** [Business Calendar](#business-calendar)

**Values (draft list):** intraday cycle; end-of-day cycle; give-up and take-up window; late give-up or correction window; exercise window; payment cycle

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service or payment system, business date, time zone, calendar version.

**Connected to:**

- *frames* [Cut-off](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#cut-off)
- *dates* [CCP Report](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#ccp-report)

### Identifier Scheme

*A numbering system, such as ISIN for instruments or MIC for venues, that says who issues the numbers, what they look like and where they are unique.*

**Definition.** Governed system for assigning identifiers to one type of business object (instrument, product, venue, party, account, order, execution, trade or give-up line), defining its issuing authority, format, uniqueness scope (global, per venue, per venue and session, per CCP, per issuer) and validity. The meanings of a Legal Entity Identifier code and of an Account Identifier stay on the parties page; this concept owns the general idea of a scheme and its uniqueness scope. New level-1 concept promoting the backlog item.

**Example.** Series June of Contract K carries an ISIN, Venue V's own instrument code and CCP Z's product code; three schemes, one instrument.

**Not to be confused with.** Product Family classification codes: a CFI code describes what kind of thing an instrument is; it does not say which one.

**Values (draft list):** ISIN; UPI; CFI (a classification code, not an identity); MIC (venue and segment); LEI (with its registration status kept as reference data); UTI; venue product code; CCP product code; venue order identifier; client order identifier (issued by the broker or client); venue execution or trade identifier (unique per venue and session); CCP trade identifier; venue member and trader identifiers; CCP member codes; executing broker codes used on give-ups; CCP account numbers; give-up reference or account mnemonic (per venue or CCP); the firm's own party and account codes

**Words people use:** “identifier type” (any); “symbology” (market data); “security ID source” (FIX)

**Before using it, know:** Which scheme a value belongs to and its uniqueness scope; for per-day schemes, the business date and issuing venue or CCP.

### Contract Adjustment

*A change a venue makes to existing contracts because something happened to the underlying, such as a share split or special dividend.*

**Definition.** Lifecycle event, decided by a venue and its CCP under the contract specification's adjustment rules, that changes the terms of existing series (multiplier, strike, deliverable, underlying) in response to a corporate or other event on the underlying, usually creating adjusted, non-standard series beside newly listed standard series.

**Example.** After a 2-for-1 split of Share S, Venue V adjusts existing Contract K options to strike 50 and multiplier 200, and lists new standard series beside them.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change: a rule change alters the specification for the future; an adjustment changes existing series after an underlying event under rules already in force.

**Kind of:** [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event)

**Values (draft list):** strike and multiplier adjustment; deliverable change; underlying replacement; early settlement or termination

**Words people use:** “corporate action adjustment”; “contract adjustment” (venue usage)

**Before using it, know:** Effective business date, the series affected and the adjusted terms; old and new series identifiers.

**Connected to:**

- *changes or creates* [Instrument](#instrument)
- *is triggered by an event on* [Underlying](#underlying)
- *changes the terms of positions in* [Position](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#position)
- *is announced by a notice not itself a rule change* [Rule Change](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-change)

### Clearing Contract

*The CCP's own version of a contract, under which it records positions, even when the contract is traded on more than one venue.*

**Definition.** Contract as defined and identified by a central counterparty within a clearing service, to which listed series from one or more venues are mapped; positions are held, and settlement prices published, per series of the clearing contract, each mapped from one or more venue series.

**Example.** Contract K is listed on Venue V and Venue W; CCP Z holds positions from both under one clearing contract with one product code.

**Not to be confused with.** Contract Specification: the venue publishes the specification for trading; the CCP's clearing contract is what positions and margin are held in.

**Part of:** [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)

**Words people use:** “clearing series” (for one series of the clearing contract); “CCP contract” (contextual)

**Before using it, know:** The clearing service and date; whether the venue-to-CCP mapping is one-to-one.

**Connected to:**

- *maps one or more of* [Contract Specification](#contract-specification)
- *is the contract in which positions are held* [Position](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#position)
- *is margined under* [Margin Methodology](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#margin-methodology)
- *is listed in* [Clearing Eligibility Schedule](#clearing-eligibility-schedule)
- [Settlement Price](#settlement-price) *may be published per series of* Clearing Contract

### Clearing Eligibility Schedule

*The CCP's published list of what it can clear, and within which limits.*

**Definition.** Versioned list, published by a central counterparty for a clearing service, of the contract specifications or, for OTC, product families with their trade-term ranges (currency, index, maturity, notional or other limits) that the service accepts for clearing, with effective dates and any conditions by account type or participant category; distinct from a regulatory clearing obligation and from the firm's offering.

**Example.** CCP Z's swap clearing service accepts fixed-to-floating swaps in currency C on index I up to a stated maximum maturity, from a stated effective date.

**Not to be confused with.** Collateral Eligibility Schedule: lists the assets a taker accepts as collateral; this schedule lists the contracts or OTC products a CCP accepts for clearing. And Clearing Obligation: what a regulation requires to be cleared.

**Part of:** [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)

**Words people use:** “clearing eligibility list” (contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, version and effective date; for OTC, the trade-term ranges.

**Connected to:**

- [Clearing Contract](#clearing-contract) *is listed in* Clearing Eligibility Schedule
- *lists as eligible* [Contract Specification](#contract-specification)
- *lists as eligible with trade term ranges otc* [Product Family](#product-family)
- *is changed by* [Rule Change](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-change)
- *is relied on by never equal to* [Service Offering](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-offering)
