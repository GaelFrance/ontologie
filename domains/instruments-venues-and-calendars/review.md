## 1. Concepts you approve

| Concept | Definition | Your verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[Product Family](concepts.md#product-family)** | Classification grouping of contract specifications, instruments or trades that share economic and contractual characteristics (asset class, underlying type, contract form such as future, option, forward or swap, settlement form), independent of any venue and of any counterparty; it may be organised in levels from asset class down to the finest product level at which an OTC product identifier is assigned. Not a venue's contract specification and not a service offering; a classification code such as a CFI code is a value of the classification, not the family's identity. ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Contract Specification](concepts.md#contract-specification)** | Versioned set of standard terms, published by the trading venue that lists a derivative contract and usually designated by that venue's product code, from which the contract's tradeable instruments (series) are created: underlying, size or multiplier, currency, price quotation and tick, expiry cycle and key-date rules, settlement method, option terms where relevant (call or put, exercise style, strike rules, premium or futures-style), adjustment rules and trading hours. Each version has an effective date and states the series to which it applies. | |
| **[Instrument](concepts.md#instrument)** | Standardised derivative specification, identifiable independently of any counterparty pair and any single trade, at the level at which it is listed or assigned an instrument identifier: for listed derivatives the series (contract, expiry and, for options, strike and call or put), including a strategy listed by the venue, whose legs are other instruments, and a flexible series created on request; for OTC derivatives only where a standard instrument identifier is assigned, including OTC derivatives traded on a venue that carry one. A bespoke OTC contract is not an instrument and its terms belong to the Trade. The asset, index or rate a derivative refers to is its underlying; it is an instrument only when it is itself a listed derivative. ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Trading Venue](concepts.md#trading-venue)** | Multilateral trading system or facility, operated under its own rules by an identified market operator, in which multiple third-party buying and selling interests meet, or privately negotiated trades are registered under its rules, so that contracts result; identified by a market identifier code and possibly divided into segments. Includes regulated markets, MTFs, OTFs, designated contract markets and swap execution facilities. Distinct from its operator, from a central counterparty, from a systematic internaliser or single-dealer platform, and from trade reporting services. ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Business Date](concepts.md#business-date)** | Calendar day (not a timestamp) assigned to a business object or event under a stated date role, such as trade date, CCP clearing date, value date or last trading day, resolved against a named business calendar and time zone; the event's UTC timestamp is kept beside it and need not fall on the same calendar day. | |
| **[Business Calendar](concepts.md#business-calendar)** | Versioned set of business and non-business days for a stated scope (trading venue or segment, contract, clearing service, payment system or currency, or a firm location), with early closes and, where relevant, the trading sessions and processing windows of each day and the rule that assigns their activity to a business date, published by an identified calendar owner in a stated time zone. | |
| **[Identifier Scheme](concepts.md#identifier-scheme)** | Governed system for assigning identifiers to one type of business object (instrument, product, venue, party, account, order, execution, trade or give-up line), defining its issuing authority, format, uniqueness scope (global, per venue, per venue and session, per CCP, per issuer) and validity. The meanings of a Legal Entity Identifier code and of an Account Identifier stay on the parties page; this concept owns the general idea of a scheme and its uniqueness scope. New level-1 concept promoting the backlog item. | |
| **[Trading Venue Segment](concepts.md#trading-venue-segment)** *(level 2)* | Distinct part of a trading venue, defined by the venue's rules, with its own scope of contracts or participants and possibly its own trading rules, sessions, fees or regulatory status; identified, where assigned, by a segment market identifier code linked to the venue's operating code. | |
| **[Listed Strategy](concepts.md#listed-strategy)** *(level 2)* | Instrument created by a trading venue, either predefined by the venue or defined on request by a participant, that combines two or more leg instruments in stated ratios and directions so that the combination trades as one, at one net price; its execution produces one execution per leg, and positions are held in the legs, not in the strategy. | |
| **[Flexible Series](concepts.md#flexible-series)** *(level 2)* | Listed series created on a participant's request within limits set by the contract specification or venue rules, with a non-standard expiry date, strike, exercise style or settlement method; it is listed, identified and cleared like a standard series but may have separate eligibility, minimum size, trading and fee rules. | |
| **[Underlying](concepts.md#underlying)** *(level 2)* | Asset, index, rate, commodity, other instrument or measurable variable to which a derivative contract refers and from which its value is derived, identified with its source (index administrator, benchmark, delivery specification) and, where the contract settles physically, the deliverable grade or basket. | |
| **[Key-Date Rule](concepts.md#key-date-rule)** *(level 2)* | Rule within a contract specification that determines, for each series, its role-qualified key dates (first trading day, last trading day, expiry, exercise deadline, first notice day, final settlement day, delivery day) from the expiry cycle and one or more named business calendars, including how a date falling on a non-business day is moved. | |
| **[Contract Adjustment](concepts.md#contract-adjustment)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event, decided by a venue and its CCP under the contract specification's adjustment rules, that changes the terms of existing series (multiplier, strike, deliverable, underlying) in response to a corporate or other event on the underlying, usually creating adjusted, non-standard series beside newly listed standard series. | |
| **[Clearing Contract](concepts.md#clearing-contract)** *(level 2)* | Contract as defined and identified by a central counterparty within a clearing service, to which listed series from one or more venues are mapped; positions are held, and settlement prices published, per series of the clearing contract, each mapped from one or more venue series. | |
| **[Clearing Eligibility Schedule](concepts.md#clearing-eligibility-schedule)** *(level 2)* | Versioned list, published by a central counterparty for a clearing service, of the contract specifications or, for OTC, product families with their trade-term ranges (currency, index, maturity, notional or other limits) that the service accepts for clearing, with effective dates and any conditions by account type or participant category; distinct from a regulatory clearing obligation and from the firm's offering. | |
| **[Trading Session](concepts.md#trading-session)** *(level 2)* | Named period of a venue's trading day for a stated scope (venue, segment or contract), with start and end times in the venue's time zone, the trading phase it allows (auction, continuous, trade-at-settlement, registration only) and the rule that assigns activity in it to a business date. | |
| **[Settlement Price](concepts.md#settlement-price)** *(level 2)* | Official price for a business date, of a stated kind (daily or final), published by the venue or by the CCP under its rules for a venue series or a series of the clearing contract; used to value listed positions, including for variation margin on futures and futures-style options and for premium margin, and to settle expiring contracts. Cleared OTC positions are valued by the CCP's own valuation, not by a settlement price. | |
| **[Processing Window](concepts.md#processing-window)** *(level 2)* | Named period of a clearing date or payment day, for a stated scope (clearing service, payment system), during which a process step is accepted, for example an intraday or end-of-day margin cycle, a give-up and take-up window, a late give-up or correction window, an exercise window or a payment cycle, with start and end times in a stated time zone and the business date it belongs to. Cut-offs, CCP reports, margin runs and positions are read against it. It is distinct from a Trading Session, which covers the venue's trading hours. | |

## 3. Open questions on meaning

A proposed answer comes from the expert review; it is **not a decision** until the owner confirms it.

### Business date (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Distinguish trade, clearing, value and accounting dates and calendars/time zones.

**Proposed answer:** Every date has a role (trade date, CCP clearing date, value date, booking date, reporting date, contract key dates such as last trading day or expiry, as-of date), resolved against a named Business Calendar and time zone; event timestamps are kept in UTC. Evening or overnight sessions can carry the next day's trade date (venue-specific). Extended trading hours move cut-offs, margin runs and give-up deadlines relative to the clearing date. Confidence high. Firm decision: default role and time zone of "today". [01, 02, 07]

### Product, instrument and OTC contract (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Validate identity boundaries for listed series and bespoke OTC agreements.

**Proposed answer:** Proposed (implemented in the Draft definitions of Product Family, Instrument and Contract Specification): Product Family is a classification (CFI ISO 10962 and the UPI ISO 4914 are classification and product identifiers); Contract Specification is a venue's versioned listing template; Instrument is a standardised, counterparty-independent specification at the level at which it is listed or assigned an instrument identifier (a listed series; an OTC ISIN where assigned, with the Trade still carrying full terms); a bespoke OTC contract is a Trade, not an Instrument. Confidence high. Reference Data and OTC Product to confirm the meaning change of Instrument. [01, 03, 07, 08]

<sub>reference LD-A0016</sub>

### Product (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Product family classification versus a venue's contract specification versus a listed series versus a service offering ("our clearing product").

**Proposed answer:** "Product" returns candidates Product Family, Contract Specification, Instrument and Service Offering unless context selects one; a venue's "product code" usually designates a Contract Specification. Confidence high. [01, 05, 07]

<sub>reference LD-A0021</sub>

### Dates of a rule change (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Publication, entry into force, application, phase-in and end of transition versus the date the firm recorded the change.

**Proposed answer:** Each Rule Change carries role-qualified dates: publication, entry into force, application, phase-in and end of transition, plus the date the firm recorded it. "Effective date" without a role is ambiguous. Confidence high. [02, 05, 06, 08]

<sub>reference LD-A0022</sub>

### Current, as of, as known at (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Valid time versus record time: valid now and latest non-superseded record, valid at a business date, or known by a timestamp.

**Proposed answer:** Current = valid now and latest non-superseded record from the stated source; as of D = valid at business date D; as known at T = only records received by T. Every answer states source, as-of date and freshness. See the time convention in [modelling guidelines](../docs/modelling-guidelines.md). Confidence high. Firm decision: whether "as known at" history is needed. [07, 08, 06]

<sub>reference LD-A0023</sub>

### Eligible, mandatory, offered, covered (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** CCP clearing eligibility versus a regulatory clearing obligation for a counterparty pair versus the firm's offering versus "we cover market X" (membership, third-party access or actual offering).

**Proposed answer:** Eligibility is a CCP fact; a clearing obligation applies to a counterparty pair, a product class including trade-term ranges, and a date; offering and coverage are firm facts in the internal overlay, through a Market Access Arrangement. Never infer one from another. Confidence high. [03, 05, 06]

<sub>reference LD-A0029</sub>

### OTC and listed (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Regulatory "OTC" (derivatives not executed on a regulated market or equivalent) versus off-venue versus not exchange-listed; cleared is a separate dimension.

**Proposed answer:** Under EMIR Art. 2(7) a derivative not executed on a regulated market or an equivalent third-country market is OTC, so MTF and OTF trades count as OTC; venue type is a Regulatory Classification of Trading Venue. Scope modules by listing and clearing structure, not by that label. Confidence high. [06, 02, 03]
