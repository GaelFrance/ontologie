### Service Offering

*A service the firm sells to clients, such as execute-and-clear on listed equity index futures for institutional clients, described by what it covers, where and through which channels.*

**Definition.** Defined execution, clearing or related post-trade service that a contracting entity makes available to clients under stated terms, identified by its service kind (execution-only, clearing-only, execute-and-clear, or a related post-trade service such as average pricing or handling of trades given in), its product scope stated by product family, contract specification or clearing contract, the trading venues, venue segments or clearing services it relies on, its order handling channels and the client segments it is offered to, with a status and an effective period. The service kind indicates the usual give-up direction (execution-only usually means give-up out, clearing-only usually means give-in, by clearing submission for OTC); the actual direction of each trade is recorded on the give-up line and can differ. Distinct from a central counterparty's clearing service, from the Client role a client holds toward the contracting entity for this offering, and from the prices agreed with that client.

**Example.** An execution-only offering for Client A on Venue V, where Client A's trades are given up to Clearing Broker Y.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Service: that is a clearing house's own rulebook-defined service; the firm's offering may rely on it but is not it.

**Breaks down into:** [Service Component](#service-component) (part of), [Offering Coverage Line](#offering-coverage-line) (part of)

**Words people use:** “product” (Any); “service” (Any)

**Connected to:**

- [Fee Charge](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#fee-charge) *charged for* Service Offering — The service concerned; service nature is a mandatory facet.
- *relies on* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service), [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue) — Through a market access arrangement; instances are internal-overlay facts.
- [Contracting Entity](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#contracting-entity) *provides* Service Offering
- [Clearing Eligibility Schedule](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#clearing-eligibility-schedule) *is relied on by never equal to* Service Offering
- [Offering Eligibility Criterion](#offering-eligibility-criterion) *applies to* Service Offering
- [Client Pricing Terms](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#client-pricing-terms) *is agreed for* Service Offering
- [Bundled Execution and Clearing Fee](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#bundled-execution-and-clearing-fee) *is charged for with service kind execute and clear* Service Offering

### Service Component

*One building block of a service, for example average pricing inside an execute-and-clear service.*

**Definition.** Separately describable element of a service offering that the provider performs and may document, price or withdraw on its own, such as order execution, give-up processing, take-up of trades given in, average pricing, allocation, clearing and position maintenance, margin and collateral handling, exercise and delivery handling, or delegated regulatory reporting. A component belongs to one or more offerings; it is not a service offering unless sold on its own.

**Example.** Broker B's execute-and-clear offering for Client A includes broker-side average pricing as a component priced separately.

**Not to be confused with.** Service Kind: the kind classifies the whole offering; a component is one piece inside it.

**Part of:** [Service Offering](#service-offering)

**Values (draft list):** order execution; give-up processing; take-up of trades given in; average pricing; allocation; clearing and position maintenance; margin and collateral handling; exercise and delivery handling; delegated regulatory reporting

**Words people use:** “ancillary service” (Any); “service feature” (product usage)

**Before using it, know:** Which offering it belongs to; whether it is bundled or priced separately.

**Connected to:**

- *is priced by for the firm s own client charges* [Fee Rate](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#fee-rate)
- [Regulatory Reporting Role](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-reporting-role) *is relied on by delegated reporting* Service Component

### Offering Coverage Line

*One line of 'where and for what' a service is available, and which access route makes it possible.*

**Definition.** One entry in a service offering's coverage: the offering is available for a stated product scope (product family, contract specification or clearing contract) on one trading venue or venue segment, or in one clearing service, relying on a stated market access arrangement, with a status and an effective period.

**Example.** Broker B's clearing-only offering covers equity index options in the clearing service of CCP Z, relying on Broker B's general clearing membership there.

**Not to be confused with.** Market Access Arrangement: the arrangement is the route into the venue or CCP; the coverage line says the firm sells a service using that route.

**Part of:** [Service Offering](#service-offering)

**Words people use:** “coverage” (ambiguous: membership, access route or offering); “market coverage” (Any)

**Before using it, know:** Offering, venue or clearing service, product scope, as-of date (default: clearing date of the relevant CCP) and the status of the arrangement it relies on.

**Connected to:**

- *relies on* [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)
- *covers trading on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *covers clearing in* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *states its product scope by* [Contract Specification](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#contract-specification)
- *is available through* [Order Handling Channel](../execution/concepts.md#order-handling-channel)

### Market Access Arrangement

*The route by which someone gets into one venue or one clearing house: as a member, through another member, as a client, or under a sponsored model, with its status and dates.*

**Definition.** Access relationship by which one party can execute on one trading venue or venue segment, or clear in one clearing service, under one access model (execution: own venue membership by category, through a third-party executing member, or direct electronic access as direct market access or sponsored access; clearing: own clearing membership by category, as a non-clearing member through a general clearing member, client clearing, indirect clearing, or sponsored clearing or a direct-client model), naming the intermediary through which the party reaches the venue or clearing service; where clearing passes through a chain, each link is a separate arrangement and the chain is recorded in order. It records the role it confers, a status (only a live arrangement can be relied on) and an effective period, and is documented by one or more agreements, which are separate objects and may serve several arrangements. Execution access and clearing access are always separate arrangements. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Broker B reaches Venue V through Member M, a third-party executing member there, because Broker B is not a venue member; the route is live from a given date.

**Not to be confused with.** Trading Venue Member or Clearing Member: those are the roles a route can give; the route is the relationship and paperwork that gives them.

**Kind of:** [Agreement](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#agreement)

**Breaks down into:** [Direct Electronic Access Arrangement](#direct-electronic-access-arrangement) (kind of), [Third-Party Execution Arrangement](#third-party-execution-arrangement) (kind of), [Non-Clearing Member Arrangement](#non-clearing-member-arrangement) (kind of), [Sponsored Clearing Arrangement](#sponsored-clearing-arrangement) (kind of), [Indirect Clearing Arrangement](#indirect-clearing-arrangement) (kind of), [Access Onboarding](#access-onboarding) (step in), [Connectivity Route](#connectivity-route) (part of)

**Connected to:**

- *grants access to* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue), [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service) — Execution access to a venue or clearing access to a clearing service. Coverage is never inferred from memberships alone.
- *confers* [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role) — For example a Trading Venue Member, Clearing Member or Client role assignment; the arrangement and the resulting role are distinct.
- *intermediated by* [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role) — The intermediary's role assignment (venue member, clearing member or clearing broker); zero or one.
- [Indirect Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#indirect-client) *reaches clearing through* Market Access Arrangement
- [Non-Clearing Member](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#non-clearing-member) *is conferred by* Market Access Arrangement
- [Offering Coverage Line](#offering-coverage-line) *relies on* Market Access Arrangement
- [Access Agreement](#access-agreement) *documents* Market Access Arrangement
- [Access Requirement](#access-requirement) *must be met for* Market Access Arrangement
- [Indirect Client Account](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#indirect-client-account) *results from an indirect clearing access arrangement* Market Access Arrangement

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Eligible, mandatory, offered, covered” (open, answer proposed); “Client clearing model” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Direct Electronic Access Arrangement

*Permission given by a venue member for a client to send its own orders to the venue under the member's identity.*

**Definition.** Market access arrangement in which a venue member (the provider) permits a client (the user) to send orders electronically to a trading venue under the member's trading identity, as direct market access through the member's infrastructure or as sponsored access; the provider and the user are the two named parties of the arrangement, not new roles. Orders on the direct market access or sponsored access channel require a live arrangement of the same form.

**Example.** Client A sends orders to Venue V through Broker B's gateway under a direct market access arrangement.

**Not to be confused with.** Sponsored Clearing Arrangement: sponsored access is about sending orders; sponsored clearing is about being admitted to a clearing house.

**Kind of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Values (draft list):** direct market access; sponsored access

**Words people use:** “DEA” (EU); “DMA” (ambiguous: also any low-touch electronic flow); “sponsored access” (execution); “naked access” (informal, avoid)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, member granting access, client, form (direct market access or sponsored access), status and effective period.

**Connected to:**

- *is granted by the client s* [Trading Venue Member](../execution/concepts.md#trading-venue-member)
- *is the standing permission behind orders on the channels direct market access and sponsored access* [Order Handling Channel](../execution/concepts.md#order-handling-channel)
- *is subject to controls set as* [Access Requirement](#access-requirement)
- [Pre-trade Control Check](../execution/concepts.md#pre-trade-control-check) *requires for direct market access or sponsored access orders a live* Direct Electronic Access Arrangement

### Third-Party Execution Arrangement

*A broker reaches a venue where it is not a member by passing orders to a broker that is.*

**Definition.** Market access arrangement by which a party that is not a member of a trading venue, or is a member but chooses not to use its membership for that business, has orders executed there by a Third-party Executing Member acting under an agreement with it; brokerage owed to that member is executing brokerage paid.

**Example.** Broker B sends Client A's orders for Venue V to Member M, a third-party executing member on Venue V, because Broker B is not a member there.

**Not to be confused with.** Give-up: third-party execution concerns who executes on the venue; give-up concerns which clearing member ends up with the trade.

**Kind of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Words people use:** “third-party execution” (sponsor usage); “order routing to another broker” (Any); “executing broker of the executing broker” (informal)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, intermediary, the firm's role toward its own client, status and effective period.

**Connected to:**

- *is intermediated by the venue member role of* [Trading Venue Member](../execution/concepts.md#trading-venue-member)
- *lets the firm act as the client s executing broker while another party is the venue member so it never implies* [Executing Broker](../execution/concepts.md#executing-broker)
- *gives rise to brokerage owed to the intermediary* [Executing Broker Brokerage Charge](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#executing-broker-brokerage-charge)
- [Third-party Executing Member](../execution/concepts.md#third-party-executing-member) *is the intermediary of* Third-Party Execution Arrangement

### Non-Clearing Member Arrangement

*A venue member that is not a clearing member gets its trades cleared by a clearing member that is.*

**Definition.** Market access arrangement by which a trading venue member that is not a clearing member of the linked clearing service has its trades cleared by a general clearing member; it is documented by a Client Clearing Agreement (a CCP standard form where one exists, to verify), and the non-clearing member is a client of that clearing member.

**Example.** Broker B is a member of Venue V but not of CCP Z; Clearing Broker Y, a general clearing member at CCP Z, clears Broker B's trades.

**Not to be confused with.** Client clearing: any client may clear through a clearing member; a non-clearing member is a venue member whose status the CCP recognises.

**Kind of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Words people use:** “NCM” (listed markets); “NCM-GCM relationship” (listed markets)

**Before using it, know:** CCP and clearing service, venue, intermediary, status and effective period.

**Connected to:**

- *grants clearing access to* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *is intermediated by the general clearing member role of* [Clearing Member](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-member)
- *confers* [Non-Clearing Member](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#non-clearing-member)

### Sponsored Clearing Arrangement

*A client is admitted directly to a clearing house, with a clearing member standing behind it under the clearing house's rules.*

**Definition.** Market access arrangement under a CCP's sponsored or direct-client model by which a party is admitted to a clearing service with trades registered in its own name, with a Sponsoring Clearing Member as intermediary; 'clearing agent' is recorded as a value where the rulebook uses that term (to verify).

**Example.** Client A is admitted to the clearing service of CCP Z under its direct-client model, with Clearing Broker Y as sponsor.

**Not to be confused with.** Direct Electronic Access Arrangement in its sponsored access form: that concerns sending orders to a venue, not clearing.

**Kind of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Values (draft list):** intermediary form: sponsoring clearing member; clearing agent (where the rulebook provides, to verify)

**Words people use:** “sponsored clearing” (CCP usage); “direct-client model” (CCP usage); “sponsored” (ambiguous: execution or clearing)

**Before using it, know:** CCP and clearing service, sponsor, participant, status, effective period; which CCP model applies.

**Connected to:**

- *grants clearing access to* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)

### Indirect Clearing Arrangement

*Clearing for someone who is a client of a client of the clearing member.*

**Definition.** Market access arrangement by which a party clears in a clearing service as a client of a client of a clearing member (or further down a chain); each link is a separate arrangement and the chain is recorded in order. It results in an Indirect Client Account at the CCP, whose forms are defined on the clearing services page.

**Example.** Broker B clears at CCP Z through Clearing Broker Y and offers clearing to Client A, who becomes an indirect client at CCP Z.

**Not to be confused with.** Client clearing: there the clearing member's client is the party itself; in indirect clearing there is one more link in the chain.

**Kind of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Words people use:** “indirect clearing” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, clearing service, each link in the chain, account option, status and effective period.

**Connected to:**

- *grants clearing access to* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *confers* [Indirect Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#indirect-client)
- *is documented at each link by a* [Client Clearing Agreement](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#client-clearing-agreement)
- *offers account options governed by* [Segregation Model](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#segregation-model)

### Access Onboarding

*The steps the firm, or a client, goes through to get a new route into a venue or clearing house.*

**Definition.** Process by which a party obtains a market access arrangement: choice of access model and category, application, the venue's, CCP's or intermediary's due diligence, legal documentation, meeting access requirements, connectivity set-up and conformance testing, static data and account set-up, and go-live; it ends when the arrangement is live or the application is withdrawn. Its steps are reported as events with dates.

**Example.** To clear for clients at CCP Z, Broker B applies as a general clearing member, signs the admission documents, completes testing and goes live.

**Not to be confused with.** Client onboarding: answered from the Client role a client holds toward the contracting entity for an offering, with its status, agreements and mapped accounts; access onboarding sets up a route into a venue or CCP.

**Step in:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Values (draft list):** application; due diligence; legal documentation; requirements met; connectivity and conformance testing; static and account set-up; go-live; withdrawn

**Words people use:** “membership application” (Any); “market onboarding” (UC-002 usage)

**Before using it, know:** Holder, target venue or clearing service, access model and category.

**Connected to:**

- *must satisfy* [Access Requirement](#access-requirement)
- *sets up* [Connectivity Route](#connectivity-route)

### Connectivity Route

*The technical line used to reach a venue or clearing house.*

**Definition.** Technical path by which a holder of a market access arrangement sends and receives orders, trade registrations or clearing messages with a venue or CCP: a direct connection (co-located or remote), a connection through an independent software vendor or service bureau, through another member's infrastructure, or an order-routing network; with its sessions or user identifiers, status and effective period.

**Example.** Client A's sponsored access orders reach Venue V over Client A's own direct connection using Broker B's member identity.

**Not to be confused with.** Direct Electronic Access Arrangement: that is the permission to send orders under a member's identity; the connectivity route is the technical line, which may be the member's or the client's.

**Part of:** [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)

**Values (draft list):** direct, co-located; direct, remote; through an independent software vendor or service bureau; through another member's infrastructure; order-routing network

**Words people use:** “connectivity” (Any); “session, gateway” (venue usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Venue or CCP, holder, purpose (trading, clearing, both).

**Connected to:**

- [Access Onboarding](#access-onboarding) *sets up* Connectivity Route
- *generates cost of category connectivity and market data* [Cost](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#cost)

### Offering Eligibility Criterion

*A rule the firm applies to decide which clients can get a service.*

**Definition.** Condition set by the provider that a client must meet to receive a service offering or one of its coverage lines: party type, client segment, Client Regulatory Category or Counterparty Regulatory Status, jurisdiction, credit approval or documents signed, each as a separate criterion. A criterion that applies a regulatory condition refers to the Regulatory Classification or Regulatory Obligation it applies. Instances are recorded only in the internal layer.

**Example.** Broker B offers direct electronic access on Venue V only to clients that passed its electronic trading due diligence.

**Not to be confused with.** Access Requirement (set by a venue, CCP or regulator for the route itself); Clearing Eligibility Schedule (what the CCP accepts); Clearing Limit (how much the clearing member accepts automatically).

**Kind of:** [Rule Provision](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-provision)

**Words people use:** “client eligibility” (Any); “onboarding criteria” (Any)

**Before using it, know:** Which offering and coverage line; the client and its regulatory classification as of the date asked.

**Connected to:**

- *applies to* [Service Offering](#service-offering)
- *is tested against* [Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client)
- *may require a stated value of* [Regulatory Classification](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-classification)

### Access Agreement

*A signed document that sets up or governs a route into a venue or clearing house.*

**Definition.** Agreement that documents a market access arrangement and is not already a give-up agreement or a client clearing agreement: admission to a venue or clearing service under its rules, a direct electronic access agreement between a venue member and its client, an order-routing agreement with a third-party venue member, a non-clearing member clearing agreement, or a sponsorship or clearing agent agreement under a CCP sponsored model. One agreement may document several arrangements, and one arrangement may need several agreements.

**Example.** Client A signs a direct electronic access agreement with Broker B before sending orders to Venue V under Broker B's membership.

**Not to be confused with.** Give-up Agreement, Client Clearing Agreement (which also documents the non-clearing member model) and Execution Agreement for Cleared Trades: each keeps its own concept.

**Kind of:** [Agreement](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#agreement)

**Values (draft list):** venue admission or membership agreement; CCP clearing membership agreement; direct electronic access agreement; third-party execution agreement; sponsored clearing or clearing agent agreement

**Words people use:** “membership agreement” (Any); “DEA agreement” (EU); “NCM agreement” (listed markets)

**Before using it, know:** Parties, governing rules or law, version and effective period; which arrangements it documents.

**Connected to:**

- *documents* [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)
- *is distinct from* [Give-up Agreement](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up-agreement)
- *is distinct from* [Client Clearing Agreement](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#client-clearing-agreement)

### Access Requirement

*A condition a venue, clearing house or regulator sets before someone can use a given route in.*

**Definition.** Rule provision of a trading venue, CCP, regulator or intermediary that a party must meet to obtain or keep a given access model or membership category, such as minimum capital, default fund contribution, regulatory authorisation or registration, operational and risk-management capability, technical conformance, qualified staff, settlement and payment arrangements, or pre-trade controls for direct electronic access; versioned, with issuer and effective period.

**Example.** CCP Z requires a general clearing member to hold a stated minimum capital and contribute to the default fund.

**Not to be confused with.** Offering Eligibility Criterion: that is the firm's own rule for clients; an access requirement is imposed on the holder of the route.

**Kind of:** [Rule Provision](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-provision)

**Values (draft list):** regulatory; financial; operational and technical; legal and documentary; staff qualification

**Words people use:** “admission criteria” (CCP and venue usage); “membership requirements” (Any); “participation requirements” (PFMI)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, access model and category it applies to, version and effective period.

**Connected to:**

- [Access Onboarding](#access-onboarding) *must satisfy* Access Requirement
- *must be met for* [Market Access Arrangement](#market-access-arrangement)
- *may require* [Regulatory Authorisation](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-authorisation)
- *may implement* [Regulatory Obligation](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-obligation)
- *may require* [Default Fund Contribution](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#default-fund-contribution)
- [Direct Electronic Access Arrangement](#direct-electronic-access-arrangement) *is subject to controls set as* Access Requirement
