# Service offering and market access: overview

## What this domain covers

This domain answers two questions that sit above every trade: **what do we sell**, and **how do we, or our clients, get into each market**.

A derivatives broker sells a few services: execution only (we execute, the client clears elsewhere), clearing only (the client executes elsewhere and gives the trades to us) and execute-and-clear (both), plus related post-trade services such as average pricing. Each service is offered for a defined range of contracts, on certain venues and clearing houses, through certain order handling channels, to certain kinds of client.

To deliver any of this, someone needs a route into each venue and each clearing house. The firm may be a member itself, or reach the venue through another member, or clear through another clearing member. Clients may in turn reach the venue electronically under the firm's membership, or be admitted to a clearing house under a sponsored model.

## Why it matters

"Can we onboard Client A for contract Y at clearing house Z?" needs the offering, the client's eligibility and a live access route at once. "How much does each service earn?" needs a stable definition of the service. Getting this wrong gives confident answers such as "yes, we clear that" when the firm is only a venue member.

## Who is involved

Product owners decide the offering. Venues and clearing houses set admission rules and membership categories. Other brokers act as intermediaries where the firm is not a member. Regulators define the access routes and their controls. Legal documents each route.

## How it flows in practice

The firm defines a service offering. For each venue or clearing service the offering relies on, the firm needs an access arrangement: it applies, meets the requirements, signs the documents, connects, tests and goes live. The arrangement then gives a role, such as venue member or clearing member, under a category. A client is set up for the offering, signs the agreements and, where allowed, gets its own electronic or clearing access. Later, routes are suspended or ended and offerings launched or withdrawn.

## Ideas to hold apart

1. **Our offering and a clearing house's service.** We sell a service; a clearing house runs a clearing service under its rulebook. Both are called "service".
2. **Offered, eligible, mandatory and covered.** What we sell, what the clearing house accepts, what the law requires to be cleared, and where we have a live route are four separate facts.
3. **Membership and access route.** Membership is one way in. Others are going through another member, clearing as a client, or clearing through a chain.
4. **Execution access and clearing access.** Being able to trade on a venue says nothing about being able to clear its contracts, and the reverse.
5. **Sponsored access and sponsored clearing.** The first lets a client send orders under a member's identity. The second admits a client directly to a clearing house with a sponsoring member behind it.
6. **A standing permission and one order's route.** A client's electronic access arrangement is not the same as how a given order was handled.
7. **The offering, the client's set-up and the client's prices.** Three different things, approved by different people.
