## 1. Concepts you approve

| Concept | Definition | Your verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[Lifecycle Event](concepts.md#lifecycle-event)** | Dated occurrence that creates, changes or ends a trade, position, give-up, obligation or other business object of the trading and clearing lifecycle, or its legal effect, recorded with when it happened, when it takes effect and when it was recorded, plus business dates by role. A correction or cancellation is a new event that references the original; nothing is deleted. Changes to rules, calendars or fee schedules are Rule Changes, not lifecycle events. | |
| **[Cut-off](concepts.md#cut-off)** | Rule fixing the latest time by which a stated process step must be completed, set by a rule provision (regulation, CCP or venue rulebook, payment system rule, firm procedure) or by a term of an agreement, the source kind being recorded on each cut-off; expressed either as a local time on a business date of a named business calendar, read against the relevant processing window, or as a period after a stated triggering event; it states the consequence of missing it (rejection, lapse, late handling, charge, default procedure) and the authority that sets it. Only cut-offs set by a rule provision follow the Rule Change lifecycle. Versioned; on a given day it resolves to one exact time, which can move with early closes or extended trading hours. ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Settlement Obligation](concepts.md#settlement-obligation)** | Duty of an identified debtor toward an identified creditor to transfer a stated amount of cash in a currency, or a stated quantity of an asset, by a due date and settlement deadline through a stated settlement route, arising from an identified source: variation margin settled to market, price alignment, option premium, final cash settlement, physical delivery, a margin call to be met in cash or assets, a fee invoice or interest. It can be settled in part and netted with other obligations into one payment. Distinct from the requirement or charge it comes from and from the cash movement that settles it. | |
| **[Cash Movement](concepts.md#cash-movement)** | Transfer of funds in one currency, of a stated amount and value date, from a payer to a payee, evidenced by an identified source: a payment system, settlement bank or CCP for an external payment, or the firm's books for a transfer between accounts the firm maintains. Distinct from the settlement obligation it settles (it may settle several netted obligations, or part of one) and from the payment instruction that asked for it. A cash collateral transfer is both a cash movement and a collateral movement and is counted once. | |
| **[Operational Exception](concepts.md#operational-exception)** | Situation, raised against one or more identified business objects (trade, give-up line, position, settlement obligation, cash movement, reconciliation break), in which an expected outcome or rule has not been met and which needs action by an identified owner, usually before a stated deadline; it holds from when it is raised until it is resolved or closed. A reconciliation break, a rejected or lapsed give-up, a missed cut-off or a settlement fail can raise one but is not one. Its handling is recorded in an exception case. Technology incidents are out of scope. | |
| **[Reconciliation](concepts.md#reconciliation)** | Process of comparing two or more records of the same business objects held by different sources (for example the firm's books and a CCP report), of a stated type (trade, give-up, position, cash, margin, collateral, fee), scope, business date by role and cycle, using stated matching keys and tolerances, to establish which items match and which differ (reconciliation breaks). It does not by itself decide which record is right; where a rule names an authoritative record, the difference is still reported as a break, never silently resolved. | |
| **[Source Record](concepts.md#source-record)** | A record of a business object (trade, give-up line, position, margin requirement, margin call, settlement obligation, cash movement, access arrangement status) as held or reported by one identified source (a function of the observing firm's own books, a CCP, a venue, an executing or clearing broker, a settlement bank or a client), with that source's identifiers, native status and record time. Every harmonised status in the ontology is asserted in a source record, with the native status kept beside it; other pages refer to this rule instead of restating it. New level-1 concept promoting the backlog item. | |
| **[CCP Report](concepts.md#ccp-report)** *(level 2)* | Source record issued by a CCP to a clearing member for one clearing service, a set of CCP accounts and a clearing date (and cycle, where the CCP runs several), stating the trades, give-up and take-up results, positions, exercises and assignments, margin requirements, collateral and cash settlement amounts as the CCP has recorded them. It is the authoritative record for give-up completion (sponsor decision); for other questions its authority is a stated rule per question. | |
| **[Reconciliation Break](concepts.md#reconciliation-break)** *(level 2)* | A difference, found by a reconciliation, between records of the same business object held by different sources, or a record present in one source and missing in the other, that exceeds the stated tolerance. It says the records disagree, not which one is right. It holds from detection until it is explained and cleared, and keeps its age, the records concerned and, once known, its cause. A break may raise an operational exception; it is not one, and it does not prove that the underlying trade or payment failed. | |
| **[Position Reconciliation](concepts.md#position-reconciliation)** *(level 2)* | Reconciliation of open positions between the observing firm's books and a CCP report (at the member tier) or a client's or another broker's records (at the client tier), per CCP account, CCP position account or internal account, series of the clearing contract, position basis (gross long and short, or net), clearing date and processing window. | |
| **[Exception Case](concepts.md#exception-case)** *(level 2)* | The record of handling one or more operational exceptions: owner, priority, the business objects and counterparties concerned, actions taken, handling state, deadline and outcome. One case can group several exceptions with the same cause. Closing a case does not change any source record; corrections are made by new lifecycle events or source corrections. | |
| **[Settlement Fail](concepts.md#settlement-fail)** *(level 2)* | The record of one settlement obligation not settled in full by its settlement deadline: the unsettled amount or quantity, the deadline missed, its age, and how it ended (settled late, cancelled, replaced by a close-out or default procedure). A partial settlement leaves the unsettled part in fail. The general settlement status of an obligation (pending, settled, partially settled, failed, cancelled, unknown) is a value list on Settlement Obligation, not this concept. | |
| **[Netted Settlement Obligation](concepts.md#netted-settlement-obligation)** *(level 2)* | Settlement obligation formed by netting several settlement obligations between the same two parties, in either direction (for example between a CCP and a clearing member, or a clearing member and a client), in the same currency, with the same due date and settlement route, into one net amount payable in one direction, under the agreement or clearing service rule that allows that netting. Settling it settles each underlying obligation, which keep their own purpose and amount. | |
| **[Payment Instruction](concepts.md#payment-instruction)** *(level 2)* | Instruction given by or for the payer (or, under a direct debit arrangement, by the payee) to a settlement bank, payment system or the firm's own ledger, to transfer a stated amount in a currency on a value date in order to settle one or more settlement obligations. It can be released, rejected, amended or cancelled; it is not the cash movement it asks for. | |
| **[Settlement Account](concepts.md#settlement-account)** *(level 2)* | Account, maintained for a party by a settlement bank, payment system, CCP, custodian or triparty agent, through which the party settles cash obligations in a currency (cash settlement account), delivers and receives securities or commodities (delivery account), or holds or pledges collateral (collateral account: custody, pledge or triparty). The account is real; the observing firm holds its identifier. Not a client's cash account kept in the broker's own books, which is a Client Account, and not the CCP account in which positions are recorded. | |
| **[Settlement Deadline](concepts.md#settlement-deadline)** *(level 2)* | Cut-off by which a settlement obligation must be settled, set by the creditor's rules (CCP rulebook, payment system rules) or by a term of an agreement (for example a client clearing agreement); a margin call deadline is one. Its place under Cut-off depends on the sponsor's decision on the Cut-off meaning change. | |
| **[Firm Cut-off](concepts.md#firm-cut-off)** *(level 2)* | Cut-off set by the observing firm, in its own procedure or in a client agreement, for a client or an internal team ahead of the market cut-off it protects (for example an allocation, give-up, take-up, exercise-instruction, delivery or payment deadline), so that the firm can complete its own step in time. Its place under Cut-off depends on the sponsor's decision on the Cut-off meaning change. | |
| **[Expiry](concepts.md#expiry)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event at which a listed series, or an OTC contract, reaches the end of its life on the expiry date set by its contract specification or terms: trading has stopped, and open positions are closed by final settlement (futures), or exercised or abandoned (options), so that no position in the expiring series remains afterwards. Exercise can create a position in the underlying (for example a futures position), and settlement or delivery obligations created at expiry can remain open after it. | |
| **[Exercise](concepts.md#exercise)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event by which the holder of a long option position uses its right for a stated quantity under the clearing service's rules: on the holder's instruction (early exercise of an American-style option, or at expiry), or automatically at expiry when the option is in the money by the CCP's threshold and no contrary instruction was given. It creates obligations settled in cash or by delivery (or, for an option on a future, a futures position) and leads to assignment on the short side. | |
| **[Exercise Instruction](concepts.md#exercise-instruction)** *(level 2)* | Instruction from the holder of a long option position (a client to its clearing broker, a clearing member to the CCP) to exercise a stated quantity, or at expiry not to exercise or to exercise against the automatic rule (a contrary instruction), before the applicable cut-off. It can be amended or cancelled until that cut-off. | |
| **[Assignment](concepts.md#assignment)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event by which exercised option quantities are allocated to short positions in the same series: first by the CCP to clearing members' accounts under its published method, then by each clearing member to its clients' short positions under its own method. The assigned short must pay or deliver, or takes the resulting futures position. | |
| **[Final Settlement](concepts.md#final-settlement)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event that closes an expiring futures position, or an exercised or assigned option position, by cash settlement against a final settlement price or by physical delivery of the underlying, creating the settlement obligations concerned. Cash or physical settlement is a term of the contract specification. | |
| **[Delivery Notice](concepts.md#delivery-notice)** *(level 2)* | Notice, for a physically delivered futures contract, by which a short position holder declares its intention to deliver (and, where the rules require, a long holder states its readiness or preferences), within the notice period set by the contract specification, after which the CCP allocates the delivery to long positions. | |
| **[Trade Amendment](concepts.md#trade-amendment)** *(level 2)* | Lifecycle event that changes or ends a trade registered for clearing, made under the clearing service's rules or by agreement of the parties, referring to the original, which is kept: change of economic terms where allowed (mainly OTC), partial termination, full termination (unwind), or correction of a non-economic attribute that does not move the trade at the CCP (for example internal client account or client reference). For a CCP-facing trade, an economic change or termination ends or reduces the trade and creates a linked successor; only a non-economic correction is recorded against the same trade. A move of positions to another CCP account or clearing member is a Position Transfer or a give-up step, not an amendment; a correction from the venue's error-trade rules or the broker's client-facing correction is an Execution Correction. | |
| **[Option Premium](concepts.md#option-premium)** *(level 2)* | Amount the buyer of an option pays the seller for it: the trade price times quantity and contract multiplier. For premium-style listed options it is paid in full on the settlement of the trade, through the CCP and down each clearing tier, giving rise to settlement obligations; for futures-style options no premium is paid upfront and the option's value moves through variation margin instead. For OTC options the premium amount and payment date are terms of the trade. Premium paid or received is posted to the account's cash balance. | |
| **[Client Statement](concepts.md#client-statement)** *(level 2)* | Source record issued by a clearing broker or executing broker to a client for one or more of the client's accounts and a business date or period (daily, monthly), stating as the broker has recorded them the trades and executions, positions, cash balance, open trade equity and account equity, margin requirement and excess or shortfall, fees, commissions and cash movements. Its minimum content and frequency are set partly by regulation (to verify per regime) and by the client agreement. It is the broker's record toward the client, not the CCP's record (CCP Report), and not a demand for payment (Fee Invoice). | |

## 3. Open questions on meaning

A proposed answer comes from the expert review; it is **not a decision** until the owner confirms it.

### Fee, revenue and cash received (Open, narrowed 2026-09-20, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Separate charges, management revenue and cash settlement; define timing, gross/net, pass-through treatment and the boundary with contribution after costs.

**Proposed answer:** A charge accrues at its chargeable event, is then invoiced or debited and finally settled (Cash Movement); amount certainty (estimated or final) and billing stage are facets of Fee Charge. Collateral income is a separate stream whose inclusion in clearing revenue is undecided. Gross or net treatment of pass-through charges is a Finance decision; accounting principal-versus-agent guidance is only an analogue. Confidence high on stages, medium on treatment. [05, 04, 02]

<sub>reference LD-A0002</sub>

### Trade, transaction and execution (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Establish identity and lineage; Transaction is provisional and must not collapse all lifecycle events.

**Proposed answer:** Three identity levels linked by directional lineage, never by a fixed cardinality: the execution event, the trade, and successor trades after clearing. Each level carries its own identifiers (for example venue trade IDs, UTI ISO 23897). The MiFIR "transaction" (Art. 26) is a regulatory reporting unit and an ambiguous alias, never Transaction, which stays deferred. Confidence high. [01, 03, 06, 07, 08]

<sub>reference LD-A0008</sub>

### Variation margin (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Specify collateral versus settlement treatment under the governing arrangement.

**Proposed answer:** Settled-to-market: the daily payment settles exposure and is accompanied by a price alignment amount. Collateralised-to-market: VM is collateral to be returned, with price alignment interest. The treatment is a versioned fact per clearing service and account type; some OTC CCPs converted some or all accounts to settled-to-market (dates per service to verify). Price alignment is never part of VM. Confidence high on concepts, medium on per-CCP facts. Legal/Finance decision per service for internal reporting. [04, 03, 06]

<sub>reference LD-A0012</sub>

### Business date (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Distinguish trade, clearing, value and accounting dates and calendars/time zones.

**Proposed answer:** Every date has a role (trade date, CCP clearing date, value date, booking date, reporting date, contract key dates such as last trading day or expiry, as-of date), resolved against a named Business Calendar and time zone; event timestamps are kept in UTC. Evening or overnight sessions can carry the next day's trade date (venue-specific). Extended trading hours move cut-offs, margin runs and give-up deadlines relative to the clearing date. Confidence high. Firm decision: default role and time zone of "today". [01, 02, 07]

<sub>reference LD-A0015</sub>

### Current, as of, as known at (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Valid time versus record time: valid now and latest non-superseded record, valid at a business date, or known by a timestamp.

**Proposed answer:** Current = valid now and latest non-superseded record from the stated source; as of D = valid at business date D; as known at T = only records received by T. Every answer states source, as-of date and freshness. See the time convention in [modelling guidelines](../../docs/modelling-guidelines.md). Confidence high. Firm decision: whether "as known at" history is needed. [07, 08, 06]

<sub>reference LD-A0023</sub>

### Break, exception, reject, fail (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Reconciliation break versus operational exception versus rejected or lapsed give-up versus settlement fail versus technology incident.

**Proposed answer:** A reconciliation break says records disagree, not which is wrong; an Operational Exception is a situation requiring handling and may be raised by a break; a rejected or lapsed give-up is an event and may raise an exception but is not a trade failure; a settlement fail concerns Settlement Obligation; a technology incident is out of scope. Confidence high. [07, 02]
