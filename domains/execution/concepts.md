### Order

*A request to buy or sell a quantity of a listed contract, under conditions such as a price limit or how long it stays valid.*

**Definition.** Instruction from one party to another, or to a trading venue, to buy or sell a specified quantity of an instrument under stated execution conditions (such as order type, price limit, time in force and handling instructions). An order given by a client to its broker and each order the broker derives from it to work it are separate orders, linked parent to child.

**Example.** Client A asks Executing Broker X to buy 300 lots of an index future on Venue V at no more than a stated price, valid for the day.

**Not to be confused with.** Execution: the order is the request; the execution is the fill that answers it, and one order can have many fills or none.

**Breaks down into:** [Client Order](#client-order) (kind of), [Child Order](#child-order) (kind of), [Pre-trade Control Check](#pre-trade-control-check) (step in)

**Note.** Distinguish client-facing and venue-facing orders; care or algorithmic handling turns one client order into several venue orders. Alignment to verify: FIX order messages and status vocabulary. [01]

**Connected to:**

- [Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client) *submits* Order — Distinguish contractual client, representative and technical sender.
- *fulfilled by* [Execution](#execution) — An order can have zero or multiple fills, directly or through derived child orders; cancellations and partial execution matter.
- *has handling channel* [Order Handling Channel](#order-handling-channel) — Time-stamped; may change (a care order passed to an algorithm); child orders inherit it unless re-routed.
- [Authorised Representative](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#authorised-representative) *sends* Order

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Fill” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Client Order

*The order as the client gave it to the broker, before the broker splits or routes it.*

**Definition.** Order given by a client, or by a party authorised to act for it, to its executing broker, carrying the client's instructions on quantity, price limit, validity and handling; it is the order against which the broker reports client executions and is accountable to the client.

**Example.** Investment Manager M sends Executing Broker X an order to sell 500 lots of a bond future, to be worked through the day.

**Not to be confused with.** Child Order: the client order is what the client asked for; child orders are the pieces the broker sends to the market to work it.

**Kind of:** [Order](#order)

**Words people use:** “parent order” (desk and algorithm usage); “client-facing order” (execution)

**Before using it, know:** Who gave the order (client or authorised representative) and to which executing broker; the handling channel and its time; under direct market access the client order and the venue order may be the same order.

**Connected to:**

- *is given by* [Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client)
- *is received by* [Executing Broker](#executing-broker)
- *has at each point in time a* [Order Handling Channel](#order-handling-channel)
- *is worked through* [Child Order](#child-order)
- *is filled by* [Client Execution](#client-execution)

### Child Order

*A smaller order the broker cuts from the client's order and sends to the market.*

**Definition.** Order derived by a broker, a trader or an algorithm from a parent order to work part of its quantity, on a venue or through another broker; a child order sent to a venue is entered by a trading venue member and carries that member's identity. It keeps a link to its parent and inherits the parent's handling channel unless re-routed.

**Example.** The broker algorithm working Client A's 300-lot order sends three child orders of 100 lots to Venue V over an hour.

**Not to be confused with.** Allocation Line: a child order splits the order before execution to work it in the market; an allocation line splits the executed quantity across accounts afterwards.

**Kind of:** [Order](#order)

**Words people use:** “slice” (algorithm usage); “venue order, market order” (when sent to a venue; 'market order' is ambiguous with the order type); “street-side order” (US usage)

**Before using it, know:** The parent order and the handling party; whether it went to a venue or to another broker; channel changes over time.

**Connected to:**

- *is derived from* [Client Order](#client-order)
- *is entered on the venue by* [Trading Venue Member](#trading-venue-member)
- *is submitted to* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *is filled by* [Venue Execution](#venue-execution)
- *may be routed through* [Third-party Executing Member](#third-party-executing-member)

### Pre-trade Control Check

*An automatic check that stops an order going to the market if it breaks a limit.*

**Definition.** Check applied to an order before it reaches the venue, or by the venue on entry, against limits set by the executing broker, the venue member, the clearing member or the venue (such as price collars, maximum order size, credit or position limits), with the outcome pass, reject or hold.

**Example.** Client A's sponsored-access order for 5,000 lots is rejected because it exceeds the maximum order size set by Member W.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Limit: the same limit set by a clearing member can be applied here before execution and again at take-up or submission acceptance after it; the check is the act of applying a limit to one order.

**Step in:** [Order](#order)

**Values (draft list):** pass; reject; hold for manual review

**Words people use:** “clearing member pre-execution credit control” (contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Who set the limit (executing broker, venue member, clearing member, venue), the limit kind and the regime that requires it.

**Connected to:**

- *may apply* [Clearing Limit](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-limit)
- *requires for direct market access or sponsored access orders a live* [Direct Electronic Access Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#direct-electronic-access-arrangement)

### Order Handling Channel

*How one order was worked and reached the venue: by a person at the broker, by the broker's algorithm, by the client through the broker's systems, or by the client directly under the broker's membership.*

**Definition.** Governed classification of how a given order is worked by the executing broker and reaches the venue: care (worked by a person at the broker), broker algorithm, direct market access (orders generated by the client and routed through the broker's order infrastructure and controls) or sponsored access (orders sent by the client directly to the venue under the venue member's identity). It describes one order at a point in time and can change while the order is worked; it is not the client's permission to use direct electronic access, which belongs to the market access arrangement, and it is unrelated to a CCP's sponsored clearing model. Whether the client itself uses an algorithm, and how the price was formed (execution method), are separate attributes.

**Example.** Client A's order starts as a care order on the desk; two hours later the trader passes the rest to a broker algorithm, so the channel changes.

**Not to be confused with.** Market Access Arrangement: that says what the client is allowed to do (for example use direct electronic access); the channel says how this particular order was actually handled.

**Connected to:**

- [Order](#order) *has handling channel* Order Handling Channel — Time-stamped; may change (a care order passed to an algorithm); child orders inherit it unless re-routed.
- [Offering Coverage Line](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#offering-coverage-line) *is available through* Order Handling Channel
- [Direct Electronic Access Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#direct-electronic-access-arrangement) *is the standing permission behind orders on the channels direct market access and sponsored access* Order Handling Channel
- [Client Order](#client-order) *has at each point in time a* Order Handling Channel

### Execution

*The moment all or part of an order is filled at a price, by matching on the venue or by the venue registering a privately negotiated trade.*

**Definition.** Event by which all or part of an order is filled at an agreed price and time, either by matching on a trading venue or by the venue's registration of a privately negotiated trade under its rules (such as a block trade or an exchange for related position). An execution is recorded per order concerned: a match on a venue yields an execution for each participating order, and a broker's venue-facing and client-facing executions are separate events. Corrections and cancellations are later events that refer to it.

**Example.** Executing Broker X's child order for 50 lots is filled in two pieces of 20 and 30 lots on Venue V: two executions.

**Not to be confused with.** Trade: the execution is an event on one order; the trade is the resulting agreement between two counterparties, and the two need not match one to one.

**Kind of:** [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event)

**Breaks down into:** [Venue Execution](#venue-execution) (kind of), [Client Execution](#client-execution) (kind of), [Block Trade](#block-trade) (kind of), [Exchange for Related Position](#exchange-for-related-position) (kind of), [Strategy Leg Execution](#strategy-leg-execution) (kind of)

**Words people use:** “fill” (Order perspective)

**Note.** Formed by matching on a venue or by registration of a privately negotiated trade under venue rules (execution method, a taxonomy axis). A broker's venue-facing and client-facing executions are separate and need not be one-to-one. Busts and corrections are lifecycle events referencing the original. [01]

**Connected to:**

- [Order](#order) *fulfilled by* Execution — An order can have zero or multiple fills, directly or through derived child orders; cancellations and partial execution matter.
- *contributes to* [Trade](#trade) — Do not assume a universal one-to-one fill/trade representation.
- *occurs on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue) — When applicable; qualify by execution method: for privately negotiated trades (block, exchange for related position) the venue registers rather than matches; distinguish execution from reporting.
- [Listed Strategy](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#listed-strategy) *is executed as one execution per leg* Execution
- [Execution Correction](#execution-correction) *refers to the original* Execution
- [Activity Volume](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#activity-volume) *counts* Execution
- [Regulatory Reporting Role](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-reporting-role) *is held on* Execution

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Trade, transaction and execution” (open, answer proposed); “Fill” (open, answer proposed); “Volume” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Venue Execution

*A fill as the venue records it, against the order the member entered there.*

**Definition.** Execution of an order entered on a trading venue by a venue member, formed by matching or by registration of a negotiated trade, as recorded by the venue with its own identifier and time.

**Example.** Venue V reports that Member W's child order bought 20 lots at one price and 30 lots at another: two venue executions.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Execution: the venue execution is the market-side fill; the client execution is what the broker reports to the client, possibly combining several venue fills at an average price.

**Kind of:** [Execution](#execution)

**Words people use:** “fill, market fill, street fill” (venue-facing usage); “exchange trade” (ambiguous)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, entering member, venue identifiers (unique only per venue and session), execution timestamp and the trade date assigned by the venue calendar.

**Connected to:**

- *fills* [Child Order](#child-order)
- *occurs on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *gives rise to* [Executed Trade](#executed-trade)
- *can give rise to* [Exchange Fee Charge](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#exchange-fee-charge)
- [Client Execution](#client-execution) *reflects one or more* Venue Execution
- [Strategy Leg Execution](#strategy-leg-execution) *is produced with* Venue Execution

### Client Execution

*A fill as the broker reports it to the client.*

**Definition.** Execution reported by the executing broker to the client against the client order, for a quantity and price that reflect one or more venue executions, at their own prices or at an average price; it is the execution on which client execution fees and client confirmations are based.

**Example.** Executing Broker X reports to Client A one fill of 300 lots at the average price of seven venue fills.

**Not to be confused with.** Venue Execution: the client execution faces the client and can combine several venue fills; Allocation Line: the client execution is not yet split across accounts.

**Kind of:** [Execution](#execution)

**Words people use:** “fill, client fill” (client-facing usage); “execution report” (message, not the event)

**Before using it, know:** Client order; underlying venue executions; the order handling channel of the client order at the time of each underlying venue execution; the broker's trading capacity.

**Connected to:**

- *fills* [Client Order](#client-order)
- *reflects one or more* [Venue Execution](#venue-execution)
- *may be priced through* [Average Price Group](#average-price-group)
- *is the basis of* [Client Execution Fee](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#client-execution-fee)
- *is split by* [Allocation](#allocation)
- [Trade Confirmation](#trade-confirmation) *confirms* Client Execution — One execution, or the allocations made from it.

### Block Trade

*A large trade agreed privately between two parties and then registered on the venue under special rules.*

**Definition.** Execution of a privately negotiated trade at or above a venue's minimum size for the contract, agreed away from the order book and registered on the venue under its block trade rules (price conditions, reporting window, eligible participants); it is then cleared like any other listed trade.

**Example.** Executing Broker X arranges a 1,000-lot block in an index future between Client A and Client B and registers it on Venue V within the reporting window.

**Not to be confused with.** Large-in-scale treatment, which is a transparency waiver or deferral under trading regulation, and a bunched order, which is an order placed for several accounts for later allocation.

**Kind of:** [Execution](#execution)

**Words people use:** “block” (venue usage); “negotiated large trade” (any); “off-book trade” (EU venue usage, ambiguous)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, contract and the venue's block rules in force at the trade date; trading capacity of the arranging broker.

**Connected to:**

- *is registered on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *has minimum size set per* [Contract Specification](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#contract-specification)
- *is governed by* [Rule Provision](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-provision)
- *can be given up like any other execution* [Give-up / Take-up Process](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up--take-up-process)

### Exchange for Related Position

*A swap of a futures position against a matching position in the underlying or in an OTC derivative, agreed privately and registered on the venue.*

**Definition.** Execution of a privately negotiated exchange of a listed futures or options position for an opposite related position in another instrument (cash, OTC or physical), registered on the venue under its rules; the related position is a separate trade or holding outside the venue that must exist and be documented. Eligibility, minimum size and reporting window are set by the venue's rules for the contract.

**Example.** Client A sells 200 lots of a bond future to Executing Broker X and at the same time buys the matching bonds from X; the futures leg is registered on Venue V as an exchange for physical.

**Not to be confused with.** Block Trade: a block has no related position and needs a minimum size; an exchange for related position needs a genuine related position, usually without a minimum size.

**Kind of:** [Execution](#execution)

**Values (draft list):** exchange for physical; exchange for swap; exchange of options for options; other venue-defined related-position types

**Words people use:** “EFRP” (any); “EFP, EFS, EOO” (by related-position type); “basis trade” (desk usage, ambiguous)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, contract, related-position type and the venue's rules in force at the trade date; who holds the related leg.

**Connected to:**

- *is registered on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *is governed by* [Rule Provision](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-provision)
- *is linked to the trade in the related position which is not a listed trade* [Trade](#trade)

### Strategy Leg Execution

*The fill in one leg contract that results when a spread or option combination is filled.*

**Definition.** Execution of one leg of a listed strategy, produced by the venue when a strategy order is filled, in the leg's own instrument with a leg price assigned under the venue's rules; allocation, give-up and clearing usually operate on leg executions, not on the strategy.

**Example.** Client A's calendar spread fills for 100 lots on Venue V; the venue produces a buy of 100 March futures and a sale of 100 June futures, each with its own price.

**Not to be confused with.** Venue Execution of a single instrument: a leg execution exists only because a strategy was filled, and its price is assigned by venue rules rather than matched alone.

**Kind of:** [Execution](#execution)

**Words people use:** “leg fill” (desk usage)

**Before using it, know:** The strategy and its legs as defined by the venue; whether the firm allocates per leg or per strategy.

**Connected to:**

- *is produced with* [Venue Execution](#venue-execution)
- *is in the leg* [Instrument](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#instrument)
- *is usually allocated per leg by* [Allocation](#allocation)

### Trade

*The agreed deal between two counterparties on a derivative contract, with its terms; later steps such as clearing create new linked trades rather than changing it.*

**Definition.** Agreement between one pair of counterparties on a derivative contract with stated economic terms (instrument or bespoke terms, direction, quantity, price and trade date), resulting from one or more executions or from a later deriving event such as CCP interposition, allocation or transfer. A trade is distinct from the records that represent it; clearing, compression and porting create successor trades linked to it rather than changing it. Which clearing tier is meant (pre-clearing, CCP-facing or member-client) must be stated.

**Example.** After Client A's fills, the executed trade between Executing Broker X and the selling member exists; at CCP Z it is replaced by trades facing the CCP.

**Not to be confused with.** Execution: a trade is the agreement, an execution is the fill event; and a trade record in a system is only a representation of the trade.

**Breaks down into:** [Executed Trade](#executed-trade) (kind of), [CCP-facing Trade](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#ccp-facing-trade) (kind of), [Client Back-to-Back Trade](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#client-back-to-back-trade) (kind of)

**Words people use:** “alpha trade” (OTC clearing)

**Note.** One pair of counterparties per trade; clearing, compression and porting create successor trades linked by a “succeeds” link, never mutating the predecessor. How many trades one execution yields depends on the clearing legal model, allocation and averaging: record lineage, not counts. A bespoke OTC trade carries its own economic terms. UTIs are per regime. [01, 03, 06]

**Before using it, know:** Clearing tier and counterparty view (the open question “The trade (clearing tier)”)

**Connected to:**

- [Execution](#execution) *contributes to* Trade — Do not assume a universal one-to-one fill/trade representation.
- [Give-up / Take-up Process](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up--take-up-process) *concerns* Trade — Carry quantity, counterparties and lifecycle history.
- [Clearing Acceptance](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-acceptance) *concerns* Trade — Specify service, acceptance criteria and time.
- [Novation](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#novation) *transforms* Trade — Preserve predecessor and successor obligations/trades with directional lineage.
- [Clearing Member Acceptance](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-member-acceptance) *concerns* Trade — Or an allocated quantity of it; designation of a member is not its acceptance.
- [CCP Interposition](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#ccp-interposition) *concerns* Trade — Interposition creates the CCP-facing trades; under novation the predecessor is extinguished.
- *succeeds* [Trade](#trade) — Generic directional lineage qualified by the deriving event (interposition, allocation, compression, transfer, porting, bilateral novation); a “transforms” link remains the novation-specific case. No cardinality asserted.
- [Exchange for Related Position](#exchange-for-related-position) *is linked to the trade in the related position which is not a listed trade* Trade
- [Give-up Line](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up-line) *concerns a quantity of* Trade
- [Clearing Submission](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-submission) *concerns* Trade
- [Clearing Obligation](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#clearing-obligation) *applies to* Trade
- [Source Record](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#source-record) *represents for example* Trade
- [Trade Amendment](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#trade-amendment) *changes or ends* Trade
- [Option Premium](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#option-premium) *is owed on* Trade — The option trade.

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Trade, transaction and execution” (open, answer proposed); “Product, instrument and OTC contract” (open, answer proposed); “The trade (clearing tier)” (open, answer proposed); “OTC and listed” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Executed Trade

*The trade as it exists right after execution, before the CCP steps in.*

**Definition.** Trade at the pre-clearing tier: the trade as agreed at execution between the two parties on the venue or, for the client-facing side, between the executing broker and the client where the broker acts as principal, before or at the moment of CCP interposition. Where the clearing service's rules provide for open offer, it exists only as the record of what was matched, and the CCP-facing trades arise at matching. It is succeeded by the CCP-facing trades and, under the principal model, the client back-to-back trades.

**Example.** Client A's buy order is matched on Venue V against Member Q; at a CCP that novates at registration, the executed trade between Executing Broker X and Member Q exists until CCP Z registers it.

**Not to be confused with.** CCP-facing trade: after interposition each side faces the CCP in a new trade; the executed trade is the one before.

**Kind of:** [Trade](#trade)

**Words people use:** “pre-clearing trade” (any); “alpha trade” (OTC clearing usage); “matched trade” (venue usage)

**Before using it, know:** Counterparty view (member side or client side) and the trading capacity of the broker; on listed markets the pre-clearing tier is often very short-lived.

**Connected to:**

- *is formed by* [Venue Execution](#venue-execution)
- *is succeeded by* [CCP-facing Trade](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#ccp-facing-trade)
- *is succeeded by under the principal model* [Client Back-to-Back Trade](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#client-back-to-back-trade)
- *may be given up through* [Give-up / Take-up Process](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up--take-up-process)

### Transaction

*An ambiguous word that is not used as a concept for now: ask whether the speaker means an execution, a trade, a lifecycle event, a cash movement or a regulatory transaction report.*

**Definition.** Business operation affecting rights, obligations or balances; this broad working definition requires validation and must not become a catch-all synonym.

**Example.** "How many transactions did Client A do yesterday?" must be answered by asking: fills, trades, allocations or reported transactions?

**Not to be confused with.** Trade: a trade is a defined concept; 'transaction' is only a word that may point to it or to several others.

**Caution.** Never resolve "transaction" to this ID alone; return candidates Execution, Trade, Lifecycle Event, Cash Movement and the MiFIR reporting "transaction", and ask.

### Average Price Group

*A set of fills combined so that the client gets one average price, calculated either by a venue or CCP facility or by the broker.*

**Definition.** Set of executions, normally in one instrument and on one side for one client or order, that are combined so that the resulting trades or client-facing executions carry a single average price, under an averaging method applied either by a venue or CCP average-price facility or by the broker. Which executions are eligible, how the average is rounded and how any residual is handled follow the facility's rules or the client agreement.

**Example.** Client A's 300 lots were filled at seven different prices; Client A asked for an average price, so the seven fills form one average price group.

**Not to be confused with.** Allocation: averaging sets one price for many fills; allocation splits the quantity across accounts.

**Breaks down into:** [Average Price Residual](#average-price-residual) (part of)

**Note.** Qualifier "averaging performed by": venue or CCP facility, or broker; both are in scope (sponsor decision 2026-11-01). Rounding residuals: proposed as separately identifiable amounts, pending Finance and Operations (open question). [01]

**Connected to:**

- *groups* [Execution](#execution), [Trade](#trade) — Eligibility, weighting, rounding and resulting records depend on context.
- [Client Execution](#client-execution) *may be priced through* Average Price Group

### Average Price Residual

*The small leftover amount created when an average price is rounded.*

**Definition.** Amount left over when an average price is rounded for booking, so that the value booked at the rounded average differs from the value of the underlying executions, attributable to an identified average price group; its owner and treatment follow the averaging rules or the client agreement and remain an open question for Finance and Operations.

**Example.** Client A's seven fills average to a price that has to be rounded to the price increment; the leftover is recorded against that average price group.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Charge: a residual is not a fee for a service; it arises from rounding a price.

**Part of:** [Average Price Group](#average-price-group)

**Words people use:** “rounding residual” (any); “average price cash adjustment” (some venues, to verify)

**Before using it, know:** Average price group, averaging performer, currency and who bears it; no treatment is approved.

**Connected to:**

- *may give rise to a settlement obligation settled by a cash movement* [Settlement Obligation](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#settlement-obligation)

### Allocation Instruction

*The client's instruction saying which accounts, and which clearing brokers, should receive which part of the executed quantity.*

**Definition.** Instruction, given by a client or by a party authorised to act for it (such as its investment manager), specifying how an executed or average-priced quantity is split across target accounts and, where relevant, the receiving clearing brokers. It may be standing (agreed in advance for a defined scope of activity), given with the order before execution, or given after execution; it is versioned and can be amended until the applicable cut-off. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Investment Manager M tells Executing Broker X to put 100 lots in Fund F1's account and 200 lots in Fund F2's account cleared by Clearing Broker Y.

**Not to be confused with.** Allocation: the instruction is the request; the allocation is the split actually made.

**Breaks down into:** [Standing Allocation Instruction](#standing-allocation-instruction) (kind of)

**Connected to:**

- *instructs* [Allocation](#allocation) — Versions are kept; the deadline is a Cut-off.
- *references* [Execution](#execution), [Average Price Group](#average-price-group) — The executions or average-price group whose quantity is split.
- [Authorised Representative](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#authorised-representative) *gives* Allocation Instruction
- [Investment Manager](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#investment-manager) *gives* Allocation Instruction
- [Give-up Line](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up-line) *carries out part of* Allocation Instruction
- [Firm Cut-off](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#firm-cut-off) *may apply to* Allocation Instruction

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Successful give-up” (decided 2026-11-01 (sponsor; see decision log)) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

<sub>Instruction · level 1 · Draft · reference LD-C0060 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Standing Allocation Instruction

*A split rule the client sets once, which the broker applies to every matching trade.*

**Definition.** Allocation instruction agreed in advance with a client for a defined scope of activity (such as contract, account or strategy), with an effective period, applied to executions in that scope unless replaced by a specific instruction. Its place as a kind of Allocation Instruction depends on the sponsor accepting the widened Allocation Instruction definition.

**Example.** Fund F tells Executing Broker X that all its bond futures trades go to its account at Clearing Broker Y until further notice.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Designation on an order: a standing instruction covers many orders over a period; a designation is given on one order.

**Kind of:** [Allocation Instruction](#allocation-instruction)

**Words people use:** “standing instruction, SSI-style allocation” (desk usage, ambiguous with settlement instructions); “default allocation” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Scope, effective period and who may override it; the version in force at the execution time.

**Connected to:**

- *is often agreed alongside* [Give-up Agreement](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up-agreement)
- *drives* [Allocation](#allocation)

<sub>Instruction · level 2 · Draft · reference LD-C0128 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Allocation

*The actual split of executed quantity across the target accounts, one line per account.*

**Definition.** Process by which an executed or average-priced quantity is assigned to one or more target accounts, in allocation lines, following an allocation instruction or a standing rule. An allocation to an account carried by a clearing member other than the executing side's clearing member (whether the trade is registered first to that member or designated to the receiving member in advance) starts a give-up; otherwise it is internal. Allocation distributes quantity; averaging, which may come before it, sets the price. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Executing Broker X books 100 lots to Fund F1's account cleared in-house and 200 lots to Fund F2's account at Clearing Broker Y, which starts a give-up.

**Not to be confused with.** Give-up / Take-up Process: only an allocation to an account at another clearing member starts a give-up; an allocation within the same clearing member is internal.

**Breaks down into:** [Allocation Line](#allocation-line) (part of)

**Note.** Allocation to an account carried by another clearing member initiates a give-up; otherwise it is internal. Averaging groups and allocation distributes; they are chained but distinct. [01]

**Connected to:**

- *allocates* [Execution](#execution), [Trade](#trade) — State allocated quantity, source object and process scope.
- *targets* [Account](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#account) — Target account does not by itself establish legal ownership.
- [Allocation Instruction](#allocation-instruction) *instructs* Allocation — Versions are kept; the deadline is a Cut-off.
- *may initiate* [Give-up / Take-up Process](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up--take-up-process) — Only when the target account is carried by a clearing member other than the one to which the execution is registered; otherwise the allocation is internal.
- [Client Account](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client-account) *is targeted by* Allocation
- [Client Execution](#client-execution) *is split by* Allocation
- [Strategy Leg Execution](#strategy-leg-execution) *is usually allocated per leg by* Allocation
- [Standing Allocation Instruction](#standing-allocation-instruction) *drives* Allocation

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Fill” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

<sub>Process · level 1 · Draft · reference LD-C0020 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Allocation Line

*One row of a split: so many lots, at this price, to this account.*

**Definition.** One part of an allocation that assigns a stated quantity, at a stated price, from an identified execution or average price group to one target account, with its route (internal, or give-up to a named clearing member). An allocation line routed to another clearing member gives rise to exactly one give-up line, whose status is tracked on the give-up page. The allocation line's own status covers allocation states only. When the target account is carried by another clearing member, it is designated by an Account Identifier (give-up reference).

**Example.** Executing Broker X's allocation of Client A's 300 lots has two lines: 100 lots to Fund F1 internally and 200 lots to Fund F2 at Clearing Broker Y.

**Not to be confused with.** Fill: an allocation line splits quantity already filled; it is never a fill.

**Part of:** [Allocation](#allocation)

**Values (draft list):** route: internal; give-up; allocation status: pending; booked; amended; cancelled

**Words people use:** “allocation, alloc” (desk usage); “booking line” (middle office usage)

**Before using it, know:** Source execution or average price group, target account and its clearing member, route and status, and the version of the instruction applied.

**Connected to:**

- *targets* [Account](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#account)
- *gives rise to when routed to another clearing member* [Give-up Line](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up-line)
- *must be completed by* [Cut-off](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#cut-off)

<sub>Entity · level 2 · Draft · reference LD-C0129 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Executing Broker

*The firm that provides execution to the client and answers to the client for it, whether or not it is itself a member of the venue.*

**Definition.** Role of a party that provides an execution service to a client for identified orders and is responsible to that client for how they are executed, whether it enters the orders on the venue itself as a trading venue member or through another member. It is not necessarily the venue member, the executing entity named in a regulatory transaction report, or the clearing member to which the trade is first registered.

**Example.** Executing Broker X takes Client A's order and has it executed on Venue V through Member W, because X is not a member of V.

**Not to be confused with.** Trading Venue Member: the member is the firm admitted to enter the order on the venue; it may be a different firm from the executing broker.

**Kind of:** [Broker](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#broker)

**Words people use:** “executing firm” (Execution)

**Note.** The client-facing service role; not necessarily the Trading Venue Member, the regulatory executing entity or the giving clearing member. Third-party execution through another venue member is in scope (sponsor decision 2026-11-01); memberships are never inferred. [01]

**Connected to:**

- [Third-Party Execution Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#third-party-execution-arrangement) *lets the firm act as the client s executing broker while another party is the venue member so it never implies* Executing Broker
- [Client Order](#client-order) *is received by* Executing Broker
- [Third-party Executing Member](#third-party-executing-member) *acts for* Executing Broker
- [Giving Clearing Member](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#giving-clearing-member) *clears for* Executing Broker

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Executing broker, firm, entity” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Trading Venue Member

*A firm admitted under a venue's rules to enter orders and register trades there in its own name.*

**Definition.** Role of a party admitted under a trading venue's rules, as member or participant, to enter orders and register trades in its own name; distinct from clearing membership and from the client-facing Executing Broker role.

**Example.** Member W is admitted to Venue V as a trading-only member: it enters orders but clears through another firm.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Member: being admitted to trade on a venue does not mean being admitted to clear at its CCP.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Breaks down into:** [Third-party Executing Member](#third-party-executing-member) (kind of)

**Words people use:** “non-clearing member” (Listed markets); “executing firm” (Execution)

**Connected to:**

- *admitted to* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue) — Qualify by membership category and effective period; venue membership does not imply clearing membership.
- [Non-Clearing Member](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#non-clearing-member) *is held together with* Trading Venue Member
- [Direct Electronic Access Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#direct-electronic-access-arrangement) *is granted by the client s* Trading Venue Member
- [Third-Party Execution Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#third-party-execution-arrangement) *is intermediated by the venue member role of* Trading Venue Member
- [Child Order](#child-order) *is entered on the venue by* Trading Venue Member
- [Fee Incentive Programme](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#fee-incentive-programme) *is joined by* Trading Venue Member

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Executing broker, firm, entity” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Third-party Executing Member

*A venue member that another broker uses to reach a venue where it is not a member.*

**Definition.** Role of a trading venue member that enters orders on the venue for an executing broker that is not a member of that venue, or that is a member but chooses not to use its own membership for that business, under an agreement between them and a Third-party Execution Arrangement; the executing broker remains responsible to its client and owes the member brokerage (brokerage paid, distinct from executing brokerage received).

**Example.** Executing Broker X is not a member of Venue T, so it routes Client A's child orders to Member W, which enters them on Venue T.

**Not to be confused with.** Executing Broker: the executing broker serves the client; the third-party executing member serves the executing broker on the venue.

**Kind of:** [Trading Venue Member](#trading-venue-member)

**Words people use:** “third-party broker, executing agent” (desk usage); “sub-broker” (any, ambiguous)

**Before using it, know:** Venue, membership category and effective period; never inferred from execution activity.

**Connected to:**

- [Child Order](#child-order) *may be routed through* Third-party Executing Member
- *acts for* [Executing Broker](#executing-broker)
- *is admitted to* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *is the intermediary of* [Third-Party Execution Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#third-party-execution-arrangement)
- *is owed* [Executing Broker Brokerage Charge](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#executing-broker-brokerage-charge)

<sub>Role · level 2 · Draft · reference LD-C0130 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Execution Correction

*A later fix or cancellation of a fill, recorded as a new event that points back to the original.*

**Definition.** Event by which a venue, under its error-trade rules, or the broker toward its client, under the client agreement, cancels or corrects a previously recorded execution (price, quantity, account or other detail), at any time, referring to the original execution, which is kept. If the trade is already registered at a CCP, the CCP records the resulting change to the registered trade, which may lead to a Trade Amendment or raise an Operational Exception.

**Example.** Venue V cancels a trade executed outside its price limits ten minutes after the match; Executing Broker X then corrects Client A's fill.

**Not to be confused with.** Trade Amendment: a change made to a registered trade under the clearing service's rules or by agreement of the parties; an execution correction comes from the venue's error-trade rules or the broker's client-facing correction, even after registration.

**Kind of:** [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event)

**Values (draft list):** cancellation (bust); price adjustment; quantity correction; correction of non-economic details

**Words people use:** “trade bust, busted trade” (venue usage); “error trade” (venue usage); “cancel and correct” (desk usage)

**Before using it, know:** Who initiated it (venue, member, broker), on which side (venue-facing or client-facing), and its effect on allocations and give-ups already made.

**Connected to:**

- *refers to the original* [Execution](#execution)
- *is governed by the venue s error trade rules in* [Rule Provision](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#rule-provision)
- *may raise* [Operational Exception](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#operational-exception)

<sub>Event · level 2 · Draft · reference LD-C0125 · approved by the Listed Execution product owner</sub>

### Trade Confirmation

*The message that confirms to a client what was executed for it, at what price and cost.*

**Definition.** Notice sent by an executing broker, or by a clearing broker for trades it clears, to a client confirming the details of one client execution, or of the allocations made from it: contract, side, quantity, price or average price, trade date, account, venue where required, and the fees and commissions charged, within the time and with the content that the applicable rules and the client agreement require (to verify per regime). It confirms what has been executed; it does not register the trade for clearing. Confirmation between the parties of a cleared OTC trade is outside this concept.

**Example.** After Client A's order is filled on Exchange X, Broker B sends it a confirmation with quantity, average price and commission.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Statement: the confirmation covers one execution or allocation; the statement covers the account for a day or a month.

**Values (draft list):** per execution; per allocation; end-of-day summary

**Words people use:** “confirmation, confirm” (broker to client, listed)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, client, execution or allocation confirmed, regime.

**Connected to:**

- *confirms* [Client Execution](#client-execution) — One execution, or the allocations made from it.
- *is issued by* [Broker](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#broker)
