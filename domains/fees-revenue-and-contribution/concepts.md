### Fee Charge

*An amount one party owes another for a service, such as an exchange fee or a client clearing fee.*

**Definition.** Obligation of exactly one debtor toward exactly one creditor to pay an amount for a service, arising from a chargeable event or a period under an applicable fee schedule or agreed terms; it carries its amount certainty (estimated or final) and billing stage (accrued, invoiced, disputed, adjusted, settled, written off), and may be paid by a party other than the debtor or collected by a party other than the creditor. Includes late and penalty charges arising from a missed cut-off or a settlement fail. Distinct from the revenue it may contribute to and from the cash movement that settles it. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Client A owes Clearing Broker Y a clearing fee for each lot registered at CCP Z today.

**Not to be confused with.** Revenue: a charge is what is owed on one event; revenue is a management total built from many charges under a stated basis.

**Breaks down into:** [Client Execution Fee](#client-execution-fee) (kind of), [CCP Fee Charge](#ccp-fee-charge) (kind of), [Pass-through Charge](#pass-through-charge) (kind of), [Bundled Execution and Clearing Fee](#bundled-execution-and-clearing-fee) (kind of), [Regulatory Transaction Fee](#regulatory-transaction-fee) (kind of), [Fee Rebate](#fee-rebate) (kind of), [Fee Adjustment](#fee-adjustment) (step in), [Fee Invoice](#fee-invoice) (step in)

**Note.** Facets: amount certainty (estimated, final) and billing stage (accrued, invoiced, disputed, adjusted, settled, written off). Proposed definitional cardinality: exactly one debtor and one creditor. [05, 08]

**Connected to:**

- *has debtor* [Party](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party) — Debtor may differ from actual payer.
- *has creditor* [Party](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party) — Creditor may differ from collector or ultimate beneficiary.
- *calculated under* [Fee Schedule](#fee-schedule) — Use the applicable tariff version and conditions; calculation is not recognition.
- *arises from* [Execution](../execution/concepts.md#execution), [Trade](../execution/concepts.md#trade), [Clearing Acceptance](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-acceptance), [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event) — The chargeable event; periodic charges arise from a period rather than an event.
- *charged for* [Service Offering](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-offering) — The service concerned; service nature is a mandatory facet.
- *recharges* [Fee Charge](#fee-charge) — A charge passed on to another debtor, for example executing brokerage paid by the clearing broker and recharged to the client; zero or more.

### Client Execution Fee

*What a client pays its broker for executing its orders.*

**Definition.** Fee charge owed by a client to the provider of an execution service for executing its orders, stated on a charge basis (per contract side, round turn, ticket, allocation or notional) and under a pricing form (all-in, including venue charges, or fees-plus, with venue charges passed on separately). When the client's trades are given up to another clearing broker, the same charge is also executing brokerage. Distinct from the venue's own fee.

**Example.** Client A pays Executing Broker X a fee per contract side for orders worked by the care desk.

**Not to be confused with.** Exchange Fee Charge: the venue's own fee, which the broker owes the venue and may pass on.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Words people use:** “execution fee” (Any)

**Note.** State the charge basis (per contract side, round turn, allocation, ticket, notional) and grain. An all-in rate must be decomposed before comparison with [Exchange Fee Charge](#exchange-fee-charge) or [CCP Fee Charge](#ccp-fee-charge). [01]

**Connected to:**

- [Client Execution](../execution/concepts.md#client-execution) *is the basis of* Client Execution Fee

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Execution fee” (open, answer proposed); “Commission” (open, deferred by sponsor, answer proposed); “Fee Charge and Commission types” (open, answer proposed); “Volume” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### CCP Fee Charge

*A fee charged by a clearing house to its clearing members for clearing and related services.*

**Definition.** Fee charge whose creditor is a central counterparty, for a service of one of its clearing services (clearing or registration of trades, OTC maintenance, exercise, assignment or delivery, account and position services, holding of non-cash collateral, membership), owed by a clearing member or other participant under the CCP's fee schedule. Distinct from margin, default fund contributions, interest on cash collateral and any client charge passing it on.

**Example.** CCP Z charges Clearing Broker Y a registration fee per OTC trade and a fee for holding bonds as collateral.

**Not to be confused with.** Margin or default fund contribution: these are collateral the member provides, not fees.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Note.** Distinct from default-fund contributions and from interest on cash collateral; charges for holding non-cash collateral are CCP fee charges with service nature "collateral". [04, 05]

**Connected to:**

- [Pass-through Charge](#pass-through-charge) *may pass on* CCP Fee Charge

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Exchange fees” (open, answer proposed); “Fee Charge and Commission types” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Pass-through Charge

*A third-party fee that the broker recharges to its client.*

**Definition.** Fee charge owed by a client to its broker that passes on, at cost or with a stated mark-up, an identified charge the broker owes a third party (a venue, a CCP, a regulator or self-regulatory body, another executing broker or a third-party clearing member) for the client's activity; it keeps a link to the charge it passes on.

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y owes CCP Z a fee for Client A's lots and recharges it to Client A at cost on the monthly statement.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Clearing Fee: the broker's own fee for its service, as opposed to a third party's fee passed on.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Values (draft list):** recharge treatment: at cost; with mark-up

**Words people use:** “recharge” (any); “exchange and clearing fees” (client statement usage, contextual); “give-up payment” (contextual, executing brokerage paid on the client's behalf)

**Before using it, know:** The charge passed on, its creditor, the recharge treatment, and the gross or net rule of the reporting basis.

**Connected to:**

- *may pass on* [Exchange Fee Charge](#exchange-fee-charge)
- *may pass on* [CCP Fee Charge](#ccp-fee-charge)
- *may pass on* [Executing Broker Brokerage Charge](#executing-broker-brokerage-charge)
- *may pass on* [Regulatory Transaction Fee](#regulatory-transaction-fee)
- *is shown gross or net according to* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis)

### Bundled Execution and Clearing Fee

*One combined price for executing and clearing the same trades.*

**Definition.** Single fee charge owed by a client to a broker that provides both execution and clearing of the same trades under an execute-and-clear service, quoted as one rate; for revenue per service it must be split between execution and clearing by a rule stated in the management reporting basis.

**Example.** Client A pays Broker X one rate per round turn that covers both executing and clearing its futures at CCP Z.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Execution Fee and Client Clearing Fee: charged separately; a bundled fee needs a split before it feeds either revenue figure.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Words people use:** “all-in commission” (contextual); “commission” (ambiguous, blocked); “round-turn rate” (listed usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** The service offering, the split rule, the charge basis.

**Connected to:**

- *is charged for with service kind execute and clear* [Service Offering](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-offering)
- *is a candidate reading of* [Commission](#commission)
- *is split according to* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis)

### Regulatory Transaction Fee

*A per-trade fee levied by a regulator or industry body, not by the venue or the CCP.*

**Definition.** Fee charge whose creditor is a regulator or self-regulatory organisation, levied per trade, per contract or on traded value under a published rule of that body and owed by a member, broker or clearing member, often collected through a venue, CCP or clearing broker and passed on to the client. Neither a venue fee nor a CCP fee. The name is the industry's; 'transaction' here does not resolve to the blocked concept Transaction.

**Example.** In a US regime, a per-round-turn assessment levied by a self-regulatory body on futures trades, collected by the clearing broker and passed on to Client A.

**Not to be confused with.** Exchange Fee Charge: charged by the venue operator, even if collected the same way.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Words people use:** “regulatory fee” (any); “assessment fee” (US usage, to verify)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, levying body, rule and version, collector.

**Connected to:**

- [Pass-through Charge](#pass-through-charge) *may pass on* Regulatory Transaction Fee
- *is owed to including a self regulatory organisation* [Regulatory Authority](../regulation-and-rule-changes/concepts.md#regulatory-authority)

### Fee Rebate

*Money a venue, CCP or broker pays back after volume or quoting conditions are met.*

**Definition.** Amount owed back by the creditor of earlier fee charges to their debtor because the conditions of a fee incentive programme or of client pricing terms were met over a period; it refers to the charges or activity it rewards and is kept apart from the reduced rate (discount) and from a decision not to charge (waiver).

**Example.** Venue V owes Broker X a monthly rebate under its market-making programme; separately Broker X owes Client A a rebate for passing a volume tier.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Adjustment: a waiver or correction changes one charge; a rebate rewards activity over a period under a programme.

**Kind of:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Values (draft list):** direction from the firm's view: received from a venue or CCP; granted to a client

**Words people use:** “rebate” (any); “volume rebate” (any); “LP rebate” (venue usage)

**Before using it, know:** Programme or terms, period, direction (received or granted), and whether it is netted against the fees it rewards.

**Connected to:**

- *is owed under* [Fee Incentive Programme](#fee-incentive-programme)
- *is settled by* [Cash Movement](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#cash-movement)
- *is netted or shown gross according to* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis)

### Fee Adjustment

*A later change to a fee already charged, such as a correction, a waiver or a write-off.*

**Definition.** Event that changes a fee charge after it was accrued or invoiced, by correction, cancellation, waiver, credit, dispute outcome or write-off, referring to the original charge and stating its own effect, reason and approver; the original is never deleted.

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y waives Client A's give-in fees for one month after a booking error and issues a credit against the original charges.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Rebate: a rebate rewards activity under a programme, not a change to one charge.

**Step in:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Values (draft list):** kind: correction; cancellation; waiver; credit; dispute outcome; write-off

**Words people use:** “credit note” (contextual, the document); “waiver” (contextual); “fee correction” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Original charge, reason, approver, effective date, and whether the adjustment is in the same period as the original.

**Connected to:**

- *may be shown on* [Fee Invoice](#fee-invoice)
- *may resolve* [Operational Exception](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#operational-exception)

### Fee Invoice

*The bill that asks for payment of a set of fees.*

**Definition.** Demand for payment issued by a creditor (or its collector) to a debtor for a set of fee charges and adjustments over a billing period, with a total per currency and a due date; one invoice can carry charges of several service natures and of several creditors when one organisation collects for others, so its lines must be decomposed before aggregation.

**Example.** CCP Z's monthly bill to Clearing Broker Y lists CCP clearing fees and Venue V's trading fees it collects for Venue V.

**Not to be confused with.** Cash Movement: a debit to the client's cash account in the broker's books settles the obligation; it is not a kind of invoice.

**Step in:** [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)

**Values (draft list):** form: invoice; client statement (periodic statement listing charges)

**Words people use:** “bill” (any); “monthly statement” (client usage)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, debtor, billing period, currency, due date; the creditor of each line.

**Connected to:**

- [Fee Adjustment](#fee-adjustment) *may be shown on* Fee Invoice
- *may be issued by* [Fee Collector](#fee-collector)
- *gives rise to* [Settlement Obligation](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#settlement-obligation)
- *is settled by* [Cash Movement](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#cash-movement)
- *is checked by a fee reconciliation* [Reconciliation](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#reconciliation)

### Fee Schedule

*The price list and rules that say how much is charged, published by a venue or CCP or agreed with a client.*

**Definition.** Versioned set of rates, charge bases, conditions, discounts, caps, minimums and incentive programmes, issued by an identified issuer (a trading venue operator, a CCP, a regulator or self-regulatory body, or a broker in its client pricing terms) for a defined scope (service, clearing service, venue or segment, contract specification, client or client segment) and effective period, under which fee charges are determined.

**Example.** Venue V's price list for equity index futures, valid from the first of next month.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Charge: the schedule sets the rates; the charge is what is owed on a given event.

**Breaks down into:** [Published Fee Schedule](#published-fee-schedule) (kind of), [Client Pricing Terms](#client-pricing-terms) (kind of), [Fee Rate](#fee-rate) (part of), [Fee Incentive Programme](#fee-incentive-programme) (part of)

**Note.** Generic parent of public venue or CCP tariffs and negotiated client terms, distinguished by issuer and scope; versioned; a Rule Change can change it. [05]

**Connected to:**

- [Fee Charge](#fee-charge) *calculated under* Fee Schedule — Use the applicable tariff version and conditions; calculation is not recognition.
- *applies to* [Service Offering](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-offering), [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service), [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue), [Contract Specification](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#contract-specification) — Versioned; issuer and effective period required; public tariffs and negotiated client terms are distinguished by issuer and scope.
- [Trading Venue Segment](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue-segment) *can have its own fee schedule* Fee Schedule
- [Flexible Series](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#flexible-series) *may have its own fees under* Fee Schedule

### Published Fee Schedule

*The public price list of a venue or CCP, which changes by circular on a set date.*

**Definition.** Fee schedule issued by a trading venue operator, a CCP, or a regulator or self-regulatory organisation, made public to its members or participants, with versions and effective dates; it may be published with or annexed to a rulebook, but its meaning stays here. Its changes are Rule Changes announced by Change Notices, including any approval, filing or self-certification step.

**Example.** CCP Z's fee schedule for its listed derivatives clearing service, version effective from the start of next quarter.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Pricing Terms: agreed with one client and confidential, not published.

**Kind of:** [Fee Schedule](#fee-schedule)

**Values (draft list):** issuer kind: trading venue operator; CCP; regulator or self-regulatory body

**Words people use:** “price list” (venue and CCP usage); “tariff” (any); “fee grid” (some venues)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, scope (venue or segment, clearing service, contract), version and effective period; the date role used to pick the version.

### Client Pricing Terms

*The prices a broker has agreed with one client for its services.*

**Definition.** Fee schedule agreed between the broker's contracting entity and a client, possibly negotiated once for all clients of a commercial group, applying to a stated scope (clients, service offerings, service components, period); it sets the pricing form (all-in or fees-plus), rates, discounts, caps, minimums and the treatment of passed-on charges. A Commercial Group is the scope of the terms, never a party to them. A rate for the firm's own service points to the Service Component it prices.

**Example.** Client A's terms with Clearing Broker Y: a fees-plus rate per lot for listed clearing at CCP Z, with CCP fees passed on at cost.

**Not to be confused with.** Published Fee Schedule: public and the same for all who qualify, whereas client terms are negotiated.

**Kind of:** [Fee Schedule](#fee-schedule)

**Values (draft list):** pricing form: all-in; fees-plus; pass-through rule: at cost; with mark-up; not passed on

**Words people use:** “rate card” (sales usage); “fee letter” (contextual, the document); “pricing schedule” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Client or commercial group, service offering, effective period and version; pricing form.

### Fee Rate

*One line of a price list: how much is charged for one kind of activity.*

**Definition.** Single line of a fee schedule that sets an amount or percentage for a stated scope (product or contract, account type, execution method, service nature, client segment, membership status or programme), on a stated charge basis and currency, possibly tiered by activity volume, valid for the schedule's effective period.

**Example.** Venue V's rate per contract side for block trades in its index futures, for client accounts of non-members.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Charge: the rate is the price; the charge is what is owed when the activity happens.

**Part of:** [Fee Schedule](#fee-schedule)

**Values (draft list):** scope: product family, contract specification or clearing contract; trading venue or venue segment; strategy versus leg; flexible versus standard series; block or negotiated trade versus on-book; account type or segregation level; service nature (for venue, CCP and regulatory fees) or service component (for the firm's own client charges); charge basis: per contract side; per round turn; per ticket or allocation; per notional; percentage of premium; periodic

**Words people use:** “tariff line” (any); “price point” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Schedule version, scope qualifiers, charge basis, currency, tier.

### Fee Incentive Programme

*A scheme that lowers fees or pays rebates to those who meet agreed volume or quoting obligations.*

**Definition.** Set of conditions, published by a venue or CCP in its fee schedule or agreed with a client in its pricing terms, under which participants who register and meet stated obligations or volume thresholds over a period receive reduced rates or rebates; for example a liquidity provider, market-making or volume-tier programme.

**Example.** Venue V's market-making programme for new options contracts, paying a rebate to registered members who meet quoting obligations each month.

**Not to be confused with.** Fee Rebate: the programme is the rule; the rebate is the amount owed when the rule is met.

**Part of:** [Fee Schedule](#fee-schedule)

**Values (draft list):** kind: liquidity provider or market making; volume tier; new product launch; client volume tier

**Words people use:** “incentive scheme” (any); “LP programme” (venue usage); “market-making scheme” (MiFID II usage, to verify)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, participant registration, period, conditions; whether benefits are passed on to clients is a firm matter.

### Client Clearing Fee

*What a client pays its clearing broker for clearing its trades, listed or OTC.*

**Definition.** Fee charge owed by a client to its clearing broker for clearing its trades in one or more clearing services, arising from clearing events (registration of a listed lot or an OTC trade, give-in, exercise, assignment or delivery, transfer) or from a period (OTC maintenance on open trades or notional, account fees), stated on a charge basis and pricing form. Distinct from CCP fee charges passed on to the client and from income earned on the client's collateral.

**Example.** Client A pays Clearing Broker Y a fee per lot cleared at CCP Z and a monthly maintenance fee per open interest rate swap.

**Not to be confused with.** CCP Fee Charge: the CCP's own fee to its clearing member, which may be passed on.

### Executing Broker Brokerage Charge

*What an executing broker is owed for executing trades that are cleared elsewhere, often paid by the clearing broker and recharged to the client.*

**Definition.** Fee charge owed to an executing broker for executing orders, most often for executions given up to a clearing broker, whose debtor (the client, or the broker that engaged the executing broker) and actual payer (often the clearing broker, which then recharges the client) are stated. From the firm's view it is brokerage received when the firm is the executing broker and brokerage paid when the firm owes or pays another executing broker.

**Example.** Executing Broker X executes for Client A and gives the trades up to Clearing Broker Y, which pays X's brokerage and debits Client A.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Execution Fee: overlaps when the client owes its own executing broker; brokerage can also be owed by a broker that hired a third-party executing member.

**Words people use:** “execution fee” (Any); “brokerage” (UK/EU usage)

**Note.** Viewpoint matters: received as executing broker, paid and recharged as clearing broker; debtor, payer and recharge explicit. Label unchanged. [01, 05]

**Connected to:**

- [Third-Party Execution Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#third-party-execution-arrangement) *gives rise to brokerage owed to the intermediary* Executing Broker Brokerage Charge
- [Third-party Executing Member](../execution/concepts.md#third-party-executing-member) *is owed* Executing Broker Brokerage Charge
- [Pass-through Charge](#pass-through-charge) *may pass on* Executing Broker Brokerage Charge

### Exchange Fee Charge

*A fee charged by the venue operator, for trading or related services such as market data or connectivity.*

**Definition.** Fee charge whose creditor is the operator of a trading venue, for a stated service nature (trading, registration of negotiated trades, market data, connectivity, membership or other), owed by the venue member, clearing member or other debtor that the venue's fee schedule names, and possibly collected by another organisation such as the CCP. Distinct from the CCP's own fees, from regulatory transaction fees and from any charge passing it on to a client.

**Example.** Venue V charges its member a fee per contract side traded in its order book, collected through CCP Z.

**Not to be confused with.** CCP Fee Charge: charged by the clearing house, not the venue, even when both appear on one bill.

**Words people use:** “execution fee” (Any)

**Note.** Creditor is the venue operator; record the collector separately; service-nature facet mandatory. [05, 02]

**Connected to:**

- [Listed Strategy](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#listed-strategy) *may be charged per leg or per strategy by venue rule* Exchange Fee Charge
- [Venue Execution](../execution/concepts.md#venue-execution) *can give rise to* Exchange Fee Charge

### Commission

*A loose commercial word for what a broker charges per contract; it must be resolved to a specific fee before use.*

**Definition.** Commercial label for the remuneration a broker charges a client per contract or per trade, quoted per side or per round turn, for execution, clearing or both; not an economic category of its own, so every use must be resolved to the client execution fee, the client clearing fee or a bundled execution and clearing fee. Deferred by the sponsor; resolution stays blocked. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** A client asks for 'our commission on Venue V futures': the answer depends on whether execution, clearing or a bundled fee is meant.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Execution Fee: one possible meaning of commission among several.

**Caution.** Never resolve "commission" to this ID alone; return candidates Client Execution Fee, Client Clearing Fee and a bundled execute-and-clear charge, and ask.

**Connected to:**

- [Bundled Execution and Clearing Fee](#bundled-execution-and-clearing-fee) *is a candidate reading of* Commission

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Commission” (open, deferred by sponsor, answer proposed); “Fee Charge and Commission types” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Revenue

*What the business earns over a period, measured under stated management rules.*

**Definition.** Management measure of the income an observing entity earns from its business over a period, in a stated currency and under a stated management reporting basis (including whether pass-through charges are shown gross or net), attributable to a target such as a client, commercial group, service offering or order handling channel; built from fee charges earned and other income streams the basis includes. Distinct from cash received, from contribution after costs and from accounting revenue.

**Example.** Execution and clearing revenue earned from Client A last quarter, in euro, with venue fees shown net.

**Not to be confused with.** Cash received: revenue can be reported before or without the money arriving.

**Breaks down into:** [Execution Revenue](#execution-revenue) (kind of), [Clearing Revenue](#clearing-revenue) (kind of), [Collateral Revenue](#collateral-revenue) (kind of), [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis) (part of)

**Before using it, know:** Observing entity, attribution target, management basis, period, currency, gross or net of pass-through, service mix; for Execution Revenue also order handling channel (sponsor decision 2026-11-01)

**Connected to:**

- *attributed to* [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role), [Commercial Group](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#commercial-group), [Service Offering](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-offering) — Only under a semantic contract; observing entity, period and currency are mandatory. Revenue is attributed per service to that service's contractual client (sponsor decision 2026-11-01).
- [Contracting Entity](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#contracting-entity) *is the observing entity for* Revenue
- [Intra-group Client](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#intra-group-client) *is flagged for elimination in* Revenue

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Fee, revenue and cash received” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20, answer proposed); “Revenue by client” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20 and 2026-11-01, answer proposed); “Volume” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Execution Revenue

*What the business earns from executing orders, per client and per handling channel.*

**Definition.** Revenue an observing entity earns from execution services, attributed to the Service Offering (and its service kind) under which the client is served and to the contractual client of the execution relationship, and split by order handling channel; it includes client execution fees and executing brokerage received, with venue charges passed on shown gross or net as the management reporting basis states.

**Example.** Execution revenue from Client A's care orders and from its direct market access orders, shown separately.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Revenue: earned from clearing the trades, possibly for a different client relationship.

**Kind of:** [Revenue](#revenue)

**Before using it, know:** Observing entity, attribution target, management basis, period, currency, gross or net of pass-through, service mix; for Execution Revenue also order handling channel (sponsor decision 2026-11-01)

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Revenue by client” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20 and 2026-11-01, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

<sub>Measure · level 1 · Draft · reference LD-C0041 · accountable owner: the Revenue domain owner (not yet approved)</sub>

### Clearing Revenue

*What the business earns from clearing trades, per client and per clearing service.*

**Definition.** Revenue an observing entity earns from clearing services, attributed to the Service Offering (and its service kind) under which the client is served, to the contractual client of the clearing relationship, and also per Clearing Service of the CCP; it includes client clearing fees, with CCP charges passed on shown gross or net as the management reporting basis states. Collateral income is a separate stream whose inclusion is decided by the Collateral Manager and is never implicit.

**Example.** Clearing revenue from Client A at CCP Z's listed derivatives service, excluding interest on its cash collateral.

**Not to be confused with.** Collateral Revenue: income from client collateral, a separate stream with its own owner.

**Kind of:** [Revenue](#revenue)

**Note.** Inclusion of collateral income (interest on client cash, price alignment pass-through) is undecided and never implicit. [04, 05]

**Before using it, know:** Observing entity, attribution target, management basis, period, currency, gross or net of pass-through, service mix; for Execution Revenue also order handling channel (sponsor decision 2026-11-01)

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Revenue by client” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20 and 2026-11-01, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

<sub>Measure · level 1 · Draft · reference LD-C0042 · accountable owner: the Revenue domain owner (not yet approved)</sub>

### Collateral Revenue

*What the business earns from holding clients' collateral, reported separately from clearing fees.*

**Definition.** Revenue an observing entity earns from client collateral over a period: income from client cash collateral net of interest paid to clients, where the firm earns any; fees for holding non-cash collateral; and other streams the Management Reporting Basis includes (for example price alignment amounts received and not passed on, if the Collateral Manager so decides). Price alignment amounts are otherwise neither fees nor collateral revenue. It is never included in clearing revenue implicitly.

**Example.** Income from the difference between interest received on Client A's cash collateral and the interest paid to Client A, plus a fee for holding its bonds.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Revenue: fees for clearing trades; collateral revenue comes from the collateral, not the trades.

**Kind of:** [Revenue](#revenue)

**Values (draft list):** stream: interest on cash collateral; non-cash collateral fees; other

**Words people use:** “collateral income” (any); “NII on client cash” (finance usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Observing entity, period, currency, streams included, and the decision on inclusion in clearing revenue.

**Connected to:**

- [Price Alignment Amount](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#price-alignment-amount) *counts in only if the management reporting basis says so* Collateral Revenue
- *is built in part from* [Cash Collateral Interest](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#cash-collateral-interest)
- *is defined by* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis)

### Management Reporting Basis

*The agreed rules that say how revenue figures are put together.*

**Definition.** Approved, versioned set of rules under which management revenue, cost and contribution are reported: observing entity, attribution rule (per service to the contractual client, roll-up to commercial group, intra-group elimination), period and date role, reporting currency and conversion rule, gross or net presentation of pass-through charges and rebates, split rule for bundled fees, and the income streams included. Each economic component is counted once: where one measure includes another (for example clearing revenue including collateral revenue when the Collateral Manager so decides), the basis states it and the two are never added together for the same scope.

**Example.** Revenue reported per contracting entity, by CCP clearing date, in euro, with exchange fees shown net and collateral income shown separately.

**Not to be confused with.** Accounting policy: management reporting may differ from the accounts and is not a statement of accounting treatment.

**Part of:** [Revenue](#revenue)

**Values (draft list):** pass-through presentation: gross; net; rebate presentation: gross; netted

**Words people use:** “management basis” (repository usage); “reporting rules” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Version and owner; an answer always names the basis it uses.

**Connected to:**

- [Pass-through Charge](#pass-through-charge) *is shown gross or net according to* Management Reporting Basis
- [Bundled Execution and Clearing Fee](#bundled-execution-and-clearing-fee) *is split according to* Management Reporting Basis
- [Fee Rebate](#fee-rebate) *is netted or shown gross according to* Management Reporting Basis
- [Collateral Revenue](#collateral-revenue) *is defined by* Management Reporting Basis
- [Cost](#cost) *is measured under* Management Reporting Basis — Corrected 2026-11-09 (direction was reversed).
- [Contribution](#contribution) *is measured under* Management Reporting Basis — Corrected 2026-11-09 (direction was reversed).
- *rolls revenue up to* [Commercial Group](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#commercial-group)
- *sets the date role of* [Business Date](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#business-date)
- [Cost Perimeter](#cost-perimeter) *is versioned with* Management Reporting Basis

### Cost

*What the business spends to deliver its services, measured under stated management rules.*

**Definition.** Management measure of expense that an observing entity incurs for its business over a period, in a stated currency and under a stated management reporting basis, classified by cost category (venue and CCP fees not recharged, third-party brokerage, third-party clearing, connectivity and market data, funding and collateral, default fund funding, attributed shared cost) and attributable to a target only under a stated attribution rule; which costs a contribution measure deducts is set by an approved cost perimeter.

**Example.** Exchange fees the firm owed Venue V and did not pass on to clients last month.

**Not to be confused with.** Pass-through Charge: a third-party fee recharged to the client is not a cost to the firm in the same way.

**Note.** Cost-category facet: non-recharged venue and CCP fees, third-party brokerage, third-party clearing, connectivity and market data, funding and collateral, allocated shared cost. [05]

**Connected to:**

- [Connectivity Route](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#connectivity-route) *generates cost of category connectivity and market data* Cost
- [Default Fund Contribution](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#default-fund-contribution) *gives rise to a funding cost in* Cost
- *is measured under* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis) — Corrected 2026-11-09 (direction was reversed).
- [Cost Perimeter](#cost-perimeter) *selects categories of* Cost

### Contribution

*What a client, service or venue brings in after deducting the costs an approved list includes.*

**Definition.** Management revenue attributable to a target (client relationship, commercial group, service offering, venue or clearing service) less the costs within an explicitly approved cost perimeter, for a stated observing entity, period and currency; no formula or default cost perimeter is approved.

**Example.** Client A's revenue less the venue and CCP fees the firm absorbed for it, for one quarter in euro.

**Not to be confused with.** Revenue: before any cost. Which costs are deducted is set by the cost perimeter named with each contribution, never by the word contribution alone.

**Breaks down into:** [Cost Perimeter](#cost-perimeter) (part of)

**Before using it, know:** Observing entity, attribution target, management basis, period, currency, gross or net of pass-through, service mix; for Execution Revenue also order handling channel (sponsor decision 2026-11-01)

**Connected to:**

- *derived from* [Revenue](#revenue), [Cost](#cost) — Only under an approved semantic contract and cost perimeter; no formula approved.
- *is measured under* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis) — Corrected 2026-11-09 (direction was reversed).

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Fee, revenue and cash received” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20, answer proposed); “Revenue by client” (open, narrowed 2026-09-20 and 2026-11-01, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Cost Perimeter

*The agreed list of costs that are deducted to get contribution.*

**Definition.** Approved, versioned list of cost categories, with their attribution rules, that a given contribution measure deducts from revenue; any cost outside the list is excluded from that measure. No default perimeter is approved.

**Example.** A first perimeter limited to venue and CCP fees not recharged and third-party brokerage and clearing.

**Not to be confused with.** Cost: the perimeter selects which costs count; cost is the expense itself.

**Part of:** [Contribution](#contribution)

**Values (draft list):** candidate cost categories (no default perimeter): venue and CCP fees not recharged; third-party brokerage; third-party clearing; connectivity and market data; funding and collateral; default fund funding; introducer or fee-sharing payments; attributed shared cost

**Words people use:** “cost scope” (any); “contribution level” (finance usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Version, owner, approval date; categories included.

**Connected to:**

- *selects categories of* [Cost](#cost)
- *is versioned with* [Management Reporting Basis](#management-reporting-basis)

### Fee Collector

*A party that collects a fee for someone else.*

**Definition.** Role of a party that bills and collects a fee charge on behalf of its creditor without being the creditor, such as a CCP collecting a venue's trading fees from clearing members, or a clearing broker collecting executing brokerage or regulatory fees; the collector passes the amount on and does not earn it.

**Example.** CCP Z collects Venue V's trading fees from Clearing Broker Y on its monthly bill and pays them to Venue V.

**Not to be confused with.** Creditor: the party the fee is owed to and who earns it.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Words people use:** “billing agent” (any); “collecting organisation” (repository usage)

**Before using it, know:** Which creditor it collects for, and under which rule or agreement.

**Connected to:**

- *collects on behalf of the creditor* [Fee Charge](#fee-charge)
- *may issue* [Fee Invoice](#fee-invoice)

### Activity Volume

*How much was traded or cleared, in a stated unit and counting rule.*

**Definition.** Measure of business activity for a stated scope and period, counted in a stated unit (contract sides, round turns, tickets or trades, notional), on a stated counting basis (single or double), at a stated stage (executed, cleared, open) and from a stated source (published by a venue or CCP, or the firm's own records).

**Example.** Client A's cleared volume at CCP Z last month in contract sides, single-counted, from the firm's records.

**Not to be confused with.** Position: volume counts activity over a period; a position is what is open at a point in time.

**Values (draft list):** unit: contract sides; round turns; tickets or trades; notional; counting basis: single; double; stage: executed; cleared (open quantities are a Position, not activity); executed side: venue-facing; client-facing; strategy counting: per strategy; per leg; execution method scope: on-book only; including negotiated trades; source: published by a venue or CCP; the observing firm's records

**Words people use:** “volume” (ambiguous); “lots” (listed usage, contextual); “tickets” (OTC usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Unit, counting basis, stage, source, scope, period.

**Connected to:**

- *may set the tier of* [Fee Rate](#fee-rate)
- *may decide eligibility under* [Fee Incentive Programme](#fee-incentive-programme)
- *counts* [Execution](../execution/concepts.md#execution)
- *counts* [Clearing Acceptance](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-acceptance)
