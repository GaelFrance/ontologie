### Clearing Service

*One line of business of a CCP, such as its listed equity derivatives clearing or its interest rate swap clearing, with its own members, accounts, margin and rules.*

**Definition.** Distinct line of clearing operated by one central counterparty under its rules for a defined product scope (its eligible contracts or OTC products), with its own membership categories, CCP account types, margin methodology, fee schedule and default-management arrangements; default resources may be shared with other services of the same CCP. Not the firm's Service Offering.

**Example.** CCP Z runs two clearing services: one for listed futures and options, one for interest rate swaps; Clearing Broker Y is a member of the first only.

**Not to be confused with.** Service Offering: the clearing service a broker sells to its clients, not the CCP's.

**Breaks down into:** [Clearing Contract](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#clearing-contract) (part of), [Clearing Eligibility Schedule](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#clearing-eligibility-schedule) (part of), [Default Fund Contribution](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#default-fund-contribution) (part of)

### Clearing Member

*A firm admitted by a CCP to one of its clearing services, in whose name trades are registered and which answers to the CCP for them.*

**Definition.** Role of a party admitted to a Clearing Service under a membership category defined in that service's rules, which entitles it to have trades registered in its own name in CCP accounts and makes it directly answerable to the central counterparty for them, within the clearing scope its category allows (own business, affiliates, clients, non-clearing members). Held per clearing service and for an effective period. A participant admitted under a sponsored or direct-client model holds the Sponsored Clearing Participant role; it also holds Clearing Member only where that CCP's rules call it a member (to verify per rulebook).

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y is a general clearing member of CCP Z's listed derivatives service, so it may clear its own trades and those of its clients there.

**Not to be confused with.** Sponsored Clearing Participant: admitted in its own name under a sponsored model, but a clearing member only where the CCP's rules say so.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Words people use:** “non-clearing member” (Listed markets)

**Note.** Membership is per clearing service. A non-clearing member is a venue member without this role. FCM is a US registration, not this role. [02, 03]

**Connected to:**

- [Give-up / Take-up Process](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#give-up--take-up-process) *has giving member* Clearing Member — The clearing member holding the trade before give-up, normally the executing side's clearing member.

### Clearing Broker

*The firm that clears a client's trades for it and is answerable to that client, whether or not it is itself a member of the CCP.*

**Definition.** Role of a party that provides clearing of an identified client's trades in a clearing service under a Client Clearing Agreement and is responsible to that client for them, either as a clearing member of that service or through another clearing member, including a client of a clearing member that clears for its own indirect clients; distinct from admission at the CCP (Clearing Member) and from regulatory registrations such as futures commission merchant.

**Example.** Client A clears its futures at CCP Z through Clearing Broker Y, which is a member there; at CCP W, Clearing Broker Y clears Client A's trades through another member.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Member: admission at the CCP; and FCM, a US registration of the legal entity.

**Kind of:** [Broker](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#broker)

**Words people use:** “clearing broker” (Client clearing); “FCM” (US)

**Connected to:**

- *clears through* [Clearing Member](#clearing-member) — When the clearing broker is not itself a member of the clearing service; at most one per service and period.
- [Receiving Clearing Member](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#receiving-clearing-member) *acts for* Clearing Broker
- [Client Back-to-Back Trade](#client-back-to-back-trade) *has as one party* Clearing Broker

**Open questions on its meaning:** “Brokerage paid” (open, answer proposed); “Clearing broker, clearing member, FCM” (open, answer proposed) — see the [review sheet](review.md).

### Central Counterparty

*The role of the clearing house that steps in between buyer and seller and becomes the counterparty to each.*

**Definition.** Role of a legal entity that interposes itself between the counterparties to trades accepted in one or more of its clearing services, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer, by the legal mechanism (novation, open offer or other) and at the moment its rules define. Its authorisation, recognition or registration in each regime is a separate Regulatory Classification.

**Example.** After CCP Z accepts a trade between Clearing Members P and Q, P faces CCP Z and Q faces CCP Z; P and Q no longer face each other.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Service: the CCP acts through one or more services, each with its own rules.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Words people use:** “DCO” (US)

**Note.** Exercised through one or more clearing services. Authorisation (EMIR Art. 14, extension per service Art. 15), recognition of third-country CCPs (Art. 25) and QCCP status (CRR Art. 4(1)(88)) are time-bounded Regulatory Classifications, to verify. [02, 06]

**Connected to:**

- [Clearing Acceptance](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-acceptance) *accepted by* Central Counterparty — Within an identified Clearing Service and for an identified member account; acceptance is evidence of, not identical to, CCP Interposition.
- [Clearing Service](#clearing-service) *operated by* Central Counterparty — Exactly one CCP per clearing service (definitional); a CCP operates one or more services.

### CCP Account

*An account that a CCP opens in one clearing service, for a member's own business or for its clients, where the CCP records positions and attributes margin and collateral.*

**Definition.** Account opened by a central counterparty within one clearing service, in the name of a clearing member or, under a sponsored or direct-client model, of the participant admitted in its own name, of a type defined by that service's rules (house; client omnibus, net or gross; individually segregated client; indirect client, basic or gross omnibus; regime-specific customer account classes such as the US futures, foreign futures and cleared swaps customer accounts), to which the CCP attributes positions and, directly or through a linked margin or collateral unit defined by its rules, margin requirements and collateral. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y holds at CCP Z a house account, a net omnibus client account and an individually segregated account for Client A.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Account: the account the clearing broker keeps in its own books for each client and maps to a CCP account.

### House Account

*The CCP account for a clearing member's own trading.*

**Definition.** CCP account in which a clearing member's own trades and positions are recorded and, where the member chooses and the service's rules allow, those of its affiliates; it is kept apart from client accounts, and its margining basis (net or gross) is stated per clearing service.

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y's own hedging trades at CCP Z are registered in its house account; its affiliate Firm R is cleared there too.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Omnibus Account: holds clients' positions, kept apart from the house account.

**Kind of:** [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

**Words people use:** “proprietary account” (any); “house origin” (US); “own account” (EU)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service; whether affiliates are cleared here or as clients.

**Connected to:**

- *is held in the name of* [Clearing Member](#clearing-member)
- *is opened within* [Clearing Service](#clearing-service)
- *may contain* [CCP Position Account](#ccp-position-account)

### Client Omnibus Account

*One CCP account shared by several clients of a clearing member, kept apart from the member's own business.*

**Definition.** CCP account, held in a clearing member's name, in which the positions and collateral of several clients are recorded together, apart from the member's own; it is net or gross, and its margining basis is stated per clearing service. The US customer account classes at a derivatives clearing organisation are margined gross, and cleared swaps customer collateral is attributed per customer (to verify); they must not be equated with EU or UK net omnibus accounts.

**Example.** Clients A, B and C of Clearing Broker Y are cleared at CCP Z in Y's gross omnibus client account; each client has its own position account inside it.

**Not to be confused with.** An internal pooled account: a grouping in the broker's own books, which the CCP does not see.

**Kind of:** [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

**Values (draft list):** net omnibus (EU, UK); gross omnibus (EU, UK); US futures customer account (Section 4d segregation, to verify); US foreign futures customer account (Part 30 secured amount, to verify); US cleared swaps customer account (Part 22, LSOC, to verify)

**Words people use:** “omnibus” (ambiguous; see the open question on omnibus, house and client); “customer segregated origin” (US); “OSA, omnibus segregated account” (EU usage)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, regime, net or gross basis, and the internal accounts mapped to it as of the clearing date.

**Connected to:**

- *is held in the name of* [Clearing Member](#clearing-member)
- *applies* [Segregation Model](#segregation-model)
- *contains when gross* [CCP Position Account](#ccp-position-account)
- *is mapped from the clients internal accounts* [Account](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#account)

### Individually Segregated Client Account

*A CCP account used for one client alone, so its positions and collateral are visible separately at the CCP.*

**Definition.** CCP account, opened in a clearing member's name for one client only, in which that client's positions and the collateral provided for it are recorded apart from all other clients. Where collateral above the client's requirement must sit is set by the regime (for example posted to the CCP for individually segregated clients under EU and UK EMIR, to verify) and otherwise by the CCP's rules and the client clearing agreement.

**Example.** Client A elects individual segregation at CCP Z; Clearing Broker Y opens an account for Client A alone in Y's name.

**Not to be confused with.** US cleared swaps (LSOC) accounts: legally segregated per customer but held in a shared omnibus account at the DCO.

**Kind of:** [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

**Words people use:** “ISA” (EU usage); “individual client segregation” (EMIR)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, regime, the client's election and its effective date; where any excess collateral is held.

**Connected to:**

- *is held in the name of* [Clearing Member](#clearing-member)
- *is opened following* [Segregation Election](#segregation-election)
- *holds collateral attributed to one client* [Collateral](../margin-and-collateral/concepts.md#collateral)

### Indirect Client Account

*A CCP account for the clients of a clearing member's client.*

**Definition.** CCP account, held in a clearing member's name for a client that itself clears for indirect clients, in which the indirect clients' positions and collateral are recorded apart from that client's own business and from the member's. EU rules provide a basic omnibus form (all indirect clients of that client together) and a gross omnibus form (each indirect client's positions kept apart and margined separately).

**Example.** Broker B clears through Clearing Broker Y at CCP Z and offers clearing to its own client, Fund F; Fund F's positions sit in Y's indirect client account for Broker B.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Omnibus Account: for direct clients of the member, not for clients of its clients.

**Kind of:** [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

**Values (draft list):** basic omnibus indirect client account (EU); gross omnibus indirect client account (EU); listed and OTC variants (separate EU rules)

**Words people use:** “indirect clearing account” (EU)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, regime, the client that provides indirect clearing, the basic or gross form.

**Connected to:**

- *is held in the name of* [Clearing Member](#clearing-member)
- *results from an indirect clearing access arrangement* [Market Access Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#market-access-arrangement)

### CCP Position Account

*A sub-account at the CCP where positions are kept apart inside a larger account.*

**Definition.** Subdivision of a CCP account, opened under a clearing service's rules, in which the CCP keeps open positions separately, for example one per client inside a gross omnibus account. Margin is calculated on a Margin Portfolio that can group several position accounts, or split one.

**Example.** Inside Clearing Broker Y's gross omnibus account at CCP Z, Client A and Client B each have a position account; margin is called on the omnibus account.

**Not to be confused with.** Client Account: kept by the clearing broker in its own books; a position account is kept by the CCP.

**Part of:** [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

**Words people use:** “sub-account” (CCP usage, ambiguous); “position-keeping account” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service and the CCP's account structure as of the clearing date.

**Connected to:**

- [House Account](#house-account) *may contain* CCP Position Account

### Segregation Model

*The rules that say how a client's positions and assets are kept apart from the clearing member's and from other clients'.*

**Definition.** Set of rules, set by a regulation, a CCP's rules or an agreement, for keeping the positions and assets of one party or group of parties apart from those of others (for example clients from the clearing member, or one client from all other clients), at a stated level (CCP books or clearing member books) and under a stated regime. It describes how records and assets are separated; the legal protection that results is a separate conclusion of Legal.

**Example.** Under omnibus segregation, Client A's positions sit at CCP Z with other clients' but apart from Clearing Broker Y's own; under individual segregation they sit alone.

**Not to be confused with.** Legal protection: whether the client's assets would actually be safe in a default is a separate conclusion of Legal.

**Note.** Public instances, not subclasses: EMIR omnibus and individual client segregation (Art. 39); indirect-clearing account structures (Delegated Regulations (EU) 2017/2154 for listed and 2017/2155 for OTC); US futures-customer segregation, cleared-swaps LSOC (17 CFR Part 22) and foreign-futures secured amount (Part 30); paragraphs to verify. State the level (CCP or member books). Legal protection is a Legal conclusion. [02, 03, 04, 06]

**Connected to:**

- [Client Clearing Agreement](#client-clearing-agreement) *elects* Segregation Model — Per clearing service and time-bounded; legal protection is a Legal conclusion, never inferred from the model name.
- [CCP Account](#ccp-account) *implements* Segregation Model — Together with the clearing service, the model determines the margining basis (gross or net) and collateral attribution.
- [Indirect Clearing Arrangement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#indirect-clearing-arrangement) *offers account options governed by* Segregation Model
- [Client Omnibus Account](#client-omnibus-account) *applies* Segregation Model
- [Segregation Election](#segregation-election) *selects one option of* Segregation Model
- [Porting](#porting) *depends on the segregation applied to* Segregation Model

### Agreement

*Any arrangement that creates rights and duties between parties, whether signed or accepted by joining published rules.*

**Definition.** Arrangement creating rights and obligations between identified parties, for example for a service, documented by one or more signed documents or by adherence to published rules (such as a CCP rulebook or a venue's rules), with an effective period.

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y is bound to CCP Z by its membership documents and the CCP rulebook; Client A is bound to Clearing Broker Y by its client clearing agreement.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Provision: a single rule within a rulebook or regulation, not the relationship it governs.

### Client Clearing Agreement

*The contract between a client and its clearing broker that sets how the client is cleared: legal model, segregation, margin, collateral, limits, fees and what happens on default.*

### Cleared OTC Position

*The cleared swaps still running in one account on one day.*

**Definition.** Position made of the set of open cleared OTC trades (CCP-facing trades, or client back-to-back trades at the member-to-client tier) recorded for an identified account within a clearing service, as of a clearing date. ### CCP-facing Trade

*The cleared trade that a member holds with the CCP after clearing, in its house account or in a client account.*

**Definition.** Kind of Trade between a central counterparty and the clearing member, or the participant admitted in its own name under a sponsored or direct-client model, that holds the CCP account in which it is registered (house or client). It is created by CCP Interposition under a clearing service's rules and succeeds the executed trade on that side. A change of holding member or account (give-up registration, position transfer, porting), a change of economic terms, compression or close-out ends or reduces it and creates a linked successor trade; it is never changed in place. Only a non-economic correction (for example a client reference) is recorded as a Trade Amendment against the same trade.

**Example.** Executing Broker X's trade with Executing Broker Q is cleared at CCP Z; Clearing Broker Y now holds a trade with CCP Z in its client account for Client A.

**Not to be confused with.** Executed trade: the trade agreed before clearing, which clearing replaces.

**Kind of:** [Trade](../execution/concepts.md#trade)

**Words people use:** “cleared trade” (ambiguous with the client back-to-back trade); “CCP trade” (contextual); “beta and gamma trades” (OTC clearing, one per side)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, CCP account and tier; the legal model of the client relationship for a client account.

**Connected to:**

- [Executed Trade](../execution/concepts.md#executed-trade) *is succeeded by* CCP-facing Trade

### Client Back-to-Back Trade

*The matching trade between the clearing broker and its client that exists when the broker clears as principal.*

**Definition.** Under the principal-to-principal client clearing model, the trade between a clearing broker and its client that mirrors, on opposite sides, the CCP-facing trade held in the client's account; it arises from the client clearing agreement, not from the CCP's rules. The same pattern repeats between a client and its indirect client. Under the agency model no such trade exists: the clearing broker carries the CCP-facing trade for the client and guarantees its performance.

**Example.** Clearing Broker Y holds a swap with CCP Z in its client account for Client A and, on opposite terms, the same swap with Client A.

**Not to be confused with.** CCP-Facing Trade: the trade the member holds with the CCP; the back-to-back trade is the one it holds with the client.

**Kind of:** [Trade](../execution/concepts.md#trade)

**Words people use:** “member-client trade” (ambiguity register term); “client trade” (OTC client clearing); “mirror trade”

**Before using it, know:** Legal model of the client clearing agreement for that service and regime; tier (member to client, or client to indirect client).

### Position Transfer

*Moving open positions from one CCP account to another on request, for example when a client changes clearing broker.*

**Definition.** Movement, at the request of a clearing member or its client and under a clearing service's transfer rules, of existing open positions (or open cleared trades) from one CCP account to another, within one clearing member or between two clearing members, without a new execution; the client behind the positions normally stays the same, and the price basis, consent, deadlines and fees are set by the CCP's rules. Distinct from a give-up, which attributes a new trade, and from porting, which follows a member default.

**Example.** Client A moves its open futures from Clearing Broker Y to Clearing Broker V at CCP Z; both members agree and CCP Z moves the positions.

**Not to be confused with.** Porting: a move forced by a clearing member's default, with its own rules and collateral leg.

**Kind of:** [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event)

**Values (draft list):** trigger: client changes clearing broker; trigger: account correction; trigger: clearing member exit or restructuring; scope: within one member, between members

**Words people use:** “transfer” (listed, ambiguous with give-up); “position move” (any)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, source and target accounts and members, trigger, effective clearing date; collateral moves separately if at all.

**Connected to:**

- *moves positions from and to* [CCP Account](#ccp-account)

### Porting

*Moving clients' positions and collateral from a failed clearing member to a back-up member, or closing them out if that fails.*

**Definition.** Process, run under a CCP's default rules after a clearing member default, by which the positions recorded in a client account of the defaulting member, and the collateral attributable to them, are transferred to a back-up clearing member that accepts them, or, if transfer is not achieved within the time the rules allow, closed out with any balance returned to the clients or for their account. Positions and collateral are separate legs with separate outcomes.

**Example.** After Clearing Member P defaults at CCP Z, Client A's individually segregated account is ported to Back-up Clearing Member Y; the omnibus clients who named no back-up are closed out.

**Not to be confused with.** Position Transfer: a voluntary move requested by the member or client.

**Step in:** [Clearing Member Default](#clearing-member-default)

**Values (draft list):** outcome: ported, closed out, partly ported; leg: positions, collateral

**Words people use:** “portability” (regulatory, the property of being portable); “transfer of client positions on default” (EMIR)

**Before using it, know:** Clearing service, account kind, segregation election, back-up member, legal form of collateral; whether it actually succeeds is a Legal and CCP fact, never inferred from the account kind.

- [Clearing Member Default](#clearing-member-default) *may start* Porting

### Client Default

*When a clearing broker declares its own client in default and takes action on that client's positions.*

**Definition.** Declaration by a clearing broker, under a client clearing agreement, that its client is in default, which allows the broker to close out, transfer or hedge the client's positions and use its collateral. It is distinct from a Clearing Member Default declared by a CCP, and from a settlement fail or an unmet margin call, which may lead to it but are not it.

**Example.** Client A misses several margin calls; Clearing Broker Y declares an event of default under their agreement and closes out Client A's futures.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Member Default: declared by the CCP against a member, not by a broker against its client.

**Kind of:** [Lifecycle Event](../post-trade-operations/concepts.md#lifecycle-event)
