## What you are asked to review

- **Regulatory Affairs:** Take one CCP circular, one exchange notice and one consultation (EU, UK, US). Can each be recorded as one or more rule changes, with a status and dates, against the provision or published specification it changes?
- **Legal and Compliance:** Are regulatory classifications (qualifying CCP, FCM, client category) attached to the right entity, role, account or service, with a basis and a validity period?
- **Listed Execution product owner:** Do venue rule changes (block thresholds, EFRP rules, direct electronic access and algorithm controls) fit the rule change concept?
- **OTC Clearing product owner:** Does Regulatory Obligation describe the clearing obligation well enough: counterparty classes, product scope, phase-in dates and exemptions?
- **Compliance:** Position Limit is new: does it cover limits, accountability levels and reportable levels as you monitor them?
- **Everyone:** for each concept in section 1, is the definition **correct**, **complete** and **worded the way your teams use it**? Write *OK*, *Change* (say what) or *Unsure*. Is a concept your area relies on **missing**, or one **unnecessary**? The [concept cards](concepts.md) give examples.
- **Everyone:** for each open question in section 3, do you **agree with the proposed answer**? If not, what is the rule in practice?
- How *our* firm works goes in the [questions for our firm](company-questions.md), never in the definitions. Mention internal terms or system names only in your reply, never in this repository.

## 1. Concepts you approve

| Concept | Definition | Your verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[Regulatory Regime](concepts.md#regulatory-regime)** | Named framework of law and regulation in one jurisdiction (for example EU EMIR, UK EMIR, MiFID II and MiFIR, CRR, the US Commodity Exchange Act with CFTC rules), made up of its legal acts, technical standards and rules and the official guidance published on them, identified by jurisdiction, the authorities that adopt and supervise it, and its legal instruments, and applicable during an effective period. One jurisdiction has several regimes; an onshored copy of another jurisdiction's law is a separate regime. Regimes may be related by dated equivalence, recognition or substituted-compliance decisions. A CCP or venue rulebook is not part of any regime. | |
| **[Rule Provision](concepts.md#rule-provision)** | Versioned, citable unit of a legal act, technical standard, official guidance or relief, a CCP or venue rulebook or procedure, or the firm's own procedure, with its issuer, binding force, scope and effective period, that imposes a requirement, grants a permission, exemption or relief, defines a term or sets a parameter (such as a threshold or a deadline). Provisions can contain provisions; the citable level used is stated. A version is never edited: a new version supersedes it. Industry association guidance is not a rule provision. The firm's own procedures are recorded only in the internal layer. | |
| **[Rule Change](concepts.md#rule-change)** | Proposed or adopted change to one rule provision, or to another published, versioned specification (fee schedule, contract specification, business calendar, cut-off set by a rule, margin methodology, margin parameter set announced by notice, collateral eligibility schedule, clearing eligibility schedule, access requirement), by which it is created, amended, replaced or withdrawn. It is followed from proposal to application, with a lifecycle status (proposed or in consultation, adopted, approved or self-certified where the issuer's supervisor must act, in force, applicable, superseded, withdrawn) and dates qualified by their role (publication, consultation close, adoption, entry into force, application, phase-in, end of transition, and the date the firm recorded it). Contract terms agreed with a client, routine parameter publications under an unchanged method, and new series listed under an unchanged contract specification are not rule changes; nor is a change of one party's regulatory classification. One published notice can announce several rule changes, and one rule change can be announced in several notices. Type: Process (proposed). ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Regulatory Classification](concepts.md#regulatory-classification)** | Time-bounded status that a named regulatory regime gives to a legal entity (or a branch where the regime says so), a role assignment, an account, a trading venue or venue segment, a CCP or clearing service, an instrument, contract specification or product family, or a trade, with its basis (authority decision or registration, notification, calculation, self-assessment, representation by the counterparty), the source that asserts it and the evidence; valid time and record time are both kept, and 'unknown' is a value, never a default. ⚠️ *meaning changed 2026-11* | |
| **[Regulatory Obligation](concepts.md#regulatory-obligation)** | Duty that a regulatory regime imposes on parties holding a stated regulatory classification or role, for a defined product or activity scope, from stated application dates, subject to conditions, thresholds and exemptions, and defined by one or more rule provisions that may change separately. Whether an obligation applies to a named party is a conclusion recorded only in the internal layer. | |
| **[Clearing Obligation](concepts.md#clearing-obligation)** *(level 2)* | Regulatory obligation to clear trades in a declared class of derivatives at an authorised or recognised CCP, from phased application dates. In the EU and UK it applies to pairs of counterparties holding in-scope statuses; in the US it applies to the class unless an exception or exemption is elected (to verify). Never applies to a product alone. An intragroup exemption depends on the group as the regime defines it, recorded as a regulatory classification of the counterparty pair, never on the commercial group or the intra-group client flag. | |
| **[Regulated Product Class](concepts.md#regulated-product-class)** *(level 2)* | Category of derivatives defined by a rule provision of a regime as the scope of an obligation or classification, by asset class, product type and, where relevant, trade terms (currency, index, maturity range, settlement type), versioned with the provisions that define it. Instruments, contract specifications or trades are placed in it by a regulatory classification. | |
| **[Phase-in Step](concepts.md#phase-in-step)** *(level 2)* | Dated stage in the application of a regulatory obligation or rule change to a defined subset of parties or products (counterparty category, size band, product class, activity), with its application date and any end of transition; a rule usually applies through several steps rather than from one date. | |
| **[Rulebook](concepts.md#rulebook)** *(level 2)* | Complete, versioned body of rules, procedures and clearing or trading conditions issued by one CCP or trading venue operator, binding its members or participants by contract and under its supervisor's oversight; it contains the infrastructure's rule provisions and is not part of any regime. It refers to the issuer's Published Fee Schedule and Contract Specifications, whose meanings are defined on the fees and instruments pages; a change to one is a Rule Change whose changed object is that schedule or specification. The firm's own procedures are rule provisions without a rulebook. | |
| **[Regulatory Relief](concepts.md#regulatory-relief)** *(level 2)* | Rule provision issued by an authority or its staff that removes, defers or modifies the effect of another provision for named parties or a defined class, often for a limited period and on conditions: exemption, no-action relief, transitional measure, supervisory forbearance statement or deferral. It never amends the provision it relieves. | |
| **[Equivalence Decision](concepts.md#equivalence-decision)** *(level 2)* | Rule provision by which an authority of one regime finds that the rules of another jurisdiction meet its own for a stated purpose (CCP recognition, trading venue treatment, compliance by substitution), with its scope, conditions, effective period and any expiry. It relates two regimes; the status of a single CCP or venue that relies on it is a separate regulatory classification. | |
| **[Change Notice](concepts.md#change-notice)** | Published document by a regulator, CCP, venue operator or industry body that announces, proposes or explains one or more rule changes: consultation paper, final report, published legal act, circular, market notice, clearing advisory, rule filing or self-certification, official guidance or Q&A, with its issuer, reference, publication date and language. It is evidence of rule changes, not the change itself; one notice can announce several changes and one change can appear in several notices. | |
| **[Consultation](concepts.md#consultation)** *(level 2)* | Public stage of a proposed rule change in which the issuer invites comments on a draft by a response deadline, identified by the consultation document, its questions and its closing date; it may lead to adoption, a revised proposal or withdrawal. | |
| **[Rule Change Approval](concepts.md#rule-change-approval)** *(level 2)* | Step in which the supervisor of a CCP or venue approves, does not object to, validates, stays or rejects a change to that infrastructure's rulebook, margin model or fees, or in which the infrastructure self-certifies the change where its regime allows; until it occurs, the change is announced but not yet adopted. | |
| **[Change Impact Assessment](concepts.md#change-impact-assessment)** *(level 2)* | The firm's assessment of one rule change: which business objects it affects (clearing services, venues, account types, segregation models, give-up and acceptance steps, margin, collateral, fees, contracts, client documents), how (scope, timing, data, legal effect, cost, process, client communication), for which roles, with what confidence, whether interpretation is required, and the resulting decision (act, plan, monitor, ignore), with its owner, reviewers and sign-off status. Instances are internal. | |
| **[Regulatory Interpretation](concepts.md#regulatory-interpretation)** *(level 2)* | Documented reading of what a rule provision means or how it applies, recorded with its author kind (authority, industry association, external counsel, the firm), its basis, confidence, status and sign-off; where the firm's reading is flagged 'interpretation required', it is signed off by Regulatory Affairs and business management with Legal and Compliance. It never changes the provision and is not law. | |
| **[Regulatory Authorisation](concepts.md#regulatory-authorisation)** *(level 2)* | Regulatory classification of a legal entity (or of a branch where the regime says so) recording that an authority has authorised, registered, recognised, designated or exempted it to carry out a stated activity under a regime, with its scope, conditions, effective period and current standing (granted, conditional, suspended, withdrawn, applied for). | |
| **[Regulatory Authority](concepts.md#regulatory-authority)** *(level 2)* | Role of a public body that, under a regime, adopts rules, supervises, authorises or registers parties, grants relief or decides equivalence, within a stated remit (jurisdiction, sector, type of entity); includes legislators, supervisory authorities, central banks acting as supervisors and supranational authorities. Standard setters whose principles are not binding are recorded as issuers of guidance only. | |
| **[Regulatory Reporting Role](concepts.md#regulatory-reporting-role)** *(level 2)* | Role that a regime assigns to a party for one report type on an identified execution or trade: for example the executing entity of a transaction report, the reporting counterparty, or the entity responsible for reporting (to verify per regime). It is never inferred from the Executing Broker, Trading Venue Member or Giving Clearing Member roles. The report object itself stays in the backlog. | |
| **[Position Limit](concepts.md#position-limit)** *(level 2)* | Maximum position, or a position level that triggers scrutiny (accountability level) or reporting (reportable level), in a commodity derivative or other listed contract, set by a regulator or by a trading venue under its rules for the positions held or controlled by one person or group, aggregated across accounts and firms as the rule states, for a stated period (spot month, single month or all months) and net or by side, with the exemptions the rule allows (for example bona fide hedging). It applies to the holder of the positions; monitoring it for clients is a firm duty recorded in the internal layer. Distinct from a Clearing Limit, which a clearing member sets for its own credit and risk control. | |

## 3. Open questions on meaning

A proposed answer comes from the expert review; it is **not a decision** until the owner confirms it.

### Clearing broker, clearing member, FCM (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Service role toward the client versus admission at a CCP versus a regulatory registration (FCM in the US).

**Proposed answer:** Clearing Broker is the service role toward the client and may exist without membership of the clearing service; Clearing Member is admission at the CCP; FCM is a US regulatory registration held by a legal entity, recorded as a Regulatory Classification, and only a contextual alias. Confidence high. [02, 03, 05, 06, 08]

<sub>reference LD-A0019</sub>

### Dates of a rule change (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Publication, entry into force, application, phase-in and end of transition versus the date the firm recorded the change.

**Proposed answer:** Each Rule Change carries role-qualified dates: publication, entry into force, application, phase-in and end of transition, plus the date the firm recorded it. "Effective date" without a role is ambiguous. Confidence high. [02, 05, 06, 08]

<sub>reference LD-A0022</sub>

### Current, as of, as known at (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Valid time versus record time: valid now and latest non-superseded record, valid at a business date, or known by a timestamp.

**Proposed answer:** Current = valid now and latest non-superseded record from the stated source; as of D = valid at business date D; as known at T = only records received by T. Every answer states source, as-of date and freshness. See the time convention in [modelling guidelines](../docs/modelling-guidelines.md). Confidence high. Firm decision: whether "as known at" history is needed. [07, 08, 06]

<sub>reference LD-A0023</sub>

### Group (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Regulatory consolidation group versus commercial group versus CCP affiliate versus legal parent in public entity-relationship data.

**Proposed answer:** A regulatory consolidation group (used in some threshold calculations), a Commercial Group, a CCP's "affiliate" and a legal parent in public entity-relationship data are different; regulatory statuses never attach to a commercial group. Confidence high. [06, 05, 07]

<sub>reference LD-A0031</sub>

### OTC and listed (Open, answer proposed)

**To decide:** Regulatory "OTC" (derivatives not executed on a regulated market or equivalent) versus off-venue versus not exchange-listed; cleared is a separate dimension.

**Proposed answer:** Under EMIR Art. 2(7) a derivative not executed on a regulated market or an equivalent third-country market is OTC, so MTF and OTF trades count as OTC; venue type is a Regulatory Classification of Trading Venue. Scope modules by listing and clearing structure, not by that label. Confidence high. [06, 02, 03]
