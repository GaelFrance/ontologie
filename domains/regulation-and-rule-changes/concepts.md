### Regulatory Regime

*A body of law and regulation of one jurisdiction, such as EU EMIR or the US Commodity Exchange Act with the CFTC's rules.*

**Definition.** Named framework of law and regulation in one jurisdiction (for example EU EMIR, UK EMIR, MiFID II and MiFIR, CRR, the US Commodity Exchange Act with CFTC rules), made up of its legal acts, technical standards and rules and the official guidance published on them, identified by jurisdiction, the authorities that adopt and supervise it, and its legal instruments, and applicable during an effective period. One jurisdiction has several regimes; an onshored copy of another jurisdiction's law is a separate regime. Regimes may be related by dated equivalence, recognition or substituted-compliance decisions. A CCP or venue rulebook is not part of any regime.

**Example.** UK EMIR is a separate regime from EU EMIR, even where the texts started out identical.

**Not to be confused with.** Rulebook: the rules of one CCP or exchange, which are not part of any regime; and jurisdiction, which is just the country or area where several regimes apply.

**Breaks down into:** [Rule Provision](#rule-provision) (part of), [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation) (part of), [Regulated Product Class](#regulated-product-class) (part of)

**Connected to:**

- [Branch](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#branch) *may bring the business under a further* Regulatory Regime
- [Client Regulatory Category](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client-regulatory-category) *is defined under* Regulatory Regime

### Rule Provision

*One citable rule, in one version: an article of a regulation, a paragraph of a CCP rulebook, a staff relief letter or a firm procedure.*

**Definition.** Versioned, citable unit of a legal act, technical standard, official guidance or relief, a CCP or venue rulebook or procedure, or the firm's own procedure, with its issuer, binding force, scope and effective period, that imposes a requirement, grants a permission, exemption or relief, defines a term or sets a parameter (such as a threshold or a deadline). Provisions can contain provisions; the citable level used is stated. A version is never edited: a new version supersedes it. Industry association guidance is not a rule provision. The firm's own procedures are recorded only in the internal layer.

**Example.** The paragraph of CCP Z's procedures that sets the latest time to take up a give-up, in the version applying from a given date.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change: the move from one version of the rule to the next; and Change Notice: the document that announces it.

**Part of:** [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)

**Breaks down into:** [Offering Eligibility Criterion](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#offering-eligibility-criterion) (kind of), [Access Requirement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#access-requirement) (kind of), [Rulebook](#rulebook) (kind of), [Regulatory Relief](#regulatory-relief) (kind of), [Equivalence Decision](#equivalence-decision) (kind of)

### Rulebook

*The full set of rules a CCP or exchange publishes for its members.*

**Definition.** Complete, versioned body of rules, procedures and clearing or trading conditions issued by one CCP or trading venue operator, binding its members or participants by contract and under its supervisor's oversight; it contains the infrastructure's rule provisions and is not part of any regime. It refers to the issuer's Published Fee Schedule and Contract Specifications, whose meanings are defined on the fees and instruments pages; a change to one is a Rule Change whose changed object is that schedule or specification. The firm's own procedures are rule provisions without a rulebook.

**Example.** CCP Z's clearing rules, its procedures and its default management rules, each in a dated version, together form its rulebook.

**Not to be confused with.** Regulatory Regime: law of a jurisdiction; a CCP rulebook must comply with a regime but is not part of it.

**Kind of:** [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)

**Values (draft list):** rules; procedures; clearing conditions; trading conditions; contract terms; default management rules; fee rules

**Words people use:** “rules and procedures” (contextual); “clearing conditions” (contextual, some CCPs); “exchange rules”

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, version and effective period, which part (rules, procedures, conditions), which clearing services or venue segments it covers.

**Connected to:**

- [Market Operator](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#market-operator) *issues* Rulebook

- *governs* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *governs* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)
- *is changed after* [Rule Change Approval](#rule-change-approval)

### Regulatory Relief

*An official statement that a rule will not be enforced, or does not apply, for some firms or products, usually for a time and on conditions.*

**Definition.** Rule provision issued by an authority or its staff that removes, defers or modifies the effect of another provision for named parties or a defined class, often for a limited period and on conditions: exemption, no-action relief, transitional measure, supervisory forbearance statement or deferral. Its legal effect is recorded separately from its form: exemption (the provision does not apply to the beneficiaries), deferral (its application is postponed) or non-enforcement position (an authority or its staff states it will not take or recommend enforcement action; the provision still applies, as with a staff no-action letter). It never amends the provision it relieves.

**Example.** Regulator R's staff states it will not recommend enforcement of a reporting requirement against swap dealers for a stated period while systems are updated.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change: an amendment of the rule itself; and Regulatory Interpretation: a reading of the rule, which grants nothing.

**Kind of:** [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)

**Values (draft list):** form: exemption; no-action relief; transitional measure; supervisory forbearance; deferral; exemptive order · legal effect: exemption; deferral; non-enforcement position

**Words people use:** “no-action letter” (US, contextual); “forbearance” (contextual); “temporary relief”

**Before using it, know:** Relieved provision and version, beneficiaries (named or by class), conditions, start and expiry dates, issuer and its binding force.

**Connected to:**

- *relieves* [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation) — The legal effect (exemption, deferral or non-enforcement position) is stated on the relief; a non-enforcement position does not remove the obligation. Label changed 2026-11-09 (was removes_or_defers).
- *is issued by* [Regulatory Authority](#regulatory-authority)
- *can depend on* [Regulatory Classification](#regulatory-classification)

### Equivalence Decision

*A formal decision that another country's rules are good enough for a stated purpose, which often lets its CCPs or venues be used.*

**Definition.** Rule provision by which an authority of one regime finds that the rules of another jurisdiction meet its own for a stated purpose (CCP recognition, trading venue treatment, compliance by substitution), with its scope, conditions, effective period and any expiry. It relates two regimes; the status of a single CCP or venue that relies on it is a separate regulatory classification.

**Example.** Regime A deems Regime B's CCP rules equivalent until a stated date; CCPs of Regime B can then apply for recognition in Regime A.

**Not to be confused with.** Regulatory Authorisation (recognition): the status of one CCP; and qualifying CCP status, which governs capital treatment.

**Kind of:** [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)

**Values (draft list):** equivalence; comparability determination; substituted compliance; time-limited equivalence

**Words people use:** “equivalence” (EU and UK usage); “substituted compliance” (US usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Deciding regime and authority, assessed regime, purpose, conditions, effective period and expiry.

**Connected to:**

- *relates two* [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)
- *is a condition of* [Regulatory Authorisation](#regulatory-authorisation)
- *can change the treatment of trades on* [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue)

### Regulatory Obligation

*A duty the law puts on firms of a given status for given products from a given date, such as the duty to clear certain swaps.*

**Definition.** Duty that a regulatory regime imposes on parties holding a stated regulatory classification or role, for a defined product or activity scope, from stated application dates, subject to conditions, thresholds and exemptions, and defined by one or more rule provisions that may change separately. Whether an obligation applies to a named party is a conclusion recorded only in the internal layer.

**Example.** Under a regime, financial counterparties above a clearing threshold must clear interest rate swaps of a stated class with other such counterparties from a phase-in date.

**Not to be confused with.** Regulatory Classification: the status that triggers the duty; and Rule Provision: one article or paragraph, whereas one obligation is often spread over several provisions with its own phase-in.

**Part of:** [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)

**Breaks down into:** [Clearing Obligation](#clearing-obligation) (kind of), [Phase-in Step](#phase-in-step) (part of)

**Values (draft list):** clearing; trading on a venue (derivatives trading obligation); active account at an EU CCP; transaction and trade reporting; offer of client segregation and disclosure of its protection and cost; fair and transparent commercial terms for clearing services; position limits and position reporting; best execution; controls on direct electronic access and algorithmic trading; client information duties; risk mitigation for uncleared trades (out of current scope)

**Words people use:** “regulatory requirement” (generic); “mandate” (US usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, holder classification or role, product scope, conditions and exemptions, application and phase-in dates, defining provisions and their versions. Applicability to a named party is never stated from the public layer.

**Connected to:**

- [Access Requirement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#access-requirement) *may implement* Regulatory Obligation

### Clearing Obligation

*The legal duty for certain pairs of counterparties to clear certain swaps.*

**Definition.** Regulatory obligation to clear trades in a declared class of derivatives at an authorised or recognised CCP, from phased application dates. In the EU and UK it applies to pairs of counterparties holding in-scope statuses; in the US it applies to the class unless an exception or exemption is elected (to verify). Never applies to a product alone. An intragroup exemption depends on the group as the regime defines it, recorded as a regulatory classification of the counterparty pair, never on the commercial group or the intra-group client flag.

**Example.** Two financial counterparties trading a euro interest rate swap of a mandated class and tenor must clear it at an eligible CCP; the same swap between an exempt pair need not be cleared.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing eligibility: a CCP accepting a product for clearing does not make it mandatory; and 'cleared': a trade cleared voluntarily is not thereby subject to the obligation.

**Kind of:** [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation)

**Words people use:** “mandatory clearing” (contextual); “clearing mandate” (contextual); “clearing requirement” (US usage)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, both counterparties' classifications and their dates, product class with trade-term ranges, trade date and application date, exemptions claimed, CCP status (authorised or recognised).

**Connected to:**

- *covers trades in* [Regulated Product Class](#regulated-product-class)
- *applies to pairs holding* [Regulatory Classification](#regulatory-classification)
- *is met through* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *applies to* [Trade](../execution/concepts.md#trade)
- *applies through* [Phase-in Step](#phase-in-step)

### Phase-in Step

*One of the dates at which a new rule starts to apply to one group of firms or products.*

**Definition.** Dated stage in the application of a regulatory obligation or rule change to a defined subset of parties or products (counterparty category, size band, product class, activity), with its application date and any end of transition; a rule usually applies through several steps rather than from one date.

**Example.** A clearing obligation applies first to clearing members, later to other financial counterparties, and last to smaller firms, each from its own date.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change status 'applicable': the change can apply to one group and not yet to another; and Regulatory Relief: a deferral granted after the fact.

**Part of:** [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation)

**Words people use:** “phase-in”; “category 1, 2, 3” (contextual, EU clearing obligation); “compliance date” (US usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, the subset it applies to, application date (date role), end of transition, defining provision version.

**Connected to:**

- [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation) *applies through* Phase-in Step
- [Clearing Obligation](#clearing-obligation) *applies through* Phase-in Step
- *can also stage* [Rule Change](#rule-change)

### Regulated Product Class

*A legally defined group of derivatives that a rule refers to, such as 'euro fixed-to-floating interest rate swaps from 28 days to 50 years'.*

**Definition.** Category of derivatives defined by a rule provision of a regime as the scope of an obligation or classification, by asset class, product type and, where relevant, trade terms (currency, index, maturity range, settlement type), versioned with the provisions that define it. Instruments, contract specifications or trades are placed in it by a regulatory classification.

**Example.** A class of index credit default swaps, defined by index family, series and tenor, is declared subject to a clearing obligation.

**Not to be confused with.** Product Family: an economic grouping by asset class, underlying and contract form, set by industry practice, with no legal effect. Clearing Eligibility Schedule: what one CCP accepts. 'Traded on a trading venue' is a regulatory classification of an instrument, not a product class; UPI reference-data attributes, not the UPI itself, are a classification input.

**Part of:** [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)

**Values (draft list):** clearing obligation class; trading obligation class; active account category; US future, swap or security-based swap; security futures product (US); traded on a trading venue (EU)

**Words people use:** “class” (regulatory, contextual); “regulated derivative class”

**Before using it, know:** Regime, defining provision version, trade-term ranges, as-of date.

**Connected to:**

- [Clearing Obligation](#clearing-obligation) *covers trades in* Regulated Product Class
- *is defined in* [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)
- *is mapped to never equal to* [Product Family](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#product-family)
- *is the value of product level* [Regulatory Classification](#regulatory-classification)
- *sets the product scope of* [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation)

### Rule Change

*A change to a rule or to a published specification, followed from proposal or consultation until it applies, with its status and its dates.*

**Definition.** Proposed or adopted change to one rule provision, or to another published, versioned specification (fee schedule, contract specification, business calendar, cut-off set by a rule, margin methodology, margin parameter set announced by notice, collateral eligibility schedule, clearing eligibility schedule, access requirement), by which it is created, amended, replaced or withdrawn. It is followed from proposal to application, with a lifecycle status (proposed or in consultation, adopted, approved or self-certified where the issuer's supervisor must act, in force, applicable, superseded, withdrawn) and dates qualified by their role (publication, consultation close, adoption, entry into force, application, phase-in, end of transition, and the date the firm recorded it). Contract terms agreed with a client, routine parameter publications under an unchanged method, and new series listed under an unchanged contract specification are not rule changes; nor is a change of one party's regulatory classification. One published notice can announce several rule changes, and one rule change can be announced in several notices. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** CCP Z proposes to move its give-up take-up deadline thirty minutes earlier; the change is adopted, approved by its supervisor and applies from a stated clearing date.

**Not to be confused with.** Change Notice: the circular or publication that announces one or more changes; and a change in a client's or CCP's regulatory status, which is a new classification, not a rule change.

**Breaks down into:** [Consultation](#consultation) (step in), [Rule Change Approval](#rule-change-approval) (step in), [Change Impact Assessment](#change-impact-assessment) (step in)

**Words people use:** “circular, market notice” (Change impact)

### Consultation

*The period during which a regulator or CCP asks the market to comment on a proposed rule.*

**Definition.** Public stage of a proposed rule change in which the issuer invites comments on a draft by a response deadline, identified by the consultation document, its questions and its closing date; it may lead to adoption, a revised proposal or withdrawal.

**Example.** Regulator R publishes a consultation paper on changes to segregation disclosures, open for comment for three months.

**Not to be confused with.** Adopted rule: a consultation is not law and must always be shown as 'not yet in force'; and Change Notice: the consultation paper is the document, the consultation is the stage.

**Step in:** [Rule Change](#rule-change)

**Values (draft list):** open; closed; feedback published; withdrawn

**Words people use:** “consultation paper” (the document); “request for comment” (US usage); “proposed rule” (US usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, proposed provisions, closing date, status, link to any later adoption.

**Connected to:**

- *is opened by* [Change Notice](#change-notice)
- *closes on a* [Business Date](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#business-date)

### Rule Change Approval

*The regulator's sign-off of a CCP's or exchange's rule change, or the CCP's own certification where that is how the regime works.*

**Definition.** Step in which the supervisor of a CCP or venue approves, does not object to, validates, stays or rejects a change to that infrastructure's rulebook, margin model or fees, or in which the infrastructure self-certifies the change where its regime allows. Adoption by the issuer, this approval or self-certification, entry into force and application are distinct steps whose order depends on the regime; a change that needs approval cannot enter into force before it.

**Example.** CCP Z announces a margin model change 'subject to regulatory approval'; the approval comes two weeks later and the change applies from the next clearing date.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change status 'adopted' for a law, which needs no separate approval; and Change Impact Assessment, which is the firm's own review.

**Step in:** [Rule Change](#rule-change)

**Values (draft list):** approval requested; self-certified; approved; non-objection; validated; stayed; rejected; not required

**Words people use:** “regulatory approval” (contextual); “self-certification” (US usage); “rule filing” (US usage, contextual)

**Before using it, know:** Infrastructure, supervisor, filing or approval route, outcome, decision date, conditions.

**Connected to:**

- *is decided by* [Regulatory Authority](#regulatory-authority)
- *concerns a change to* [Rulebook](#rulebook)

### Change Impact Assessment

*The firm's written view of what a rule change touches and what it will do about it.*

**Definition.** The firm's assessment of one rule change: which business objects it affects (clearing services, venues, account types, segregation models, give-up and acceptance steps, margin, collateral, fees, contracts, client documents), how (scope, timing, data, legal effect, cost, process, client communication), for which roles, with what confidence, whether interpretation is required, and the resulting decision (act, plan, monitor, ignore), with its owner, reviewers and sign-off status. Instances are internal.

**Example.** For CCP Z's new give-up deadline, the assessment lists the give-up process and the client documents as affected, rates confidence high, and decides to act before the application date.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change: the published change itself; and Regulatory Interpretation: one reading inside the assessment where the text is unclear.

**Step in:** [Rule Change](#rule-change)

**Breaks down into:** [Regulatory Interpretation](#regulatory-interpretation) (part of)

**Values (draft list):** impact type: scope, timing, data, legal effect, cost, process, documentation, client communication; decision: act, plan, monitor, ignore; status: draft, under review, signed off, closed; confidence: high, medium, low

**Words people use:** “impact analysis”; “regulatory change assessment”

**Before using it, know:** The rule change and its status, as-of date, assessing and signing-off roles, the 'public-layer reading, not legal advice' label for agent drafts.

**Connected to:**

- *uses* [Regulatory Interpretation](#regulatory-interpretation)

### Regulatory Interpretation

*Someone's written reading of what a rule means, kept separate from the rule itself.*

**Definition.** Documented reading of what a rule provision means or how it applies, recorded with its author kind (authority, industry association, external counsel, the firm), its basis, confidence, status and sign-off; where the firm's reading is flagged 'interpretation required', it is signed off by Regulatory Affairs and business management with Legal and Compliance. It never changes the provision and is not law.

**Example.** An industry association reads a new active account condition one way; the firm's signed-off reading for its own case is recorded separately, in the internal layer.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Provision, including official guidance: guidance is issued by the authority; an interpretation is a reading by anyone, including the firm. And Regulatory Relief, which grants something.

**Part of:** [Change Impact Assessment](#change-impact-assessment)

**Values (draft list):** author kind: authority statement, industry association, external counsel, firm; status: draft, signed off, superseded

**Words people use:** “reading”; “legal view” (contextual); “industry guidance” (when issued by an association)

**Before using it, know:** Provision version read, author kind, basis, confidence, sign-off status and roles; firm readings are internal.

**Connected to:**

- [Change Impact Assessment](#change-impact-assessment) *uses* Regulatory Interpretation
- *is a reading of* [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)

### Regulatory Classification

*A status that a regime gives to someone or something for a period, such as 'financial counterparty above the clearing threshold' or 'recognised third-country CCP'.*

**Definition.** Time-bounded status that a named regulatory regime gives to a legal entity (or a branch where the regime says so), a role assignment, an account, a trading venue or venue segment, a CCP or clearing service, an instrument, contract specification or product family, a trade, or a pair of legal entities where the regime gives the status to the pair (for example an intragroup exemption), with its basis (authority decision or registration, notification, calculation, self-assessment, representation by the counterparty), the source that asserts it and the evidence; valid time and record time are both kept, and 'unknown' is a value, never a default. — *meaning changed in 2026-11, accepted as Draft*

**Example.** Client A represents that it is a financial counterparty above the clearing threshold for interest rate derivatives under EU EMIR from a stated date.

**Not to be confused with.** Regulatory Obligation: the duty that follows from a status; and a commercial client segment, which carries no regulatory meaning.

**Breaks down into:** [Client Regulatory Category](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#client-regulatory-category) (kind of), [Counterparty Regulatory Status](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#counterparty-regulatory-status) (kind of), [Regulatory Authorisation](#regulatory-authorisation) (kind of)

**Words people use:** “FCM” (US); “DCO” (US)

**Before using it, know:** Regime, asset class where relevant, basis, effective period, evidence, as-of and record time

**Connected to:**

- *classifies* [Legal Entity](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#legal-entity), [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role), [Account](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#account), [Trading Venue](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue), [Central Counterparty](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#central-counterparty), [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service), [Instrument](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#instrument), [Contract Specification](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#contract-specification), [Product Family](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#product-family), [Trade](../execution/concepts.md#trade) — Entity-level statuses attach to the legal entity, relationship-level statuses to the role assignment; never to the Client role generically or to a commercial group. A status the regime gives to a pair of counterparties (for example an intragroup exemption) attaches to both legal entities together, for the regime, product class and period, and is never read as a status of either one alone. Targets aligned with the definition 2026-11-09.
- *under* [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime) — Record regime, asset class where relevant, basis, effective period and evidence; valid time and record time both kept.
- [Trading Venue Segment](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#trading-venue-segment) *can carry its own regulatory classification* Regulatory Classification
- [Offering Eligibility Criterion](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#offering-eligibility-criterion) *may require a stated value of* Regulatory Classification
- [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation) *applies to holders of* Regulatory Classification

### Regulatory Authorisation

*An official licence or registration that lets a firm, CCP or venue do a stated activity in a jurisdiction.*

**Definition.** Regulatory classification of a legal entity (or of a branch where the regime says so) recording that an authority has authorised, registered, recognised, designated or exempted it to carry out a stated activity under a regime, with its scope, conditions, effective period and current standing (granted, conditional, suspended, withdrawn, applied for).

**Example.** The legal entity acting as Clearing Broker Y is registered in the US as a futures commission merchant.

**Not to be confused with.** Counterparty Regulatory Status: a counterparty category (financial or not, above or below a threshold), not a licence. Qualifying CCP status is a capital-treatment classification that follows from authorisation or recognition, recorded as a separate Regulatory Classification.

**Kind of:** [Regulatory Classification](#regulatory-classification)

**Values (draft list):** CCP authorisation; third-country CCP recognition (with tier where applicable); derivatives clearing organisation registration or exemption; futures commission merchant registration; swap dealer registration; designated contract market or swap execution facility designation; regulated market, MTF or OTF authorisation; investment firm or bank authorisation

**Words people use:** “FCM” (US, contextual); “DCO” (US, contextual); “licence”; “registration”; “recognition”

**Before using it, know:** Regime, granting authority, holder entity, scope (activity, clearing services, asset classes), period, standing, public register consulted and its as-of date.

**Connected to:**

- [Access Requirement](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#access-requirement) *may require* Regulatory Authorisation
- *is held by* [Legal Entity](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#legal-entity)
- *is granted under* [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)
- *is granted by* [Regulatory Authority](#regulatory-authority)
- *can be limited to* [Clearing Service](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#clearing-service)
- *can depend on* [Equivalence Decision](#equivalence-decision)

### Change Notice

*The circular, notice or publication through which a change is announced.*

**Definition.** Published document by a regulator, CCP, venue operator or industry body that announces, proposes or explains one or more rule changes: consultation paper, final report, published legal act, circular, market notice, clearing advisory, rule filing or self-certification, official guidance or Q&A, with its issuer, reference, publication date and language. It is evidence of rule changes, not the change itself; one notice can announce several changes and one change can appear in several notices.

**Example.** CCP Z's circular announces three changes at once: a new haircut table from one date, a new cut-off from another, and a fee change still subject to approval.

**Not to be confused with.** Rule Change: the change it announces, which has its own status and dates; one circular is often several changes.

**Values (draft list):** consultation paper; final report or draft technical standard; published legal act; circular or market notice; clearing advisory; rule filing or self-certification; official guidance or Q&A; press release (low evidence)

**Words people use:** “circular” (contextual); “market notice” (contextual); “member notice”; “special executive report” (one exchange group's usage)

**Before using it, know:** Issuer, reference, publication date and time zone, version (a notice can be corrected), language, which rule changes it announces.

**Connected to:**

- [Contract Adjustment](../instruments-venues-and-calendars/concepts.md#contract-adjustment) *is announced by* Change Notice — A market notice or clearing advisory announces the adjustment; the adjustment is not itself a rule change. Corrected 2026-11-09 (target was Rule Change).
- *announces* [Rule Change](#rule-change) — One notice can announce several rule changes and one change several notices. Corrected 2026-11-09 (was part_of).
- [Consultation](#consultation) *is opened by* Change Notice

### Regulatory Authority

*A public body that makes or enforces financial rules, such as a market supervisor or a central bank.*

**Definition.** Role of a public body, or of a self-regulatory organisation exercising powers that a regime delegates to it, that, under a regime, adopts rules, supervises, authorises or registers parties, grants relief or decides equivalence, within a stated remit (jurisdiction, sector, type of entity); includes legislators, supervisory authorities, central banks acting as supervisors and supranational authorities. Standard setters whose principles are not binding are recorded as issuers of guidance only.

**Example.** Regulator R supervises CCP Z, approves its rule changes and publishes guidance on the clearing obligation.

**Not to be confused with.** CCP or venue operator: they issue rulebooks but do not hold this role.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Values (draft list):** legislator; supervisory authority; central bank acting as supervisor; supranational authority; self-regulatory organisation (can issue rule provisions and levy fees)

**Words people use:** “regulator”; “competent authority” (EU usage, contextual); “supervisor”

**Before using it, know:** Jurisdiction and remit, which regime, which entities it supervises.

**Connected to:**

- [Regulatory Transaction Fee](../fees-revenue-and-contribution/concepts.md#regulatory-transaction-fee) *is owed to including a self regulatory organisation* Regulatory Authority
- [Regulatory Relief](#regulatory-relief) *is issued by* Regulatory Authority
- *adopts or supervises* [Regulatory Regime](#regulatory-regime)
- *issues* [Rule Provision](#rule-provision)
- *grants* [Regulatory Authorisation](#regulatory-authorisation)
- *decides* [Rule Change Approval](#rule-change-approval)

### Regulatory Reporting Role

*The party a regulation names as responsible for a given report on a trade.*

**Definition.** Role that a regime assigns to a party for one report type on an identified execution or trade: for example the executing entity of a transaction report, the reporting counterparty, or the entity responsible for reporting (to verify per regime). It is never inferred from the Executing Broker, Trading Venue Member or Giving Clearing Member roles. The report object itself stays in the backlog.

**Example.** For Client A's execution on Venue V, Executing Broker X is the executing entity in the transaction report, while Member M entered the order and Clearing Broker Y cleared it.

**Not to be confused with.** Executing Broker: the party that serves the client; the reporting role follows the regime's own test and may fall on another party.

**Kind of:** [Party Role](../parties-and-roles/concepts.md#party-role)

**Values (draft list):** executing entity in a transaction report; reporting counterparty; entity responsible for reporting

**Before using it, know:** Regime, report type, execution or trade.

**Connected to:**

- *is defined by* [Regulatory Obligation](#regulatory-obligation)
- *is held on* [Execution](../execution/concepts.md#execution)
- *is relied on by delegated reporting* [Service Component](../service-offering-and-market-access/concepts.md#service-component)

### Position Limit

*A cap, set by a regulator or a venue, on how many contracts one holder may hold, or a level above which it is watched or reported.*

**Definition.** Maximum position, or a position level that triggers scrutiny (accountability level) or reporting (reportable level), in a commodity derivative or other listed contract, set by a regulator or by a trading venue under its rules for the positions held or controlled by one person or group, aggregated across accounts and firms as the rule states, for a stated period (spot month, single month or all months) and net or by side, with the exemptions the rule allows (for example bona fide hedging). Each level states its kind: a limit (exceeding it is a breach), an accountability level (exceeding it lets the venue ask for information or require action, but is not in itself a breach) or a reportable level (reaching it triggers reporting); the consequence follows the kind and the rule, never the amount alone. It applies to the holder of the positions; monitoring it for clients is a firm duty recorded in the internal layer. Distinct from a Clearing Limit, which a clearing member sets for its own credit and risk control.

**Example.** Fund F approaches the spot-month limit on a commodity future at Exchange X and must reduce its position before expiry.

**Not to be confused with.** Clearing Limit: set by the clearing broker for its own credit risk, not by a regulator or venue.

**Values (draft list):** level kind: limit; accountability level; reportable level · period: spot month; single month; all months combined

**Words people use:** “accountability level, reportable level” (US exchanges and regulators)

**Before using it, know:** Regime or venue, contract, limit kind (limit, accountability or reportable level), period, aggregation basis, exemptions.

**Connected to:**

- *set by* [Rule Provision](#rule-provision) — A regulator's or a venue's rule.
- *applies to* [Position](../clearing-services-and-accounts/concepts.md#position) — Positions aggregated by holder or controller across accounts and firms, as the rule states.
- *is distinct from* [Clearing Limit](../clearing-give-up-and-acceptance/concepts.md#clearing-limit)
